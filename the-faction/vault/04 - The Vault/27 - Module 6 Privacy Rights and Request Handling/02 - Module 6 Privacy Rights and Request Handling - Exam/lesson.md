# Module 6: Privacy Rights and Request Handling — Exam

> Exam for **Module 6: Privacy Rights and Request Handling** (The Vault) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. A client asks why privacy rights concern the builder rather than just the legal team. What is this module's answer?

- **A.** Rights are legal abstractions that never touch systems, so the builder's only duty is linking the notice to a contact address
- **B.** Rights concern builders only in regulated industries, since general products rarely receive any requests worth planning for
- **C.** Every right eventually arrives as a request in an inbox, and whether the system can answer was decided when it was built  ✅
- **D.** Rights matter to builders because regulators audit code directly, and inspection readiness is a development responsibility

> **Answer:** C

### Q2. A user writes: "send me a copy of my data and tell me how it is used." Which right is this, in builder terms?

- **A.** Access: the buildable shape is a per-user export gathering their data across stores into a readable copy with usage information  ✅
- **B.** Portability: the buildable shape is a migration tool that transmits the account directly to a named competitor service
- **C.** Objection: the buildable shape is a processing stop that halts every purpose the moment any user sends such a message
- **D.** Rectification: the buildable shape is an audit trail showing every change ever made to the fields the user asks about

> **Answer:** A

### Q3. A user requests their data "in a format I can take elsewhere." What does the portability right require the build to produce?

- **A.** A printed statement mailed to the user, since physical delivery is what distinguishes portability from ordinary access
- **B.** A PDF summary styled for reading, since portability is about comprehension rather than any technical property of the file
- **C.** Screenshots of the user's profile pages, since visual completeness is the standard the portability right actually sets
- **D.** A machine-readable format such as JSON or CSV, structured so the data can move to another service the user chooses  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q4. A team treats an erasure request as a someday task. What operational reality from this module contradicts them?

- **A.** Requests have no deadline, but goodwill erodes, so the pressure to respond is entirely commercial rather than anything regulatory
- **B.** Rights have deadlines: about a month under GDPR-style rules and 45 days under CCPA-style rules, both extendable for complexity  ✅
- **C.** Deadlines exist but restart whenever the company asks a clarifying question, so the clock is effectively under its control
- **D.** Deadlines apply only to access requests, so erasure and correction can queue indefinitely behind other engineering work

> **Answer:** B

### Q5. A company wants to honor rights requests but cannot say which tables, logs, or vendors hold user data. What does this module identify as missing?

- **A.** The data map: rights cannot be serviced over data you cannot find, and classification plus data flows are the prerequisite  ✅
- **B.** A bigger support team, since rights handling is fundamentally a staffing question rather than a system design question
- **C.** A legal retainer, since only counsel can determine which of the systems in an architecture actually hold personal data
- **D.** A better ticketing tool, since request intake software is what turns scattered storage into an answerable estate

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. A deletion routine removes the user row and stops. The user remains in logs, the search index, and the email tool. What does this module say?

- **A.** The routine is compliant, since the primary table is the legal system of record and derived copies expire quietly on their own schedule
- **B.** The routine is compliant if the remaining copies are renamed, since pseudonymized leftovers satisfy the erasure right
- **C.** Erasure must reach primary storage, caches, search indexes, and the vendors processing on your behalf; this deletion is incomplete  ✅
- **D.** The routine is compliant because logs are exempt, and search indexes rebuild themselves too often for deletion to matter

> **Answer:** C

### Q7. An erasure request covers invoices that tax law requires keeping. How does this module direct the build to behave?

- **A.** Delete everything anyway, since the erasure right is absolute and retention statutes yield to any explicit user demand
- **B.** Retain the specific records law requires with a documented reason, and delete or anonymize the rest of the user's data  ✅
- **C.** Refuse the whole request, since any legal retention duty anywhere in the account voids the erasure right entirely
- **D.** Transfer the invoices to the user and then delete them locally, since handover satisfies both duties simultaneously

> **Answer:** B

### Q8. A client wants to promise that deletion instantly removes users from all backups. What does this module say to do instead?

- **A.** Make the promise, since customers expect it and platform backup behavior is too obscure for anyone to ever check
- **B.** Silently exclude backups from the deletion report, since what the report omits cannot contradict what marketing said
- **C.** Rebuild every backup on each deletion request, since reprocessing archives is the standard cost of the erasure right
- **D.** Disclose the backup schedule and let backups age out on it, since honest wording beats promising the impossible  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q9. A rights process emails full data exports to whoever asks, with no checks. What does this module call the likely outcome?

- **A.** A breach from your own rights process: sending data to the wrong person is just that, so verification comes first  ✅
- **B.** Excellent customer service, since removing friction from rights handling is the direction every regulator is pushing
- **C.** A minor process gap, since impostors rarely bother with rights requests and the risk stays theoretical in practice
- **D.** A liability for the requester alone, since whoever asked for the export bears the consequences of where it lands

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. One company demands notarized ID for newsletter unsubscribes; another asks users to confirm control of the account email. How does this module judge them?

- **A.** The notarized demand is safer, since maximum verification protects the company best whatever the friction costs users
- **B.** Both are acceptable, since any consistent verification standard fully satisfies the requirement regardless of proportionality
- **C.** Verification must be proportionate: confirm account or email control, escalate only when stakes demand it, never obstruct  ✅
- **D.** Neither matters, since verification is a courtesy step that companies may skip whenever volume makes it inconvenient

> **Answer:** C

### Q11. A builder plans separate projects for access and portability. What does this module observe about the two rights?

- **A.** They conflict, since readable output for access and structured output for portability cannot come from the same pipeline
- **B.** Portability replaces access under newer laws, so building the structured export alone covers both rights adequately
- **C.** Access is a legal duty while portability is optional, so the second project can be cut when the budget tightens
- **D.** One capability, two outputs: a per-user export produces the readable copy and the structured file from one gathering  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q12. A client asks how much correction (rectification) tooling to build. What does this module suggest as the standard shape?

- **A.** A dispute arbitration workflow with dual approval, since contested facts are the main traffic that correction paths receive
- **B.** Profile self-service for most rectification, plus an admin path for the rest; most corrections need no special machinery  ✅
- **C.** A quarterly batch process, since corrections are rare enough to accumulate and handle in scheduled maintenance windows
- **D.** Nothing, since data accuracy is the user's responsibility and the right exists mainly on paper across every regime

> **Answer:** B

### Q13. A user objects to marketing. The flag flips in the app, but the email platform and retargeting tool keep going. What does this module require?

- **A.** The stop must reach every sending system: objection generalizes consent withdrawal, and CCPA opt-outs work the same  ✅
- **B.** The flag is the legal record, so downstream tools can continue until their own sync schedules naturally catch up
- **C.** Objection covers only the channel the user named, so the retargeting tool may continue until separately objected to
- **D.** The user must repeat the objection to each vendor directly, since processors answer only to their own opt-out channels

> **Answer:** A

### Q14. A small client asks what the operational side of rights handling looks like. Which set of pieces does this module name?

- **A.** A dedicated compliance officer, an annual audit, and an outside firm on retainer to execute each arriving request
- **B.** A public transparency report, a bug bounty, and a rights FAQ, since visibility is what regulators reward most reliably
- **C.** Nothing formal, since ad hoc handling by whoever sees the email first is proportionate for any company that is under fifty employees
- **D.** An intake channel, a request log with dates and deadlines, verification steps, and a runbook: verify, execute, respond, record  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q15. A team ships the product first and plans rights handling after the first request arrives. How does this module describe that first request?

- **A.** A gentle forcing function, since real requests arrive with generous timelines that comfortably fund the needed build
- **B.** An archaeology project under a deadline: discovering where data lives after the fact, with the clock already running  ✅
- **C.** A support ticket like any other, since rights requests rarely require more than a reply with an attachment or two
- **D.** A sales opportunity, since users who exercise rights are the segment most engaged with the product experience

> **Answer:** B

### Q16. A build deleted user data from its own systems, but the email and analytics vendors kept everything. What does this module say about the state of that deletion?

- **A.** Incomplete: processors are part of your capability, and their deletion endpoints and processes belong to your deletion path  ✅
- **B.** Complete, since vendor-held copies belong to the vendors and sit outside what any deletion request can lawfully reach at all
- **C.** Complete if the contracts call vendors independent controllers, since labels in agreements settle where duties end
- **D.** Complete after thirty days, since processor copies are presumed to expire on the standard commercial retention cycle

> **Answer:** A

### Q17. You want AI to produce the data map that rights handling queries. Which prompt matches the directing pattern in this module?

- **A.** Write a privacy policy section on rights, since documenting the rights is what makes a system able to answer them
- **B.** Delete all personal data older than one year, since a smaller estate is the fastest route to an accurate data map
- **C.** Using this schema and processor list, generate every location where a user's personal data can exist, keyed by user identifier  ✅
- **D.** List the privacy laws in each state and country we serve, since the map of jurisdictions is the true map rights handling needs

> **Answer:** C

### Q18. You are directing AI to build the deletion capability. Which instruction encodes the judgment this module requires?

- **A.** Hard-delete every single row that references the user anywhere at all, since uniform total destruction is the only defensible policy
- **B.** Mark the user inactive and hide their profile from view, since display suppression is what deletion means in practice
- **C.** Delete only what the user lists by name, since the request text defines the exact boundary of the whole obligation
- **D.** Anonymize records inside legal retention, hard-delete these tables, call processor endpoints, and report actions and reasons  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q19. Before shipping, you ask AI to review the build for deletion coverage. Which checklist reflects this module?

- **A.** Every system a deletion would touch: caches, search indexes, analytics, email tools, AI embeddings, flagging any with no path  ✅
- **B.** The main database tables listed in the ORM models, since object mappings are the authoritative inventory of personal data
- **C.** The systems the client remembers using, since institutional memory is the most current record of where the data actually flows
- **D.** The vendors with the largest invoices, since spend is the reliable proxy for which services hold meaningful user data

> **Answer:** A

### Q20. A five-person client balks at buying compliance software for request tracking. What does this module say satisfies the record-keeping need?

- **A.** Only certified DSAR platforms count as records, so the purchase is unavoidable once the first request has arrived
- **B.** A simple request log, even a spreadsheet: request, date, type, verification, actions, response date, tracked from receipt  ✅
- **C.** The email inbox itself, since message timestamps can reconstruct any timeline a regulator could reasonably request later on
- **D.** Memory plus goodwill, since companies under the enforcement radar have no practical need to evidence their handling

> **Answer:** B

### Q21. A well-prepared team receives its first erasure request. Per the module's scenario, what does the process look like?

- **A.** A war room: leadership convenes, counsel drafts holding language, and engineering reverse-engineers where the user data actually lives
- **B.** A negotiation: the team offers account credit in exchange for withdrawing the request before any deletion is attempted
- **C.** Boring: verify, run the deletion capability, log retained records with reasons, respond in plain language well inside the deadline  ✅
- **D.** A referral: the request forwards to the hosting platform, since infrastructure providers execute erasure on request

> **Answer:** C

### Q22. Requests at a company are handled ad hoc: no log, no deadline tracking. What failure mode does this module predict?

- **A.** Deadlines missed silently, with no evidence of compliance to show when asked; the process needs a log and a clock  ✅
- **B.** Faster handling overall, since informal processes shed the bureaucratic steps that slow the well-documented ones down
- **C.** No failure at all, provided the team is conscientious, since diligence substitutes fully for structure at small scale
- **D.** Only a reporting inconvenience, since regulators accept reconstructed timelines assembled after an inquiry arrives

> **Answer:** A

### Q23. Two vendors could fill the same slot in a build. One documents deletion and export APIs; the other has neither. What does this module direct?

- **A.** Choose on price and features alone, since rights capabilities are a legal concern that never belongs in vendor selection
- **B.** Treat rights capabilities as selection criteria: check deletion and export when choosing, not after the first request  ✅
- **C.** Choose either, since a vendor without deletion endpoints can always be compelled by contract amendment later on
- **D.** Choose the second if it is cheaper, and plan to migrate away if a deletion request ever actually materializes

> **Answer:** B

### Q24. A deletion request arrives from someone claiming to act for a deceased relative, and a second is tangled in an active dispute. What does this module direct?

- **A.** Execute both immediately, since hesitation on any erasure request is the compliance failure regulators punish hardest
- **B.** Reject both as invalid, since rights are personal and lapse in death or in litigation under every major privacy regime
- **C.** Handle both from the standard runbook, since edge cases exist precisely to test how general the written process is
- **D.** Escalate verification proportionately and route the legal edge cases, third parties and disputes, to counsel  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q25. Looking across this whole module, what is the governing principle of privacy rights and request handling?

- **A.** Rights handling is customer service: warm responses and fast replies matter far more than anything that the underlying systems can do
- **B.** Rights handling is legal correspondence: the quality of the letters exchanged determines compliance more than the build
- **C.** Rights are capabilities designed in at build time: a data map, export, deletion with encoded judgment, and a process with a clock  ✅
- **D.** Rights are a scale problem: small products can safely ignore them until request volume justifies dedicated tooling

> **Answer:** C
