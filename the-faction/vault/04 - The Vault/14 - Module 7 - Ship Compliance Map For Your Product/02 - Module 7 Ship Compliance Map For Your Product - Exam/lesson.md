# Module 7: Ship: Compliance Map For Your Product — Exam

> Exam for **Module 7 — Ship: Compliance Map For Your Product** (The Vault) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. A builder starts their compliance map by listing every regulation they have ever heard of, then researching each one. Per the Module 7 decision tree, what should they do instead?

- **A.** Start with what data the product collects, then users, locations, and industry, letting each answer narrow the next  ✅
- **B.** Start with the industry question, since vertical rules like HIPAA are the most severe and must surface before anything else
- **C.** Keep the regulation list approach but ask AI to confirm every entry against its primary source before recording it
- **D.** Start with the locations question, since geography controls which national and state statutes could possibly apply

> **Answer:** A

### Q2. A builder's map is a single column of regulation names: GDPR, CCPA, PCI DSS. They feel done. What does Module 7 say this map is missing?

- **A.** The specific requirement, current status, and gap for each entry, which turn the reading list into a work plan  ✅
- **B.** Citation links to each regulation's official text so a client can verify the list against primary sources
- **C.** A risk score for every regulation so the team knows which items carry the largest potential fines and penalties
- **D.** The name of a responsible professional for each regulation so accountability is documented inside the map itself

> **Answer:** A

### Q3. A builder's gap list includes: draft a privacy policy, add a cookie consent banner, sign a BAA with a dental office client, and build a deletion flow. Which gap belongs in the professional-review column?

- **A.** The privacy policy, because it is a public legal document and any wording mistake exposes the company to costly claims
- **B.** The cookie consent banner, because EU consent standards are strict and a misconfiguration risks GDPR penalties
- **C.** The deletion flow, because GDPR Article 17 governs it and any statutory requirement calls for counsel review first
- **D.** The BAA with the dental client, because signing that contract creates legal obligations, which is counsel territory  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q4. A US-only consumer app suddenly gets its first wave of EU signups. The compliance map was built for US users only. What does Module 7 say happens now?

- **A.** Nothing yet, since GDPR obligations only begin once EU users become a meaningful share of the total user base
- **B.** Add GDPR to the map marked as compliant, since the app already has a privacy policy and a working deletion flow
- **C.** Block all EU signups until counsel signs off, since serving any EU user without a completed DPA is a violation
- **D.** Treat the signups as a map update trigger, re-run the location branch of the tree, and document GDPR status and gaps  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q5. A client emails a builder: are we GDPR compliant now that your work is done? Per Module 7, what is the right response?

- **A.** Say yes, since the map shows every GDPR gap closed, and a documented process is the accepted working standard of compliance
- **B.** Share the map: these regulations appear to apply, here is our current status, and here is what still needs counsel review  ✅
- **C.** Decline to answer anything compliance related, since only a licensed attorney may discuss GDPR with a paying client
- **D.** Say the product is compliant but add a disclaimer that the statement is informational rather than legal advice

> **Answer:** B

### Q6. AI drafts a builder's compliance map and cites a specific user-count threshold for a state privacy statute. What should the builder do before that number goes on the map?

- **A.** Record it as drafted, since the audit prompt already instructs the AI to double check thresholds before responding
- **B.** Verify the threshold against the primary source before recording it, since AI can cite stale or incorrect numbers  ✅
- **C.** Send the entire draft map to counsel for line by line review, since AI research is never usable without a lawyer
- **D.** Ask a second AI model to confirm the figure, and record it once two independent models agree on the same number

> **Answer:** B

### Q7. A builder mapped their product during planning. Before launch they quietly added an analytics SDK and a phone number field. What does Module 7 require now?

- **A.** Nothing, since analytics tools and contact fields are standard features already fully covered by the planning-stage map
- **B.** Note the additions in a changelog appendix and fold them into the map at the next scheduled quarterly review
- **C.** Re-run the decision tree against the shipped build, since the map must evaluate what shipped, not what was planned  ✅
- **D.** Remove the analytics SDK until the map is redone, since unmapped data collection must never reach production

> **Answer:** C

### Q8. A builder cannot decide whether SOC 2 preparation belongs in the builder column or the professional column. What test does Module 7's gap sort rule apply?

- **A.** Ask whether the gap can be closed in code, since anything a builder can implement belongs in the builder column
- **B.** Ask what professional help would cost, and keep the item in the builder column whenever counsel is unaffordable
- **C.** Ask whether AI can draft the deliverable, since anything AI can produce is builder work by the course definition
- **D.** Ask whether closing it involves a contract, a certification, or a regulated-industry judgment call; if yes, professionals  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q9. A vibecoder about to ship a scheduling app for dentists asks why they should bother building a compliance map first. What is the Module 7 answer?

- **A.** The map surfaces triggers like HIPAA before launch, instead of a demand letter surfacing them after shipping  ✅
- **B.** The map doubles as the legal certification most dental office clients require before signing a purchase order
- **C.** The map transfers liability to the AI vendor that produced the research if a regulation turns out to be missed
- **D.** The map lets the builder state the product is HIPAA compliant in sales copy once every row shows closed

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. A product's map was finished at launch. Six months later the team starts storing card numbers for a new billing feature. What does this mean for the map?

- **A.** Nothing, since payment processing is handled by the processor and never changes a product's own compliance scope
- **B.** Only the privacy policy row changes, since taking payments means the policy must disclose billing data
- **C.** The map needs a fresh pass, since adding payments is a defined trigger that activates a new branch of the tree  ✅
- **D.** Every row must be deleted and rebuilt from zero, since any product change makes the prior tree answers unusable

> **Answer:** C

### Q11. A builder wants to close map gaps by directing AI instead of hiring help. Which set of gaps does Module 7 place squarely in that builder column?

- **A.** Drafting a privacy policy for review, wiring up cookie consent, and building data deletion and export flows  ✅
- **B.** Drafting the DPA for enterprise customers, since data processing terms are templated and rarely negotiated in practice
- **C.** Preparing the SOC 2 attestation package, since controls are documented and AI can compile all the evidence
- **D.** Completing a BAA template found online, since standard HIPAA language is public and reused across startups

> **Answer:** A

### Q12. A builder has written solid event triggers into their map: new regions, new data types, payments. Why does Module 7 still require a recurring quarterly review?

- **A.** Scheduled reviews replace event triggers over time, since calendar checks are more reliable than remembered events
- **B.** The recurring review catches slow drift that no single defined trigger event would ever have flagged on its own  ✅
- **C.** Most regulations republish their thresholds quarterly, so the review keeps every cited number current on the map
- **D.** Clients expect quarterly compliance attestations, and the review produces the certificate those clients require

> **Answer:** B

### Q13. A founder wants the phrase HIPAA compliant on the pitch deck because the compliance map is finished. What does Module 7 say?

- **A.** Use the claim, since a completed map plus a signed BAA is exactly what HIPAA compliance means in practice
- **B.** Replace it with a statement like: we maintain a documented compliance process, which is accurate and defensible  ✅
- **C.** Use the claim but footnote that no official certification body exists for HIPAA, so the phrase stays informal
- **D.** Drop all compliance language from the deck, since describing a process invites diligence questions you cannot answer

> **Answer:** B

### Q14. A builder runs a B2B analytics tool with EU users and a consumer app for US dental offices. They plan to write one shared compliance map. What does Module 7 advise?

- **A.** Run each product through the tree separately, since different data, users, and industries produce different maps  ✅
- **B.** Reuse the consumer map as the base and bolt on B2B extras, since consumer rules are the strictest superset
- **C.** Skip mapping the B2B tool, since regulations target consumers and business customers carry their own compliance duties
- **D.** Map only the overlap, since one shared document is safer than two maps that can quietly fall out of sync

> **Answer:** A

### Q15. A busy builder wants to hand the entire compliance mapping job to AI and paste the output straight into the map. How does Module 7 frame AI's role here?

- **A.** Fine as is, since the module's audit prompt template was designed so AI output can go directly onto the map
- **B.** Wrong tool entirely, since compliance mapping requires legal training and AI should wait until counsel is formally engaged
- **C.** AI can walk the tree, surface candidates, and draft the map, but the builder verifies sources and routes escalations  ✅
- **D.** AI should only format the document, since letting it propose regulations creates unreviewable legal exposure

> **Answer:** C

### Q16. A map row reads: CCPA, applies to us, status reviewed. The builder considers the row finished. What does Module 7 say is still missing?

- **A.** Nothing, since applicability plus a reviewed status shows the regulation has been considered and dealt with
- **B.** A named statute section, since a row without an exact citation cannot be audited by counsel at a later date
- **C.** A completion date, since rows without deadlines turn the map into a wish list instead of a working schedule
- **D.** The specific requirement and the documented gap, since without both the row cannot drive any actual work  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q17. A healthcare client asks their builder to just fill in a DPA template so the deal can close this week. What is the Module 7 move?

- **A.** Fill it in, since a DPA template is standardized paperwork and only novel contract language needs a lawyer
- **B.** Route it to a professional, since executing data processing agreements creates obligations beyond builder scope  ✅
- **C.** Fill it in with a disclaimer that the builder is not a lawyer, shifting the responsibility back to the client
- **D.** Have AI complete the template and let the client sign it, since the client carries all of the contract risk anyway

> **Answer:** B

### Q18. A general-purpose project tool starts seeing signups from dental offices, an industry the builder never targeted. What does Module 7 direct?

- **A.** No action, since the tool was never marketed to healthcare and unsolicited signups do not change its scope
- **B.** Email the dental users to confirm they store no patient data, then note that assurance on the existing map
- **C.** Immediately sign BAAs with each office, since any healthcare adjacent user makes the product a HIPAA vendor
- **D.** Treat it as an industry trigger: re-run that branch of the tree, assess HIPAA exposure, and record status and gaps  ✅

### Q19. An agency builder finishes a client project and the client asks what was done about GDPR. Per Module 7, what does the builder hand over?

- **A.** The documented map of applicable regulations, status, and gaps, framed as diligence rather than compliance  ✅
- **B.** A note that GDPR is the client's own responsibility as controller, since builders act as processors at most
- **C.** A signed statement of compliance, since clients need real certainty before making their own launch decisions
- **D.** A referral straight to outside counsel, since a builder discussing GDPR at all is practicing law without a license

### Q21. A builder pastes their finished map into an AI audit session. Per Module 7's audit approach, what should they ask the AI to return?
