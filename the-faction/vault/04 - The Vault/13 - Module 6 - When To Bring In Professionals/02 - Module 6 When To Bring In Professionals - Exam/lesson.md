# Module 6: When To Bring In Professionals — Exam

> Exam for **Module 6 — When To Bring In Professionals** (The Vault) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. You built a scheduling tool for a dental office. Your AI regulatory map flags that appointment notes may count as health data. The client wants to launch Friday. What is the right move?

- **A.** Launch on schedule since the map is done, then book a lawyer for a post launch review if the client raises concerns
- **B.** Add encryption and access controls to the notes field so the build meets the technical bar before the launch date
- **C.** Treat health data as an automatic escalation signal and get counsel to review the data flow before the launch  ✅
- **D.** Ask the dental office to sign a waiver accepting responsibility for any health data rules that might apply here

> **Answer:** C

### Q2. Your SaaS build has exactly one red flag: it processes EU user data. A friend says one flag is not worth a legal bill and you should wait until more pile up. How do you respond?

- **A.** One trigger can carry a full regulatory regime, so severity depends on the worst signal, not how many you can count  ✅
- **B.** Agree to wait, since counsel review only becomes cost effective once a build racks up at least three separate red flags
- **C.** Split the difference by documenting the flag in your map and revisiting it after the product reaches real revenue
- **D.** Ask the friend to review the build informally first, then escalate only if they think the EU exposure looks serious

> **Answer:** A

### Q3. A client asks you to add a stored value wallet so users can preload credits into their community app. Nothing else about the build changed. Does this need counsel?

- **A.** No, because stored credits are a product feature and the underlying payment processor already handles the compliance
- **B.** No, as long as you use a well known payments API whose terms of service state that they maintain PCI certification
- **C.** Only if the wallet also supports crypto, since fiat credits inside a closed app do not raise regulatory questions
- **D.** Yes, stored value is a fintech feature and any fintech feature is an automatic trigger for review before you ship  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q4. You booked a one hour consult about your app. It is expensive and you want the hour to produce answers. What should you send the lawyer in advance?

- **A.** The full source code repository so counsel can inspect the implementation directly instead of relying on summaries
- **B.** Your AI regulatory map alone, since it already lists every statute and the lawyer can work through it during the call
- **C.** A short brief covering what the product does, who uses it, the data collected, where it lives, and open questions  ✅
- **D.** A signed engagement letter and payment upfront, because preparation matters less than securing the billable slot

> **Answer:** C

### Q5. A client asks you point blank: are we compliant with GDPR or not? You have a thorough AI generated regulatory map in hand. What is inside your scope to say?

- **A.** That your map shows which regulations plausibly apply and where counsel should look, but compliance is a legal judgment  ✅
- **B.** That the build is compliant, since your map covered every GDPR article and the architecture matches its requirements
- **C.** That compliance is impossible to determine either way, so the client should simply purchase cyber liability insurance instead
- **D.** That the client is compliant so long as you followed the map, with a disclaimer that laws can change after launch

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. You have never spoken to a lawyer. Your app is growing and touches location data. When is the right time to establish a counsel relationship?

- **A.** When a regulator or opposing lawyer first contacts you, because paying for counsel before a dispute wastes limited budget
- **B.** Now, with an intro call or small scoped engagement, so an urgent issue reaches someone who already knows your product  ✅
- **C.** At the next funding round, when investors require formal legal opinions and can absorb the retainer in the raise
- **D.** After your first paying enterprise customer, since consumer scale apps rarely attract any regulatory attention

> **Answer:** B

### Q7. A client balks at a 1500 dollar data practices review for their health adjacent app, calling it a lot of money for a small tool. How do you frame the tradeoff?

- **A.** Offer to cut the review scope in half, since a partial look at the riskiest module gives most of the protection anyway
- **B.** Agree it is steep and propose relying on your regulatory map, which covered the same statutes at no additional cost
- **C.** Note that regulators rarely pursue small apps, so the review is really about reassuring the client's own customers
- **D.** Compare it to the other column: breach notification, per violation penalties, and weeks of unbillable rebuild time  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q8. Your community app just opened public posting and uploads, and usage is exploding. A teammate says moderation tooling covers it. What is the compliance read?

- **A.** Moderation tooling is the accepted standard, so counsel is only needed if you plan to monetize the user content
- **B.** UGC risk sits with the users who post, so your terms of service assigning them liability is sufficient legal protection
- **C.** Only copyrighted media uploads raise issues, so a filter for music and video keeps the build out of trigger range
- **D.** Hosting user generated content at scale is itself an escalation signal, so counsel should review before you grow it  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q9. You are assembling the data section of your counsel brief for a consult next week. Which version makes the hour count?

- **A.** Every data category collected, where each is stored, retention period, and which third parties receive each category  ✅
- **B.** A statement that data handling follows industry best practices, keeping the brief short so it respects billed time
- **C.** The database schema with table and column names, since lawyers reason from the raw structures rather than prose summaries
- **D.** Only the categories you believe are sensitive, since listing routine data would pad the brief and burn the hour

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. Your habit tracker for therapy patients has ten users, all friends of the founder. The founder says it is too small to worry about legal review. What is true?

- **A.** They are right for now, since regulatory obligations phase in as revenue and user counts pass statutory thresholds
- **B.** They are right as long as users sign a beta agreement acknowledging the product is experimental and unsupported
- **C.** They are wrong only if the app charges money, since free tools for friends fall outside commercial regulations
- **D.** They are wrong, because obligations attach to the data category handled, not to the size of the user base or MRR  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q11. Two attorneys quote your review: a cheap generalist who does mostly real estate, and a pricier lawyer with privacy and tech experience. How do you choose?

- **A.** Pick the privacy and tech lawyer, because a generalist review can produce false confidence worse than no review  ✅
- **B.** Pick the generalist to start, since any licensed attorney can competently spot the issues in a standard data review
- **C.** Pick the generalist and send a longer brief, since preparation quality matters more than the reviewer's specialty
- **D.** Hire both, using the generalist for contracts and the specialist only if the generalist finds something alarming

> **Answer:** A

### Q12. Your build includes a sketchy analytics script you plan to remove someday. You are prepping the counsel brief and are tempted to leave it out. What should you do?

- **A.** Leave it out, since including known temporary code would waste billed time on something already scheduled to go
- **B.** Leave it out but remove the script before launch, which makes the brief accurate as of the shipping architecture
- **C.** Include it, because advice that omits part of the real system does not cover your risk, and counsel is confidential  ✅
- **D.** Mention it verbally at the end of the call so it stays out of written records that could surface in legal discovery later

> **Answer:** C

### Q13. A city agency wants to license your scheduling tool for its parks department. Same product, new customer type. Does anything change?

- **A.** Yes, contracting with a government entity is an escalation signal, so counsel should review before you sign anything  ✅
- **B.** No, the product is unchanged, and escalation signals track what the software does rather than who is paying for the seats
- **C.** Only if the agency handles public safety data, since ordinary parks scheduling is the lowest risk government work
- **D.** Only if the contract exceeds a six figure value, since procurement rules scale with the dollar size of the deal

> **Answer:** A

### Q14. Analytics show a growing chunk of signups from Germany and Brazil. Your servers and company are US only, and you never marketed abroad. What does this mean?

- **A.** Nothing yet, since regulations attach to where your company and servers sit rather than where users appear
- **B.** Cross border data movement is an escalation signal, so this shift means counsel should look at your data flows now  ✅
- **C.** You only need a translated privacy policy, since international users mainly create a disclosure formatting problem
- **D.** You should block foreign signups until revenue justifies expansion, since access control removes the exposure fully

> **Answer:** B

### Q15. Before the consult, you want to do everything that is legitimately your job as the builder. Which list stays inside your scope?

- **A.** Map which regulations plausibly apply, document the data flows, and write numbered open questions for counsel  ✅
- **B.** Determine which regulations actually bind the product, then bring counsel a conclusions memo to sign off quickly
- **C.** Draft the privacy policy and terms yourself from templates so counsel only needs to proofread the final wording
- **D.** Decide how much regulatory risk the client should accept, then ask counsel to paper that decision in the contract

> **Answer:** A

### Q16. Your kids reading app adds a feature that snaps a photo to verify the child finished a page. A teammate calls it a minor UX addition. What is the compliance read?

- **A.** It is minor if photos are deleted within a day, since short retention keeps the feature under the sensitivity bar
- **B.** It is minor because parents installed the app, and parental installation counts as consent for anything it collects
- **C.** It stacks children's data with biometric adjacent images, two sensitive categories, so counsel review comes first  ✅
- **D.** It is minor if processing happens on device, since data that never reaches your servers sits outside any regime

> **Answer:** C

### Q17. Two builders discover the same breach on the same day. One has a lawyer from a prior scoped engagement, the other starts cold calling firms. What difference should they expect?

- **A.** Little difference, since breach response is standardized work that any competent firm can spin up within a day
- **B.** The existing client gets a callback in hours from counsel who knows the product, while the stranger waits weeks  ✅
- **C.** The cold caller may do better, since fresh counsel brings no assumptions and quotes competitive emergency rates upfront
- **D.** Both wait about the same, but the existing client saves money because the retainer already covers emergencies

> **Answer:** B

### Q18. Your first consult burned forty minutes explaining what an API is and how your app stores data. For the next engagement, what fixes this?

- **A.** Book a lawyer who codes, since the real problem is that most attorneys cannot follow modern technical products at all
- **B.** Send a plain language brief in advance covering product, users, architecture, and data, so the hour goes to answers  ✅
- **C.** Book two hours instead of one, accepting that lawyer education is an unavoidable cost of any first engagement
- **D.** Skip the call format and move to email only questions, since written answers cannot burn any time on background

> **Answer:** B

### Q19. You are quoting a project: an internal document portal for a small insurance brokerage. It is a basic CRUD app you have built many times. What belongs in the quote?

- **A.** Counsel review time, because the client operates in a regulated industry and that alone triggers professional review  ✅
- **B.** No legal line item, since internal tools without public users sit outside the reach of consumer facing regulation entirely
- **C.** A discount, since repeat architecture means the compliance homework from earlier builds transfers to this client
- **D.** A clause disclaiming compliance responsibility, which shifts the regulatory burden entirely onto the brokerage

> **Answer:** A

### Q20. Your AI produced a polished regulatory map for a client build. The client wants to publish fully GDPR compliant on their site based on it. What do you tell them?

- **A.** They can publish it if the map was thorough, since a documented research process is what compliance claims ultimately rest on
- **B.** The map is a research artifact that directs counsel review, and a compliance claim needs a lawyer's judgment behind it  ✅
- **C.** They can publish it if you add a disclaimer that the assessment was AI assisted and is updated on a rolling basis
- **D.** They should publish nothing about privacy at all, since any public statement only increases litigation exposure

> **Answer:** B

### Q21. You are budgeting legal review into a client proposal for a data heavy MVP. Which framing reflects the module's cost math?

- **A.** Legal review typically costs more than the MVP build itself, so it should be deferred until the product finds real traction
- **B.** A scoped consult runs a few hundred dollars and a full data practices review a few thousand, tiny next to the downside  ✅
- **C.** Legal costs are unknowable in advance, so quote a broad contingency line and let the client decide whether to fund it
- **D.** Review pricing scales with user count, so budgeting now locks in lower rates than waiting until the audience grows

> **Answer:** B

### Q22. You are finishing the counsel brief and listing your open questions. How should that section be built so the consult produces launch decisions?

- **A.** Keep questions broad, like what are our risks, so counsel stays free to surface issues you would not know to raise
- **B.** Leave questions out entirely, since a good lawyer will identify the important issues from the data inventory alone
- **C.** Frame each question with your best guess answer attached, so counsel can confirm your reasoning quickly
- **D.** Number specific questions and order them by launch blocking priority, so the hour resolves what gates shipping  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q23. Your brief includes a section listing regulations that plausibly apply, pulled from your AI map. How should you present that section to counsel?

- **A.** As your compliance determination, so counsel can focus the hour on anything they think your analysis got wrong
- **B.** As settled findings for the low risk items, with only the ambiguous regulations flagged for counsel attention
- **C.** As pointers for counsel review, explicitly labeled as flags rather than conclusions about what the law requires  ✅
- **D.** Left out of the brief entirely, since suggesting regulations to a lawyer risks anchoring their professional judgment

> **Answer:** C

### Q24. A cofounder wants a way to decide, per build, whether legal spend is justified. Which tool from this module answers that question directly?

- **A.** The Counsel Rolodex, since knowing what your vetted attorney charges tells you whether a build justifies the spend
- **B.** The Escalation Trigger List, since counting how many of the seven signals fire tells you how much review to purchase
- **C.** The Counsel Brief Template, since drafting the brief reveals whether the build is complex enough to need a lawyer
- **D.** The Cost of Wrong Ledger, which puts scoped review cost beside realistic downside like fines and rebuild time  ✅

### Q25. Which statement best captures the governing principle of Module 6, When To Bring In Professionals?

- **A.** Builders should learn enough law through AI research that most projects can ship safely without any paid legal review at all
- **B.** Legal review is a formality larger clients expect, so builders should treat it as a sales cost, not a safeguard
- **C.** Builders identify triggers, document the system, and hand off, because knowing when you are out of your depth is a skill  ✅
- **D.** Lawyers should come in only when a build has zero remaining technical unknowns, so their time is never wasted

> **Answer:** C
