# Module 7: Building Privacy Into Every Project — Exam

> Exam for **Module 7: Building Privacy Into Every Project** (The Vault) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. A team plans to 'handle privacy properly after the incident review process matures.' Which privacy-by-design behavior does this violate?

- **A.** Proactive not reactive: privacy decisions belong at scoping, before anything ships, not after incidents force the question  ✅
- **B.** Full functionality: incident maturity is a feature question, and features always precede privacy work in any healthy sequence
- **C.** End-to-end security: incident response is the security principle, so the plan merely sequences the correct owner first
- **D.** Visibility and transparency: the plan fails only because the team has not yet published it to users in the notice

> **Answer:** A

### Q2. A new product ships with marketing unticked, minimal collection, and the shortest sensible retention, all without user effort. Which principle is this?

- **A.** Respect for the user, since courteous defaults are primarily a tone choice that shapes how the brand is perceived
- **B.** Full functionality, since defaults that lean private are what allow the feature set to expand safely later on
- **C.** Privacy as the default setting: the protective configuration is what users get when they do nothing at all  ✅
- **D.** Proactive not reactive, since shipping early with any defaults beats waiting for the perfect configuration

> **Answer:** C

### Q3. One team adds export and deletion to the data model at design time; another plans to bolt them on after launch. What does this module say?

- **A.** Both approaches land in the same place, since rights capabilities are additive features with no architectural footprint
- **B.** Privacy embedded in design means rights capabilities live in the data model from the start, not bolted on afterward  ✅
- **C.** The bolt-on approach is preferable, since real request patterns should shape the capability before anyone builds it
- **D.** The choice depends on team size, since embedded rights capabilities only pay off above a certain headcount

> **Answer:** B

### Q4. A stakeholder frames the roadmap as 'privacy versus features' and asks which should win. How does this module reframe that?

- **A.** Features win, since privacy is a compliance layer that can always be retrofitted once the product has found its true market
- **B.** Privacy wins, since regulators punish feature velocity and the safest roadmap is the one that ships least
- **C.** The framing is right but the ratio is negotiable, so teams should budget privacy as a fixed percentage of each sprint
- **D.** Full functionality rejects the trade: privacy and features together is the standard, not privacy at the expense of features  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q5. You are adding a privacy section to your build brief template. Which contents does this module specify?

- **A.** Whose data, what data, purposes and bases, regions and flows, retention, rights capabilities, and open legal questions  ✅
- **B.** The full text of applicable statutes, since a brief is only complete when the law it answers to appears inside it verbatim
- **C.** A liability disclaimer and an insurance certificate, since the brief exists to shift risk before the build begins
- **D.** The marketing consent copy alone, since the rest of the privacy posture emerges naturally during implementation

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. A builder skips privacy scoping to save an hour and hits residency and consent problems at launch. What does this module say about the economics?

- **A.** The hour was correctly saved, since problems discovered at launch arrive when the team is largest and best resourced
- **B.** Written at scoping, the privacy section costs an hour; discovered at launch, it costs the schedule  ✅
- **C.** The costs are symmetric, since privacy work takes the same effort whenever it happens in the project lifecycle
- **D.** The economics depend on the client, since some clients never notice launch delays caused by compliance rework

> **Answer:** B

### Q7. You are installing the pre-delivery privacy review. Per this module, what does it check and how long should it take?

- **A.** Classification current, consent recorded, collection minimal, retention automated, export and deletion tested, notice accurate: ten minutes  ✅
- **B.** A full re-audit of every module topic with counsel present, scheduled as a half-day workshop before each major release train
- **C.** Whatever the client asks to see, since the review exists to satisfy stakeholder curiosity rather than a fixed checklist
- **D.** Only the consent records, since the other privacy properties are stable once set and never drift at all between deliveries

> **Answer:** A

### Q8. A rushed delivery is about to skip the privacy review to save time. What does this module observe about exactly that situation?

- **A.** Skipping is fine under deadline, since the review is a professional courtesy rather than a functional safeguard
- **B.** The review should be doubled for rushed work, growing to a full hour so the time pressure cannot ever compromise its quality
- **C.** The deliveries most likely to skip the review are the rushed ones most likely to need it; the habit exists for this moment  ✅
- **D.** Rushed deliveries are exempt because their smaller scope cannot introduce the drift the review is designed to catch

> **Answer:** C

### Q9. A builder treats privacy work as silent overhead inside projects. What reframe does this module offer?

- **A.** Correct: privacy is a cost center, and the professional move is absorbing it invisibly to keep proposals competitive
- **B.** Partially correct: only enterprise clients will pay for privacy work, so it stays overhead for everyone smaller
- **C.** Privacy work is a loss leader: give it away deliberately to win projects, and recover the cost on later phases
- **D.** Everything taught is billable: scoping sessions, inventories and flow maps, consent builds, rights work, reviews  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q10. A client asks what exactly you are selling with your privacy offering. Which positioning does this module supply?

- **A.** Full compliance certification, since clients want one vendor accountable for both the build and the legal conclusions
- **B.** I make your product able to honor privacy law; your lawyer confirms the legal side: the build plus the documents, plainly bounded  ✅
- **C.** Peace of mind as a managed service, with specifics left vague so the offering can flex to whatever the client assumes
- **D.** Legal advice at builder rates, since the knowledge in this course substitutes for counsel on routine questions

> **Answer:** B

### Q11. A proposal draft promises 'compliance guaranteed.' What does this module say about that phrase?

- **A.** It is a promise only counsel can underwrite: sell the build, the documents, and the collaboration, never the legal conclusion  ✅
- **B.** It is good marketing, since confident language wins work and clients understand puffery when they read a proposal
- **C.** It is fine when the build follows every module of this course, since correct mechanics amount to compliance in law
- **D.** It is acceptable if the contract defines compliance narrowly, since careful drafting can convert the promise into a feature list

> **Answer:** A

### Q12. A client's attorney joins the project. How does this module describe the relationship between your artifacts and their work?

- **A.** Redundancy: the attorney will rebuild the inventories from scratch anyway, so sharing your documents mostly creates needless confusion
- **B.** Tension: builder documentation constrains legal strategy, so counsel prefers projects that arrive without artifacts
- **C.** Your inventories, flow maps, and bases tables are exactly what lawyers need to work efficiently; the pairing makes both cheaper  ✅
- **D.** Hierarchy: counsel directs the build day to day, and the builder's documents exist mainly to record those directions

> **Answer:** C

### Q13. This module says your defaults should carry Modules 2 through 6 "without per-project heroics." What does that look like in practice?

- **A.** A senior builder reviewing every schema by hand, since expert attention is the only reliable carrier of privacy discipline
- **B.** A monthly all-hands privacy training, since shared awareness is what keeps every project aligned without extra process
- **C.** A client-signed privacy waiver per project, since documented risk acceptance removes the need for defaults entirely
- **D.** Starting schemas, form patterns, logging configs, and region choices that embody the discipline before any project begins  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q14. A skilled builder keeps privacy quality high by remembering everything personally. What does this module predict?

- **A.** If it depends on remembering, it loses to deadlines: templates, defaults, and checklists are what carry the discipline  ✅
- **B.** Memory scales fine for a solo practice, since the failure mode only appears once a team passes several builders
- **C.** Personal vigilance outperforms process, since checklists breed complacency in exactly the builders who most need sharpness
- **D.** The approach works until the first incident, which is the correct trigger for investing in process and tooling

> **Answer:** A

### Q15. You want AI to produce the privacy section for a new brief. Which prompt matches the directing pattern in this module?

- **A.** Certify this project as privacy compliant before scoping even ends, so the brief can list compliance as already achieved
- **B.** Draft the privacy section from the brief: classification, bases per purpose, regions, retention, rights, open questions  ✅
- **C.** Summarize this privacy course into the brief, since a condensed curriculum is the strongest scoping artifact available
- **D.** Write the marketing copy for the privacy features, since client-facing language is the scoping deliverable that matters

> **Answer:** B

### Q16. You point AI at an existing product to assess its privacy posture. Which instruction matches this module?

- **A.** Score the product against competitors, since relative standing is what determines whether remediation is worth funding
- **B.** List every file that mentions personal data, since raw occurrence counts are the measure a remediation plan starts from
- **C.** Rewrite the privacy notice to match current best practices, since the notice is the artifact that audits actually examine
- **D.** Review it as a privacy auditor using modules as lens: classification, bases, minimizing, transfers, rights, by severity  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q17. A prospect has a live product and no privacy history. Per this module, what is the natural first engagement?

- **A.** Offer the privacy review of the existing product; the findings become the remediation roadmap and often the next build  ✅
- **B.** Propose a full rebuild immediately, since products without privacy history are cheaper to replace than to assess
- **C.** Decline the work, since products built without privacy cannot be remediated by anyone except their original developers
- **D.** Send them directly to counsel, since a lawyer must inventory the product before any builder can look at it

> **Answer:** A

- **A.** GDPR Deep Dive for both, since the EU regime is the superset that covers health data and request handling alike
- **B.** US State Privacy for both, since domestic clients are governed end to end by the CCPA and CPRA family of laws
- **C.** HIPAA for the health data and DSAR Systems for the request volume, following where your clients actually are  ✅
- **D.** Neither yet, since downstream courses assume enterprise scale that a services practice takes years to reach

### Q19. A builder quietly ships strong privacy work inside every build, unbilled and unmentioned. Which mistake is this?

- **A.** Overselling, since silent competence creates expectations that the builder never priced and cannot sustain
- **B.** Underselling the capability: doing differentiating work silently when clients would pay for it by name  ✅
- **C.** No mistake: privacy work belongs inside the margin, and drawing attention to it invites uncomfortable questions
- **D.** Scope creep, since privacy features the client never ordered expand the build beyond its agreed boundaries

- **A.** In a shared drive folder owned by operations, refreshed whenever a client questionnaire reveals something is missing

### Q21. A health-sector client arrives, and the builder has only this fundamentals course. What does this module say about that situation?

- **A.** Fundamentals suffice for health data, since HIPAA is simply a labeling regime layered over the same general principles
- **B.** The builder should decline all regulated work permanently, since depth courses cannot close a fundamentals gap

### Q22. A client demands a compliance guarantee before signing. Per this module's decision framework, what is the response?

- **A.** Reframe it: you deliver capabilities and documentation, counsel delivers legal conclusions; together that is the guarantee that exists  ✅
- **B.** Provide the guarantee with an indemnity cap, since bounded exposure converts an impossible promise into a priced one
- **C.** Refuse to discuss guarantees at all, since the topic signals a client relationship not worth beginning in the first place

### Q23. A build ships honest layered notices, documented flows, and rights that are easy to exercise. Which two principles do these behaviors express?

- **A.** Proactive not reactive and full functionality, since early decisions and complete feature sets are what the notices demonstrate
- **B.** Privacy as default and end-to-end security, since notice quality is a function of configuration and encryption together
- **C.** Minimization and purpose limitation, since honest notices exist chiefly to document how little a product collects
- **D.** Visibility and transparency, plus respect for the user: honest documentation, and rights easy to exercise rather than hidden  ✅

### Q24. AI drafts your brief sections, checklists, and client summaries. What role does this module leave for official sources?

- **A.** None in practice, since regulator guidance changes too slowly to affect the artifacts a working builder produces
- **B.** AI drafts, official sources confirm: regulator guidance and provider trust pages remain the verification layer  ✅
- **C.** Official sources replace drafting entirely, since template language from regulators is safer than anything generated

### Q25. Looking across this whole module, what is the governing principle of building privacy into every project?
