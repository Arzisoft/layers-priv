## Module 7: Building Privacy Into Every Project

> Direct AI to make privacy a default of the build, because privacy bolted on at the end is the most expensive rebuild there is.

## Why This Matters

Six modules of privacy capability are worth little if they depend on you remembering them under deadline pressure. This module turns them into defaults: privacy by design as an operating habit, privacy requirements as a standard part of every build brief, a review you run before every delivery, and a service line clients pay for. This is also the launchpad for the rest of the privacy chain.

## Core Concepts

**Privacy by design, operationalized.** The famous principles reduce to builder behaviors: proactive not reactive (privacy decisions at scoping, not after incidents); privacy as the default setting (opt-ins unticked, minimal collection, shortest sensible retention, without user effort); privacy embedded in design (rights capabilities in the data model, not bolted on); full functionality (privacy and features, not privacy versus features); end-to-end security (protected in transit, at rest, and in access); visibility and transparency (honest notices, documented flows); respect for the user (their rights are easy to exercise, not hidden).

**Privacy requirements belong in the build brief.** Every project brief gets a privacy section: whose data, what data (classification), purposes and bases, regions and flows, retention, rights capabilities, and open legal questions. Written at scoping, it costs an hour; discovered at launch, it costs the schedule.

**The privacy review is a pre-delivery habit.** Like Module 7 of Cursor Mastery's smoke tests: a short checklist run before anything ships. Classification current? Consent flows working and recorded? Collection minimal, retention automated? Regions and flows as documented? Export and deletion tested on a real account? Notice matching reality? Ten minutes, every delivery.

**Privacy is a service offering, not just an overhead.** Everything this course taught is billable: privacy scoping sessions, data inventories and flow maps, consent flow implementation, rights-handling capabilities, privacy reviews of existing products, and remediation builds. Businesses need these and mostly cannot build them; you now can. Positioned plainly ("I make your product able to honor privacy law; your lawyer confirms the legal side"), it differentiates you from every builder who shrugs.

**This course is the gateway, not the destination.** Downstream courses go deep where this one went wide: GDPR Deep Dive for the EU regime in detail, HIPAA for US health data, US State Privacy for the CCPA/CPRA family and its siblings, and DSAR Systems for industrial-strength rights handling. Everything they teach stands on the definitions, bases, minimization, transfers, and rights mechanics you now hold.

## How It Works

Making it standard:

1. **Add the privacy section to your brief template.** Whose data, what data, purposes and bases, regions and flows, retention, rights, legal flags. Directed drafting makes this fast; your review makes it true.
1. **Default your defaults.** Your starting schemas, form patterns, logging configs, and region choices embody Modules 2 through 6 without per-project heroics.
1. **Install the review.** A checklist file in every repo; run it before every delivery; attach the result to the delivery note. Clients notice the professionalism even when they never read the checklist.
1. **Package the offerings.** A privacy scoping session, a data inventory deliverable, a consent implementation, a rights-capability build, a privacy review of an existing product. Fixed scopes, clear boundaries, "not legal advice" stated, lawyer collaboration welcomed.
1. **Keep counsel in the loop by design.** Your documents (inventories, flow maps, bases tables) are exactly what lawyers need to work efficiently; the pairing makes both of you faster and cheaper for the client.
1. **Continue the chain.** Route yourself to the downstream course your client base needs next: health clients, HIPAA; EU-heavy work, GDPR Deep Dive; US consumer products, US State Privacy; volume rights handling, DSAR Systems.

## Common Mistakes

- **Keeping privacy as heroics.** If it depends on remembering, it will lose to deadlines. Templates, defaults, and checklists are the fix.
- **Briefs without privacy sections.** The project that skipped the hour of scoping spends the week at launch.
- **Reviews skipped when busy.** The deliveries most likely to skip the review are the rushed ones most likely to need it.
- **Underselling the capability.** Doing the privacy work silently inside builds, unbilled and unmentioned, when it is a differentiator clients would pay for.
- **Overselling into legal territory.** "Compliance guaranteed" is a promise only counsel can underwrite. Sell the build, the documents, and the collaboration.
- **Stopping at fundamentals.** Health data without HIPAA depth, or an EU enterprise client without GDPR depth, is where the downstream courses stop being optional.

## Real-World Application

Three months after finishing this course, your practice looks different. Every brief has a privacy section; scoping conversations open with whose-data-what-data questions that make clients trust you faster. Your default project skeleton carries minimal forms, consent records, retention jobs, and a rights module, so compliance capability ships without being a line item. Before each delivery you run the ten-minute review; twice it has caught a notice that no longer matched reality. One client engagement began as a privacy review of an existing product and became your largest remediation build of the year, delivered alongside their attorney who now refers you to other clients. The work did not get slower. It got more valuable.

## Decision Framework

- **New project?** Brief with privacy section, defaults on, review scheduled.
- **Existing product, no privacy history?** Offer the review; findings become the remediation roadmap.
- **Client in health, finance, or kids' products?** Fundamentals plus the relevant deep course plus counsel, before commitments.
- **Client asks for a compliance guarantee?** Reframe: you deliver capabilities and documentation; their counsel delivers legal conclusions; together that is the guarantee that exists.
- **Which downstream course next?** Follow your clients: EU exposure, GDPR Deep Dive; US health, HIPAA; US consumer scale, US State Privacy; request volume, DSAR Systems.

## Tool and Platform Notes

- The brief template and review checklist live in your project skeleton repo, versioned like code.
- Regulator guidance and provider trust pages remain your verification sources; AI drafts, official sources confirm.
- The downstream chain (GDPR Deep Dive, HIPAA, US State Privacy, DSAR Systems) assumes this course; arrive with your templates in hand and they get sharper, not replaced.

## Key Takeaways

- Privacy by design means defaults, not memory: templates, skeletons, and checklists carry the discipline.
- Every brief gets a privacy section; every delivery gets the ten-minute review.
- The capabilities you now build are billable services; sell the build and the documents, never legal conclusions.
- Lawyer collaboration is a feature of your offering, not a threat to it.
- This is the gateway: GDPR Deep Dive, HIPAA, US State Privacy, and DSAR Systems build on exactly what you now know.
