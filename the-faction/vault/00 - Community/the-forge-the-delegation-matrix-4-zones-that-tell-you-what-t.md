# THE DELEGATION MATRIX — 4 Zones That Tell You What to Keep and What to Kill

THE DELEGATION MATRIX — 4 Zones That Tell You What to Keep and What to Kill

You can't scale yourself. And you shouldn't try. The Delegation Matrix gives you a framework for deciding what stays on your plate and what gets moved off — permanently.

Every task in your business falls into one of 4 zones:

ZONE 1: DELEGATE

These are tasks that need to get done but don't need YOU to do them. Admin work, scheduling, data entry, formatting, basic research. If someone else can do it 80% as well as you, delegate it. Your 80% hire at $25/hr frees your $500/hr time.

The trap: "It's faster if I just do it myself." That's true today. It's not true over the next 12 months. Train the person (or the AI) once. Free yourself forever.

ZONE 2: REPLACE

These are tasks that shouldn't involve a human at all. Automated reports, recurring emails, invoice generation, social media scheduling, data syncing. If the task follows the same steps every time, it's a replacement candidate.

Ask yourself: "Does this task require judgment, or does it just require execution?" If it's pure execution, replace it with automation.

ZONE 3: INVEST

These are high-value activities that only you can do — and that deserve MORE of your time. Strategy, relationship building, creative direction, vision-setting, high-stakes negotiations. These are the activities that actually move your business forward.

The irony: most CEOs spend 80% of their time in Zones 1 and 2, and maybe 20% in Zone 3. Flip that ratio.

ZONE 4: PRODUCE

These are the tasks where you're in your zone of genius — the work that generates the most value and that you're uniquely good at. For some, it's sales calls. For others, it's product design. For others, it's content.

Protect Zone 4 time aggressively. Block it on your calendar. Guard it like it's revenue — because it is.

THE EXERCISE:

List every task you did this week. Put each one in a zone. If more than 30% of your time is in Zones 1 or 2, you have a delegation problem. Start moving tasks out this week — not next month.
