# There is a discipline past AI Directed Engineering. Once the product is live, yo

There is a discipline past AI Directed Engineering. Once the product is live, you need to become a conversion engineer. How do people find you. What do they see first. Where do they hesitate. What makes them buy. What makes them leave. Those are not marketing questions. Those are systems questions. Today I walk through treating your sales funnel like your codebase. Version it. Test it. Measure it. Engineer the dollar.

**PROMPT:** Direct your AI: "Build a conversion engineering system for my product with three components: (1) Buyer journey map. Map every step from first discovery to payment confirmation. For each step, identify: the page or touchpoint, the action I want the user to take, the friction points that could cause them to leave, and the metric I should track (click-through rate, time on page, form completion rate, checkout abandonment rate). (2) Conversion instrumentation plan. For each step in the journey, define the tracking event, the tool that captures it, and the dashboard view I need to monitor it. Build this as a technical implementation spec I can hand to my AI to instrument across my application. (3) A/B testing roadmap. Identify the three highest-leverage conversion points in my funnel (where the most users drop off) and design an A/B test for each. Define the hypothesis, the variant, the success metric, and the minimum sample size needed to reach statistical significance."
