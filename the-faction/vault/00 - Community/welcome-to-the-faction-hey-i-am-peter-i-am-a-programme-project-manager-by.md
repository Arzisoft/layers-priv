Hey, I am Peter, I am a Programme / Project Manager by trade specialising in delivering technology projects. I am not a coder per se but have led software engineering teams in the past. I have immersed myself in vibe coding for the last 6 months, and out of it built a B2C platform that is about to go live and alongside a gated autonomous engineering framework that helps me build. I have called the framework Hallmark as its a layered authoritative model starting with me the author of an idea, to Claude AI the orchestrator, Claude Code the Conductor and agent helpers underneath, gates require minting to proceed. nothing happens without the authority from above.   ↳ **Matt Murphy** · 2026-08-15

  > Peter, I went through the one-pager and this is a lot more mature than I expected when you first described Hallmark.
  > 
  > The “no one marks their own work” principle is the piece I like most. Architect, builder, blind verification, security, human release authority is a legitimate governance model, not just a clever prompting workflow.
  > 
  > The nine-stage lifecycle, deliberately broken tests, blind re-verification, explicit declaration of missing evidence, tamper alerts, and append-only decision record tell me you’re designing around a very important assumption: the AI will eventually be wrong, so the system has to catch it rather than trust it. That’s exactly the right posture.
  > 
  > I’d pressure-test three things as you keep evolving it.
  > 
  > First, make sure the process doesn’t become a ceremony. Measure whether each gate is actually catching defects that would otherwise escape. Track false positives, escaped defects, review cycles, and time-to-release. A gate that never changes an outcome may eventually just be expensive theater.
  > 
  > Second, watch the founder gate as you scale. One human holding final release authority is fantastic while one product is being built. Eventually that same safety mechanism can become the bottleneck, so you’ll want to think about delegated authority without losing accountability.
  > 
  > Third, be careful with language around the OWASP piece. OWASP gives you excellent security standards and verification guidance, but I wouldn’t treat an automated pass as proof that a build “conforms to industry standards.” I’d frame it as another independent security control that produces evidence for the release decision. Security is never one green checkmark.
  > 
  > But, this is really good work. You’re doing something I think a lot of AI-native builders are eventually going to discover they need: speed on the execution side, friction on the authority side.
  > 
  > And the irony is that the intentional friction probably makes you faster overall because you spend less time recovering from AI-generated stupidity later. 👊😎
