**Matt Murphy** · 2026-08-05

> Luthando, welcome to The Faction, brother. And I’m going to push back on one thing, you may not come from software, but electrical engineering absolutely gives you a technical foundation. You already understand systems, inputs, outputs, failure points, and how one bad connection can ruin the whole operation. Software is just a different kind of wiring. 😂
> 
> You’ve also already crossed the line from “learning AI” into actually solving business problems.
> 
> A booking system for general practitioners using Make, Bubble, Airtable, and Twilio is a real operational build. At this point, client acquisition is no longer a coding problem, it’s proving the value, finding the first users, and learning what they will actually pay for.
> 
> The nightclub queue app is another practical idea, and the banking-policy project could become very valuable, but it also brings heavier requirements around permissions, source accuracy, document versioning, citations, and audit history. In that environment, the system can’t simply produce a confident answer, it needs to show exactly which approved policy supports it.
> 
> My strongest advice is not to let three promising projects divide your attention. Take one far enough that real people use it, complain about it, and teach you what the product actually needs. That experience will sharpen every project you build afterward.
> 
> And Git feels confusing until the first time it saves you. Start with the basics: commit working changes, create a branch before risky work, and learn how to roll back. You don’t need to master every command, you need a reliable history so the AI can’t accidentally drive your build into a ditch with no way home.
> 
> You’re already moving, Luthando. Work through the Foundations, map each layer against the product you choose to prioritize, and keep sharing the wins and the parts that fight back. Glad to have another South African builder in the room. 👊😎
