# Transcript — A few years ago I had a conversation with a client that changed the way I scope

> A few years ago I had a conversation with a client that changed the way I scope every project.

A few years ago, I had a conversation with a client that changed the way I scope every project at Faction. They came to me with this beautiful application. It was really well designed, working features, users were signing up all day long. The conversation was about scaling it. So I asked them a question.

What's your biggest cost right now? And their answer, as always, engineering. Right? My bill. Which I expected, right?

But then I asked the second question. What's your second biggest cost? They all kind of looked at each other. They couldn't answer. They did not know what their hosting costs were.

They did not know what their third party API costs were. They did not know what their egress costs were. The application was making money and users were signing up, but they had no idea if the unit metrics worked at ten times the current volume. And that conversation taught me something, right? Builders think about features, but business owners, we think about the margins too.

And the best builders, they think about both at every stage of the project. From that point forward, every project I scoped at Faction include a cost model. Not just what it costs to build it, but also what it costs to run it at current scale, at 10x scale, at 100x scale. Because the application that scales and loses money at every level, that's not even a business. It's just a really expensive hobby.

Think about the margins while you're building. This isn't just about building. You gotta make money with it if it's a product.
