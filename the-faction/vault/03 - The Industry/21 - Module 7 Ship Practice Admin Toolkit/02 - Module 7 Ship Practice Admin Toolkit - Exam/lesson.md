# Module 7: Ship: Practice Admin Toolkit — Exam

> Exam for **Module 7: Ship: Practice Admin Toolkit** (The Industry) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. A dermatology practice hires you to build the full admin toolkit: intake, scheduling, billing support, and communication. Before directing AI to build anything, what should you produce first?

- **A.** A feature list for each of the four tools, ranked by how much staff time each one is projected to save the practice weekly.
- **B.** An integration map showing every data flow between the four components and every handoff to the practice's existing systems.  ✅
- **C.** A working prototype of the intake tool, since intake generates the patient data the other three components depend on later.
- **D.** A training curriculum for each staff role so the whole team is fully prepared before any of the components can go live.

> **Answer:** B

### Q2. In a family medicine rollout, the front desk retypes each completed digital intake into the scheduler because the two tools do not share data. The office manager says staff are managing fine. What is the real problem?

- **A.** The intake tool captured the wrong fields, so the scheduler cannot accept its output until staff edit every record first.
- **B.** Staff have become the integration layer, so you shipped a new manual job instead of a toolkit, and usage will decay under it.  ✅
- **C.** The front desk needs more scheduler training so the retyping becomes fast enough that it stops delaying patient check in.
- **D.** The office manager is overstating how well staff are coping, and you should survey the whole team before changing anything.

> **Answer:** B

### Q3. A five-provider orthopedics practice wants the whole toolkit. You must choose which component goes live in week one. Following sound deployment sequencing, what do you launch first, and why?

- **A.** Billing support, because revenue impact is the strongest proof of value and wins the physicians over before anything else.
- **B.** Intake, because it generates the patient data that every other component depends on, and nothing downstream works without it.
- **C.** Scheduling and reminders, because the win is fast and visible, and it builds the confidence that carries the later phases.  ✅
- **D.** Communication, because outreach touches every patient and delivers the largest visible change on the very first day live.

> **Answer:** C

### Q4. You are drawing the integration map for a pediatric group. The office manager asks what should happen the moment a parent books a new patient visit. Per the toolkit's data flows, what is the correct answer?

- **A.** Booking posts the visit to the billing dashboard so the biller can begin preparing the superbill before the child arrives.
- **B.** Booking starts the recall sequence so the family receives outreach about future well visits alongside the confirmation.
- **C.** Booking alerts the front desk to call the parent, collect the history verbally, and enter it so records are complete early.
- **D.** Booking triggers the intake workflow, so forms reach the parent automatically and the completed data feeds verification.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q5. An urgent care owner wants all four components launched on the same Monday to rip off the bandage. How should you respond?

- **A.** Agree, since a single launch means one training push, one go-live date, and less total disruption than four separate cutovers.
- **B.** Agree, but double the training hours in the week before launch so every role has practiced all four systems in advance.
- **C.** Compromise by launching two components on Monday and the other two that Friday so the change is split across one week.
- **D.** Explain that staff cannot absorb four new systems at once, and propose a phased rollout where each component lands in turn.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q6. During rollout planning for a multi-provider OB/GYN group, the practice manager asks why billing support is scheduled last when it is the tool the owners care about most. What is your best answer?

- **A.** Billing support carries the most compliance risk, so it needs the longest review period before any staff can touch it live.
- **B.** The biller is the busiest person in the practice, so their training must wait until the front desk is fully self sufficient.
- **C.** Billing support depends on clean data flowing from every upstream component, so it works only after the rest are stable.  ✅
- **D.** Owners lose interest quickly, so saving the tool they want most for last keeps leadership engaged through the full rollout.

> **Answer:** C

### Q7. You are designing training for the front desk at a busy family practice. They have about ninety seconds between patients. Which training approach fits how this team actually works?

- **A.** A one hour all hands demo of the full toolkit so every role sees how the components connect before anyone uses them alone.
- **B.** A complete written manual covering all four components that staff can study at home before the go-live date arrives.
- **C.** Weekly lunch and learn sessions across month one so the material is spaced out and repeated until it finally sticks.
- **D.** Role-specific quick reference cards and two minute walkthroughs anchored to each task, plus a named go-to person on site.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q8. In a three-provider pediatric practice, you can only train one person in depth on the whole toolkit. Who should it be, and why?

- **A.** The lead physician, because clinical authority is what makes the rest of the staff take a new system seriously in practice.
- **B.** The office manager, trained deepest as your champion, so authority and answers flow through someone staff already follow.  ✅
- **C.** The newest hire, because they have no old habits to unlearn and the most open schedule for extended training sessions.
- **D.** The biller, because billing support is the most complex component and errors there cost the practice real revenue quickly.

> **Answer:** B

### Q9. A month before launching reminders for an orthopedics group, you plan your adoption measurement. What must you capture now for any later improvement claim to be honest?

- **A.** A pre-launch baseline of current no-show rates and related metrics, recorded before any piece of the toolkit goes live.  ✅
- **B.** Staff predictions of how much time each tool will save, so you can compare expectations against actual results.
- **C.** A list of every reminder the practice currently sends manually, so the new system can replicate the full set on day one.
- **D.** The owners' target no-show rate, so the deployment has a concrete goal number it can be measured against after go-live.

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. Two weeks after launching digital intake at a dermatology clinic, the staff tell you they love it. Which measure best tells you whether intake is actually being adopted?

- **A.** The digital intake completion rate compared against how often the front desk falls back to handing patients a clipboard.  ✅
- **B.** The number of positive comments staff volunteer during your weekly check-in calls with the office manager and the team.
- **C.** How many staff attended the intake training session and completed the full walkthrough before the tool went live for patients.
- **D.** The total count of intake form links sent to patients since launch, since more links sent means the tool is in active use.

> **Answer:** A

### Q11. A practice manager at an urgent care says the toolkit launch went great and staff seem happy. Before you agree, what should you check?

- **A.** Whether the owner shares that view, since leadership perception ultimately decides if the engagement continues next quarter.
- **B.** Whether any staff have filed complaints, since an absence of negative feedback is the clearest sign adoption is on track.
- **C.** The usage numbers: intake completions, reminder confirmations, recall bookings, and queue actions against the baseline.  ✅
- **D.** Whether training completion reached one hundred percent, since a fully trained team is the best predictor of adoption.

> **Answer:** C

### Q12. One week after go-live at a family practice, you have fourteen friction reports ranging from a confusing button label to reminders sent at the wrong time. How do you decide what to fix first?

- **A.** Fix the reports in the order they arrived, so every staff member sees their feedback handled fairly and nobody feels ignored.
- **B.** Fix the easiest items first so the fix count climbs quickly and staff see visible momentum in the first week after launch.
- **C.** Hold all fixes until month two so you can batch them into one stable release instead of disrupting staff with small changes.
- **D.** Rank the reports by impact on staff time and practice revenue, fix the top items fast, then announce each fix to the staff.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q13. You fixed the top three friction items at a pediatric practice within days of the reports. What should you do next, and why does it matter for adoption?

- **A.** Announce the fixes to staff so they see the system responding to them, which keeps them reporting instead of reverting.  ✅
- **B.** Move straight to the next items on the list, since visible progress on the backlog matters more than talking about the work.
- **C.** Wait for staff to notice the changes themselves, since unprompted discovery is the most reliable signal the fixes landed.
- **D.** Report the fixes to the owner only, since leadership confidence is what keeps the engagement funded through the quarter.

> **Answer:** A

### Q14. Three weeks after launch at a dermatology practice, nobody has complained, but digital intake completions have fallen every week and clipboard use is climbing. What is happening?

- **A.** Normal seasonality: patient volume shifts week to week, so completion counts will vary and the trend is not yet meaningful.
- **B.** A quiet revert: staff are drifting back to old habits without announcing it, and the metrics are the only place it shows.  ✅
- **C.** A data problem: falling numbers with no complaints usually means the dashboard is undercounting completed digital forms.
- **D.** A patient problem: staff are using the tool correctly, but patients prefer paper, so the design needs a patient facing fix.

> **Answer:** B

### Q15. You confirm the quiet revert at that dermatology practice is real: the front desk hands out clipboards whenever the lobby gets busy. What is the right intervention?

- **A.** Ask the owner to require digital intake for every patient, since a mandate from leadership removes the choice causing drift.
- **B.** Find the friction that makes the digital path slower under pressure, fix it fast, and reset the workflow with the manager.  ✅
- **C.** Retire digital intake for walk-in periods and keep it for scheduled visits, since the tool has proven unfit for busy lobbies.
- **D.** Retrain the whole team from the beginning, since a full reset of the original training is the way to rebuild correct habits.

> **Answer:** B

### Q16. The practice management system at an OB/GYN group cannot accept an automated feed from your recall tool, and you cannot connect them directly. What should you do?

- **A.** Define the handoff explicitly: who moves the data, when, and in what format, documented as a formal step in the workflow.  ✅
- **B.** Leave the gap for staff to work around, since experienced front desk teams already move data between systems all day long.
- **C.** Drop recall from the toolkit scope, since a component that cannot integrate cleanly will undermine the rest of the system.
- **D.** Delay the whole deployment until the practice replaces its management system with one that supports automated data feeds.

> **Answer:** A

### Q17. At a family practice, patients complain about recall outreach telling them to book visits they already booked. Which design flaw does this expose?

- **A.** The recall sequence cannot see current schedule state, so outreach fires without checking whether the patient rebooked.  ✅
- **B.** The outreach copy is unclear, so patients misread a routine confirmation message as a request to book an additional visit.
- **C.** The cadence is far too aggressive, so messages overlap and patients receive the next touch before acting on the last one.
- **D.** The patient list is stale, so the sequence is drawing from an export taken before the most recent appointments were made.

> **Answer:** A

### Q18. You are scoping the capstone deliverable for a multi-provider group. The practice manager asks whether the adoption dashboard is included or costs extra. Per the toolkit's design, what is the right answer?

- **A.** It is an extra: dashboards are a separate reporting product, and bundling one into the toolkit dilutes the deployment work.
- **B.** It is part of the deliverable: a dashboard the manager checks weekly is how the practice knows the toolkit is working.  ✅
- **C.** It is optional: offer it only after adoption stabilizes, since early numbers are too noisy to be worth a manager's time.
- **D.** It is temporary: build it for the first month only, then retire it once the launch data confirms the rollout succeeded.

> **Answer:** B

### Q19. A practice owner suggests you deliver one all-hands hour covering the entire toolkit as the whole training plan. What is wrong with this approach?

- **A.** A room-wide demo transfers almost nothing; the biller, front desk, and manager each need a short path through their tasks.  ✅
- **B.** One hour is too short for four components, so the session should be extended to a half day with time for hands on practice.
- **C.** Owners should not set training formats, so you should insist the office manager design the plan for the team instead.
- **D.** All-hands sessions work only after go-live, so the demo should be rescheduled to the end of the first week of real usage.

> **Answer:** A

### Q20. Before go-live at an urgent care, you direct AI to draft the deployment runbook. Which contents make the runbook complete?

- **A.** A full feature inventory for all four components, with screenshots, so staff can reference every function in one document.
- **B.** The training schedule, attendance tracking for each session, and a quiz that every staff member passes before launch day.
- **C.** The integration map, the vendor contacts for the PMS and EHR, and a log of every configuration change made in the build.
- **D.** The rollout sequence, an owner per phase, go-live dates per component, a rollback plan, and a definition of done per phase.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q21. Two months after a successful launch at a pediatrics group, the deployment is stable. How should you treat the account from here?

- **A.** As complete: a stable deployment means your work is done, and lingering can make the practice feel dependent on the builder.
- **B.** As a maintenance contract only: respond when something breaks, since unsolicited check-ins read as selling more work.
- **C.** As the start of a relationship: monthly metric check-ins, quarterly reviews of what to build next, and boundary upkeep.  ✅
- **D.** As a referral source only: shift your attention to landing the next practice and revisit this one when leads run dry.

> **Answer:** C

### Q22. The office manager at a family practice you deployed asks you to add a feature that suggests diagnosis codes from visit notes. How do you respond?

- **A.** Accept it, since a deployed relationship means the practice trusts you, and turning down requests risks losing the account.
- **B.** Accept it with a disclaimer that the biller must verify every suggestion, since human review keeps the feature admin side.
- **C.** Decline the clinical scope drift, explain the boundary, and redirect to an admin-side alternative that serves the need.  ✅
- **D.** Refer them to their EHR vendor and close the topic, since anything touching visit notes is entirely outside your work.

> **Answer:** C

### Q23. A colleague tells you fixes in the first month after launch can wait until things settle, since staff expect some rough edges. Why is this wrong for a practice deployment?

- **A.** The speed of response in month one determines whether staff invest in the system or quietly revert to their old habits.  ✅
- **B.** Rough edges compound technically, so small defects left in place become expensive rebuilds once the data volume grows.
- **C.** Owners review invoices monthly, so a visible burst of early fixes is what justifies the deployment fee at first review.
- **D.** Staff will escalate formal complaints upward, and physician leadership will cancel the project before month two begins.

> **Answer:** A

### Q24. You want to tell an orthopedics owner that no-shows are at eight percent after the reminder launch. Under honest measurement, what makes that claim meaningful?

- **A.** A benchmark from published industry data showing that eight percent is below the national average for orthopedic groups.
- **B.** Sign-off from the practice manager that eight percent matches their impression of how the front desk schedule has looked.
- **C.** A recorded pre-launch baseline showing where no-shows stood before, so eight percent reflects movement, not a bare number.  ✅
- **D.** Three consecutive weeks at eight percent or lower, since a sustained streak proves the reminder system caused the change.

> **Answer:** C

### Q25. You are presenting your capstone philosophy to a practice before signing. In one principle, what defines success for the Practice Admin Toolkit?

- **A.** Coverage: the toolkit succeeds when all four components are built, connected, and delivered on the agreed launch date.
- **B.** Satisfaction: the toolkit succeeds when the owner and the office manager both say the practice is happy with the system.
- **C.** Capability: the toolkit succeeds when every workflow the practice performs manually today has an automated equivalent.
- **D.** Live is not adopted: the toolkit succeeds only when staff actually use it and the usage metrics prove it against baseline.  ✅

> **Answer:** D
