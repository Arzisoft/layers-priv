# Module 5: Job Site Safety & Compliance Data — Exam

> Exam for **Module 5: Job Site Safety & Compliance Data** (The Industry) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. Your AI builds an inspection checklist that takes 15 minutes to complete on a phone. The superintendent goes back to paper forms. What went wrong?

- **A.** Safety forms for field use must be completable in under 3 minutes with dropdowns and photo capture not text entry  ✅
- **B.** The checklist should be available on tablets only because phone screens are too small for detailed inspections
- **C.** The superintendent needs additional training on the mobile interface before they can complete inspections efficiently
- **D.** The inspection checklist should be pre-filled with passing results so the superintendent only marks items that fail

> **Answer:** A

### Q2. A worker with an expired confined space certification is assigned to enter a confined space. Your platform does not flag the issue. What is missing?

- **A.** A scheduling rule that blocks all confined space work on days when the platform detects weather conditions are unsafe
- **B.** A manual verification process where the superintendent checks each worker credentials before every task assignment
- **C.** Certification tracking with expiration alerts that prevents assignment of workers to tasks requiring current credentials  ✅
- **D.** A company-wide training schedule that automatically renews all worker certifications before their expiration dates arrive

> **Answer:** C

### Q3. Your incident report form captures the date and a text description but nothing else. OSHA requests full documentation. What fields are missing?

- **A.** A field for the estimated financial cost of the incident because OSHA requires a dollar amount on every reported event
- **B.** The name of the worker personal physician so OSHA can verify the medical treatment received after the incident
- **C.** A digital signature from the company lawyer confirming that the incident report has been reviewed for legal accuracy
- **D.** Time, location, injured party, witnesses, severity classification, root cause analysis, and corrective actions taken  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q4. Toolbox talks happen weekly on your job site but the platform has no way to document them. OSHA asks for proof of regular safety training. What do you show?

- **A.** The company safety policy document which outlines the requirement for weekly toolbox talks on all active project sites
- **B.** Nothing, because without documented topics, dates, and attendee sign-in records there is no verifiable proof they occurred  ✅
- **C.** The superintendent verbal confirmation that toolbox talks happen every Monday morning before the crew starts daily work
- **D.** The meeting room booking schedule which shows a weekly recurring event labeled safety meeting on the project calendar

> **Answer:** B

### Q5. Your inspection checklist identifies a fall hazard but no corrective action is generated. The hazard remains unaddressed for two weeks. What should the platform do?

- **A.** Generate a corrective action item with an assigned responsible person and a deadline that tracks through to resolution  ✅
- **B.** Automatically shut down the area flagged as a fall hazard until the superintendent manually marks it as resolved
- **C.** Send a daily email reminder to the project manager about the open hazard until they acknowledge it in the platform
- **D.** Escalate the unresolved finding directly to OSHA since the two-week delay clearly represents a failure to address safety risk

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. Your platform accepts subcontractor insurance certificates but does not track their expiration dates. A sub insurance lapses mid-project. What is the risk?

- **A.** The sub is automatically removed from the project schedule and cannot return to the site until coverage is restored
- **B.** The lapsed certificate triggers an audit flag that the GC insurance carrier investigates during the annual policy review
- **C.** The sub is working uninsured and if an injury occurs the GC client insurance may have to cover the claim instead  ✅
- **D.** The subcontractor is personally liable for any incidents and the project has no additional financial risk from the lapse

> **Answer:** C

### Q7. Your safety platform uses the same inspection checklist for every project type. A high-rise project has fall protection hazards that a residential remodel does not. What needs to change?

- **A.** The checklist should include every possible hazard type so it works for all project types without any customization
- **B.** The superintendent should manually add project-specific hazard items to the standard checklist before each inspection
- **C.** A separate platform instance should be created for each project type with its own preconfigured safety checklists
- **D.** Inspection checklists should be configurable by project type so each project gets hazard items relevant to its scope  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q8. OSHA arrives for a surprise audit and requests all incident reports from the past five years organized by project and date. Your records are in a shared drive with no structure. What happens?

- **A.** The OSHA inspector accepts the unstructured records as long as all required reports are present somewhere in the files
- **B.** Producing the organized records takes days of manual sorting which extends the audit and increases scrutiny from OSHA  ✅
- **C.** The company can request a 30-day extension from OSHA to organize the records before the audit formally continues
- **D.** OSHA cannot penalize the company for poor record organization because the requirement is only that records exist

> **Answer:** B

### Q9. Your daily inspection form has a checkbox for no hazards found with no option to describe what was actually inspected. Is this sufficient?

- **A.** No, the form must document which specific areas and hazard categories were inspected even when no issues are found  ✅
- **B.** Yes, a no-hazards checkbox is the standard format for daily inspections when the superintendent finds nothing to report
- **C.** The form is sufficient if the superintendent signs it because the signature certifies that a complete inspection occurred
- **D.** Only a licensed safety professional can determine whether the inspection form meets the minimum documentation standard

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. A corrective action for a trench safety violation is marked complete in the system but no one verified the fix on site. What is the problem?

- **A.** The system should automatically close corrective actions after the assigned deadline passes without requiring verification
- **B.** The project manager should verify all corrective actions remotely by reviewing photos uploaded by the superintendent
- **C.** Corrective actions must include a physical verification step where someone confirms the fix on site before closing it  ✅
- **D.** Trench safety violations are severe enough that only OSHA can verify the corrective action and close the finding record

> **Answer:** C

### Q11. Your worker certification database tracks names and cert types but not expiration dates. A forklift operator cert expired six months ago. What risk does this create?

- **A.** The forklift manufacturer warranty is voided because the operator is using the equipment without a current certification
- **B.** The worker is automatically reassigned to non-equipment tasks by the scheduling system when their cert data is missing
- **C.** The project insurance carrier denies the entire project policy renewal because one operator certification has lapsed
- **D.** An uncertified operator running equipment is an OSHA violation and creates serious safety risk if an incident occurs  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q12. Your platform logs safety incidents but does not classify them by severity. The PM treats a near-miss the same as a lost-time injury. What should change?

- **A.** Near-miss events should be removed from the incident log entirely since they did not result in any actual harm or injury
- **B.** Severity classifications should be added so incidents are triaged by impact and the most serious get immediate attention  ✅
- **C.** All incidents regardless of their severity level should receive the exact same level of response to maintain a consistent safety culture
- **D.** Severity classification should be assigned by the injured worker since they are the best judge of the incident impact

> **Answer:** B

### Q13. Your toolbox talk documentation captures the topic and date but not which workers attended. Why does this create a compliance gap?

- **A.** OSHA requires a minimum number of attendees per toolbox talk and without a count the meeting does not officially qualify
- **B.** The workers who missed the talk cannot be identified for makeup training because there is no record of who was absent
- **C.** Attendee records prove which workers received specific safety training and without them there is no individual proof  ✅
- **D.** The toolbox talk topic selection is invalid without attendee data because different groups require different safety topics

> **Answer:** C

### Q14. Your platform generates OSHA Form 300 logs automatically from incident reports. The log is missing required fields. What should you verify?

- **A.** That every incident report captures all fields required by OSHA Form 300 including case number, classification, and days away from work  ✅
- **B.** That the automatic generation process runs on the first business day of each month to keep the OSHA log continuously current
- **C.** That only incidents classified as severe are included in the Form 300 because minor incidents do not require OSHA logging
- **D.** That the company lawyer has reviewed each incident entry before it is added to the Form 300 log for regulatory submission

> **Answer:** A

### Q15. An insurance certificate uploaded to your platform shows coverage of $1M but the project requires $2M. The platform accepts it without flagging the gap. What is missing?

- **A.** A manual review process where the project manager compares every certificate against the contractual coverage minimum
- **B.** A warning to the subcontractor that their insurance is below the project minimum and they should contact their carrier
- **C.** A database of insurance carrier ratings so the platform can verify that the coverage is from a financially stable company
- **D.** Coverage validation rules that compare the uploaded certificate amounts against the project minimum insurance requirements  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q16. Your platform sends certification expiration alerts by email only. Field workers rarely check email. What delivery method should you add?

- **A.** Print the expiration alerts and post them on the job site bulletin board where field workers check for daily announcements
- **B.** Push notifications or SMS alerts that reach the worker and their superintendent on mobile devices they carry on the site  ✅
- **C.** A weekly report sent to the company HR department so they can verbally notify workers about upcoming expirations
- **D.** An automated phone call system that dials the worker directly and reads the expiration alert using text-to-speech voice

> **Answer:** B

### Q17. Your platform allows backdating of inspection records. A superintendent fills in three missed days of inspections at once. Why is this a compliance problem?

- **A.** Backdated inspections consume additional storage space because the system creates multiple records for the same calendar day
- **B.** The platform performance slows down when multiple inspection records are created simultaneously in a single session
- **C.** Backdated inspections may not reflect actual site conditions on those dates which undermines their regulatory validity  ✅
- **D.** OSHA software systems can detect timestamp inconsistencies in backdated records during an electronic audit submission

> **Answer:** C

### Q18. Your safety system tracks incidents but has no way to analyze trends across projects. The company keeps having the same type of injury. What feature is needed?

- **A.** Cross-project safety analytics that identify recurring incident types, high-risk activities, and common root causes  ✅
- **B.** A mandatory monthly safety review meeting where PMs discuss incidents verbally with company leadership
- **C.** An AI-powered prediction model that forecasts which projects will have incidents based on weather and crew size data
- **D.** A comparison dashboard that ranks projects by total incident count so the company can identify the most dangerous sites

> **Answer:** A

### Q19. Your platform stores safety records on a single server with no backup. The server fails and all inspection records from the past two years are lost. What should have been in place?

- **A.** A printed archive of every inspection record stored in a fireproof cabinet at the company main office as a physical backup
- **B.** An agreement with the hosting provider guaranteeing zero data loss because safety records are classified as critical data
- **C.** A manual export process where the safety officer downloads all records to a local drive at the end of every work week
- **D.** Automated backups with cross-region replication and tested restore procedures that ensure safety data is never permanently lost  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q20. A subcontractor provides their insurance certificate as a paper copy. Your platform only accepts digital uploads. How should you handle this?

- **A.** Reject the paper certificate and require the subcontractor to obtain a digital copy from their insurance carrier directly
- **B.** Scan or photograph the paper certificate and upload it so the platform can track the coverage details and expiration  ✅
- **C.** Accept the paper copy as sufficient and file it separately from the platform since not all documents need to be digital
- **D.** Ask the subcontractor to type their insurance information into a platform form instead of uploading the actual certificate

> **Answer:** B

### Q21. Your platform has all safety features built but adoption is low because superintendents find it slower than their current paper process. What should you focus on?

- **A.** Reducing the time per form to under 3 minutes using dropdowns, photo capture, and pre-populated fields for field speed  ✅
- **B.** Making the safety features mandatory so superintendents have no choice but to use the platform instead of paper forms
- **C.** Adding gamification elements like badges and leaderboards to encourage superintendents to complete more inspections
- **D.** Providing a comprehensive detailed training course that teaches superintendents the full capabilities of the platform safety module

> **Answer:** A

### Q22. Your corrective action tracking has an assigned person but no deadline. A finding from two months ago is still open with no urgency. What field is missing?

- **A.** A severity rating on the corrective action that determines the priority level and automatically assigns it to a queue
- **B.** A notification escalation path that alerts the next level of management after a configurable number of days pass
- **C.** A comments section where the assigned person can document progress updates toward completing the corrective action
- **D.** A required completion deadline that creates accountability and allows the system to flag overdue items for follow-up  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q23. Your safety audit shows full compliance with all documentation requirements. How often should the audit be repeated?

- **A.** Only after a recordable safety incident occurs because a clean result means the system is functioning as it was designed
- **B.** Once per year during the annual OSHA reporting period when the company submits the Form 300A summary document
- **C.** Regularly and after any process changes because new projects, crews, and procedures can introduce compliance gaps  ✅
- **D.** Safety audits do not need repeating after a passing result because the platform enforces compliance automatically

> **Answer:** C

### Q24. Your inspection form captures findings but does not include a photo attachment option. The superintendent describes a cracked foundation in text only. What is lost?

- **A.** The inspection is invalid without a photo attachment because OSHA requires visual documentation for every single finding
- **B.** Visual evidence that supports the written finding and provides undeniable context that text alone cannot fully convey  ✅
- **C.** The ability to auto-categorize the finding because the platform uses image recognition to classify inspection hazards
- **D.** The GPS location data that the platform extracts from inspection photos to automatically populate the location field

> **Answer:** B

### Q25. You complete all T5 M5 safety requirements. What ongoing practice ensures your construction safety platform remains compliant as regulations evolve?

- **A.** Monitor OSHA regulatory updates and review platform checklists and forms against current requirements periodically  ✅
- **B.** Subscribe to an industry newsletter that summarizes safety regulation changes and forward it to the development team
- **C.** Rely on the AI coding tool to automatically update safety forms when it detects that regulatory requirements have changed
- **D.** Wait for an OSHA audit finding to identify gaps because proactive monitoring of regulatory changes is not cost effective

> **Answer:** A
