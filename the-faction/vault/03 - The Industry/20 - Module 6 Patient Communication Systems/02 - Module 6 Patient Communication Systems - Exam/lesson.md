# Module 6: Patient Communication Systems — Exam

> Exam for **Module 6: Patient Communication Systems** (The Industry) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. A behavioral health clinic wants its SMS reminders to read 'Your therapy session is Thursday at 3.' The front desk sees no issue since no diagnosis is named. What do you tell them?

- **A.** The template is fine, since therapy is a service description rather than a diagnosis or a treatment detail.
- **B.** The template is fine as long as patients opted in to text reminders during the intake process.
- **C.** Naming the visit type reveals care the patient receives, so the text carries only practice, date, and time.  ✅
- **D.** The wording is acceptable if the message also includes instructions for opting out of future text messages.

> **Answer:** C

### Q2. A family medicine practice sends appointment reminders by standard SMS. The office manager asks what the texts are allowed to contain. What do you specify?

- **A.** Practice name, date, time, and a secure link, with any clinical detail held behind the portal login.  ✅
- **B.** The visit reason and provider specialty, since patients need that context to remember why they booked.
- **C.** Whatever the patient consented to at intake, since a signed consent form covers content in any channel.
- **D.** A short clinical summary of the upcoming visit, so patients arrive prepared and staff field fewer calls.

> **Answer:** A

### Q3. You audit reminder templates for a multi-provider group. One SMS reads: 'Reminder: your diabetes check with Dr. Patel is Tuesday at 2.' What is the problem?

- **A.** It lacks a confirmation option, so the practice cannot track whether the patient plans to attend the visit.
- **B.** Naming the condition in an insecure text is a disclosure, so the template must drop the clinical detail.  ✅
- **C.** Naming the provider is the violation, since staff identities are protected information in any message.
- **D.** The send timing is wrong, since reminders delivered on weekdays see lower confirmation rates than weekends.

> **Answer:** B

### Q4. You are mapping the communication calendar for a pediatric practice. The office manager asks what you will define for each touchpoint on the map. What do you list?

- **A.** A trigger, a channel, an owner, and a template, so every touch fires predictably with someone accountable.  ✅
- **B.** A budget, a vendor, and a monthly send volume, so that leadership can forecast the messaging spend accurately.
- **C.** A patient persona, a tone guide, and a brand voice, so every message reads consistently across the panel.
- **D.** A backup channel, an escalation contact, and a legal reviewer, so no message goes out without a sign off.

> **Answer:** A

### Q5. An orthopedics group hires you to overhaul patient messaging. Confirmations, recalls, and review requests are all in scope. What do you produce first?

- **A.** The recall sequence, since retention is the cheapest revenue and recalls carry the highest return.
- **B.** A map of every touchpoint in the patient relationship, since that calendar makes the whole build coherent.  ✅
- **C.** The review request flow, since reputation gains compound and it is the fastest system to stand up and test.
- **D.** The portal integration, since secure infrastructure has to exist before any outbound message can reference it.

> **Answer:** B

### Q6. A dermatology clinic loses annual skin-check patients who forget to rebook. You are designing the recall system. What is the foundation of the build?

- **A.** A due-date registry keyed to visit types, generating outreach queues instead of a spreadsheet someone updates.  ✅
- **B.** A December batch campaign that emails the full patient panel a reminder to schedule annual appointments.
- **C.** A front-desk call list printed weekly, since live phone outreach converts better than automated channels.
- **D.** A standing social media campaign reminding followers that annual skin checks are recommended for adults.

> **Answer:** A

### Q7. An OB/GYN practice's recall texts say 'You are due for a visit, call us to schedule.' Response stays poor even after three touches. What is the fix?

- **A.** Add a fourth and fifth touch, since overdue patients need more repetitions before acting.
- **B.** Move all touches to phone calls, since voice outreach carries more urgency than any text or email message.
- **C.** Shorten the message further, since briefer texts see higher engagement across healthcare messaging tools.
- **D.** Replace the call-us dead end with a direct booking link, so the sequence closes the loop in a single tap.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q8. A family medicine practice ran one recall email blast last year and almost nobody rebooked. The manager now doubts recall works at all. What do you redesign?

- **A.** The email subject lines, since open rates are the main lever and better copy will lift response directly.
- **B.** The send time, since recall emails delivered midweek at midday outperform blasts sent on other schedules.
- **C.** The outreach itself, as a multi-touch sequence with escalating channels, since one message recovers few.  ✅
- **D.** The audience, since recall should target only recently active patients who are most likely to respond.

> **Answer:** C

### Q9. A pediatric practice wants lab results texted directly to parents to save portal logins. The manager says parents find the portal annoying. What do you advise?

- **A.** Results stay behind authentication, and the text carries only a ping that something waits in the portal.  ✅
- **B.** Text the results if the parents sign a waiver, since a documented preference overrides channel restrictions.
- **C.** Text normal results only, holding abnormal ones for the portal, since routine values carry minimal risk.
- **D.** Send results by email instead of text, since email supports encryption and longer clinical explanations.

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. A multi-provider group wants a custom patient messaging app. Their EHR already includes a portal with secure threads and results delivery. What do you recommend?

- **A.** Extend the existing EHR portal experience, since a second inbox patients must remember leaves both unread.  ✅
- **B.** Build the custom app anyway, since EHR portals are dated and a branded experience will drive adoption.
- **C.** Run both in parallel for a quarter, then keep whichever inbox shows higher patient engagement numbers.
- **D.** Build the custom app for new patients only, keeping existing patients on the EHR portal to limit migration.

> **Answer:** A

### Q11. An urgent care sends post-visit care instructions through its portal, but staff cannot tell whether patients ever open them. What do you add to the build?

- **A.** A printed copy at checkout, since paper in hand is the one delivery method that guarantees patients get it.
- **B.** A follow-up phone call to every patient, since live conversation is the most reliable comprehension check.
- **C.** Confirmation of receipt, so the system records that instructions were seen and flags the ones that were not.  ✅
- **D.** A shorter instruction format, since patients skip long documents and brevity will raise the open numbers.

> **Answer:** C

### Q12. A dermatology practice wants review requests sent only to patients whose visit notes suggest they were happy. The manager calls it smart targeting. What do you tell them?

- **A.** Target satisfied patients but rotate the platforms, since spreading requests across sites masks the pattern.
- **B.** The plan works even if requests go out a week later, since delay breaks the visible link from visit to review.
- **C.** Ask every patient consistently, since filtering by predicted sentiment violates the rules platforms enforce.  ✅
- **D.** Use the plan only for patients who verbally agreed at checkout, since spoken consent covers the selection.

> **Answer:** C

### Q13. You are building post-visit instruction delivery for an orthopedics group. The manager asks who writes the instruction content itself. What do you answer?

- **A.** You draft it with AI and the manager approves it, since builders own every template inside the systems they ship.
- **B.** The EHR vendor supplies it, since standardized instruction libraries reduce liability compared to custom content.
- **C.** Each patient's insurer provides it, since coverage rules determine which aftercare steps are appropriate to send.
- **D.** The practice's clinical staff write the templates, while your build handles delivery, timing, and receipt.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q14. A multi-provider group proposes a ten dollar gift card for patients who leave a five star review. The manager expects a quick reputation lift. What do you advise?

- **A.** Offer the card for any review regardless of rating, since a neutral incentive keeps the program defensible.
- **B.** Lower the amount to something nominal, since small tokens of thanks fall under ordinary patient goodwill.
- **C.** Run it as a monthly raffle instead, since indirect rewards are treated differently from direct payments.
- **D.** Drop the incentive, since rewards conditioned on positive reviews break rules the review platforms enforce.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q15. A patient posts a glowing public review of an OB/GYN practice describing her delivery. The manager drafts: 'We loved caring for you and your baby!' What do you advise?

- **A.** Post the draft as written, since the patient disclosed the details herself and replies may mirror them.
- **B.** Send the draft by private message instead, since moving the reply out of public view resolves the concern.
- **C.** Reply with generic thanks and an offline contact path, since the draft confirms patient status in public.  ✅
- **D.** Ask the patient to edit her review first, since removing clinical detail makes a warm response safe to post.

> **Answer:** C

### Q16. A family medicine patient books a physical from a recall text, then receives three more overdue notices over the next month. What is missing from the sequence?

- **A.** A frequency cap, so no patient ever receives more than one recall message in any rolling thirty day window.
- **B.** A state check before each send, so the sequence exits automatically once the booking event is recorded.  ✅
- **C.** A preference center, so patients can choose which categories of messages they are willing to receive.
- **D.** A suppression list staff update by hand, so the front desk can remove patients who complain about volume.

> **Answer:** B

### Q17. During an audit of an internal medicine group's recall automation, you find nothing prevents outreach to deceased patients. What do you direct the AI to add?

- **A.** A status check against the record before every send, so no message fires for anyone who should not be contacted.  ✅
- **B.** An annual list scrub each January, so the registry starts every year clean before the sequences resume running.
- **C.** A softer message tone across all the templates, so any outreach that does slip through causes less distress.
- **D.** A manual review queue for every recall message, so staff approve each send before it ever leaves the system.

> **Answer:** A

### Q18. A pediatric practice asks how to send balance reminders. The billing lead wants the amount owed and the visit it came from included in the text. What do you specify?

- **A.** Include the amount but not the visit, since dollar figures alone reveal nothing about the care received.
- **B.** A minimal text noting a balance is due with a secure link, keeping the visit detail behind the login.  ✅
- **C.** Send the full detail by email instead, since billing information is administrative rather than clinical.
- **D.** Mail paper statements only, since financial communication is too sensitive for any electronic channel.

> **Answer:** B

### Q19. You audit an urgent care's confirmation SMS: 'Your stitches removal with our wound care team is Friday at 9, 123 Main St.' What is the compliant rewrite?

- **A.** 'Your wound care visit is Friday at 9 at 123 Main St.', since dropping the word stitches removes the PHI.
- **B.** 'Your procedure follow-up is Friday at 9. Reply C to confirm.', since generic clinical terms are permitted.
- **C.** 'Your appointment at Lakeside Urgent Care is Friday at 9, 123 Main St. Details and forms: [secure link].'  ✅
- **D.** 'You have an appointment this week. Call us for the details.', since removing every specific is safest.

> **Answer:** C

### Q20. A pediatric practice wants to recover overdue well-child visits. You design the outreach: a text on day one, then what across the following touches?

- **A.** Identical texts repeated weekly, since consistency of message and channel builds recognition and trust.
- **B.** Escalating channels over later touches, such as email then a phone call, each carrying the booking link.  ✅
- **C.** A certified letter as the second touch, since formal mail signals importance better than digital channels.
- **D.** Paid social ads retargeting the families, since advertising reaches parents where they already spend time.

> **Answer:** B

### Q21. An urgent care wants more reviews. The manager asks when requests should go out and how they should be built to actually convert. What do you specify?

- **A.** Shortly after the visit, with a direct link to the review platform, asked of every patient consistently.  ✅
- **B.** Quarterly batches to the full panel, since concentrated pushes create visible momentum on the platforms.
- **C.** Only after a second visit, since returning patients have shown satisfaction worth capturing.
- **D.** At checkout on a lobby tablet, since staff presence at the moment of the request lifts completion rates.

> **Answer:** A

### Q22. A dermatology manager says the practice already does recall: one postcard-style email each year to everyone overdue. Bookings barely move. What do you explain?

- **A.** The list is the problem, so the practice should buy verified contact data before sending the next campaign.
- **B.** The offer is the problem, so the email needs a discount or promotion to give patients a reason to rebook.
- **C.** The creative is the problem, so a redesigned email with stronger imagery will lift the booking response.
- **D.** One touch recovers almost nobody, so recall needs a sequence of escalating channels and a booking link.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q23. A multi-provider group's EHR portal has no secure messaging at all, and the vendor has no roadmap for adding it. The practice needs message threads. What do you do?

- **A.** Tell the practice to switch EHRs, since messaging is core and a vendor without it will fall behind on more.
- **B.** Route clinical conversations through standard email, since a documented gap justifies an interim channel.
- **C.** Use SMS threads with patient consent forms, since signed acknowledgment shifts channel risk downstream.
- **D.** Direct AI to build a secure destination covering the gap, tied into the portal patients already use.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q24. A family medicine practice posts lab results to its portal, but patients rarely log in, so results sit unread for weeks. What do you add to the system?

- **A.** A policy of calling every patient with results, since phone delivery is the channel patients reliably answer.
- **B.** A notification ping on the insecure channels saying something is waiting, with detail behind the login.  ✅
- **C.** A weekly digest email listing each result value, since summarizing content directly gets messages read.
- **D.** An automatic mail-out of printed results, since paper delivery removes the login barrier for patients.

> **Answer:** B

### Q25. Across every system in this module, confirmations, recalls, results, and reviews, one principle governs the design. A new builder asks you to state it. What do you say?

- **A.** Automate every touchpoint fully, since consistency of delivery matters more than the content of messages.
- **B.** Maximize patient convenience, since adoption depends on meeting patients in the channels they prefer.
- **C.** Match competitor communication volume, since patients judge practices against experiences seen elsewhere.
- **D.** Deliver warmth at scale by keeping the minimum in the message and the full detail behind authentication.  ✅

> **Answer:** D
