# Module 6: Testing and Refining Your Position — Exam

> Exam for **Module 6: Testing and Refining Your Position** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. A builder finishes choosing a position, building the offer, and writing the sentence, then declares the positioning work done. How does this module correct that?

- **A.** Almost done: one round of professional design on the website completes the work
- **B.** Done indeed: execution, not further analysis, is all that remains from here
- **C.** Nothing is proven yet: the position is a hypothesis until client behavior grades it  ✅
- **D.** Overdone: most builders succeed with less preparation and more raw outreach

> **Answer:** C

### Q2. An email arrives: I saw you build intake systems for clinics, and we need exactly that. Which signal category is this, and why does it matter?

- **A.** A working signal: the inquiry mentions the specialty, so the market can find and name him  ✅
- **B.** A neutral event: single inquiries carry no real information until a pattern forms
- **C.** A vanity signal: mentions of the specialty flatter without predicting any revenue
- **D.** A warning: prospects who quote your positioning back to you are usually shopping around widely

> **Answer:** A

### Q3. A builder's client tells a colleague: she builds review systems for home service companies, and the colleague repeats it almost word for word when calling. What just got confirmed?

- **A.** The client's memory: some contacts are simply much better at recall than others
- **B.** Referral accuracy: the position traveled intact through a third party, a working signal  ✅
- **C.** The colleague's own need: urgency, not positioning, is what really made the sentence stick
- **D.** Nothing yet: secondhand descriptions must match the website copy before counting

> **Answer:** B

### Q4. A prospect opens a call with: we run a clinic, so this is exactly what we need. The builder has explained nothing yet. Which signal is firing?

- **A.** Overconfidence: prospects who pre-decide are the likeliest to churn after launch
- **B.** A pricing setup: self-declared fit is a common posture before a discount request
- **C.** Coincidental fit: one aligned prospect says nothing about the position itself
- **D.** Self-qualification: the prospect placed themselves inside the position unprompted  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q5. Warm leads who found a builder on purpose still need the service explained from scratch on every call. As a signal, what is this?

- **A.** A sales skill gap: better call structure would remove the need to explain
- **B.** A working signal: explaining is simply what early conversations are for
- **C.** Audience noise: warm leads vary too much for explanations to mean anything
- **D.** A failing signal: the position is not landing even with people who sought him out  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q6. In three straight deals, prospects line a specialist up against generalists and Fiverr listings. What does this comparison set reveal?

- **A.** The specialty never registered: prospects shopped a category not an expert  ✅
- **B.** Healthy demand: appearing in many comparison sets means wide visibility
- **C.** Price strength: winning against cheap listings would prove premium pull
- **D.** Platform drift: Fiverr comparisons mean the niche buys through marketplaces

> **Answer:** A

### Q7. Deal after deal, the only conversation that decides anything is who costs less. In the signal framework, what is this pattern?

- **A.** A market condition: some quarters simply price-shop more than others
- **B.** A closing-technique gap: price talk expands when urgency is not created
- **C.** A failing signal: nothing but the number is differentiating a builder  ✅
- **D.** A volume artifact: more leads mechanically produce more price talk

> **Answer:** C

### Q8. After a month of failing signals, a builder spirals: I am just not good enough for this business. How does the module reframe what the signals mean?

- **A.** As confirmation to pivot niches immediately before more time is lost
- **B.** As information about a testable hypothesis, not a verdict on the builder  ✅
- **C.** As noise to ignore: feelings about signals matter more than the counts
- **D.** As a skills gap: failing signals usually trace to weak delivery work

> **Answer:** B

### Q9. A position is sharp and tested, but the builder's content reached almost nobody and outreach totals a handful of emails. Signals are quiet. What is this condition called?

- **A.** Distribution failure: the position is fine, and almost nobody has encountered it yet  ✅
- **B.** Position failure: quiet signals always indict the exact wording itself before anything else
- **C.** Market failure: the niche is simply too small to produce any measurable signals
- **D.** Timing failure: the niche's natural buying season simply has not arrived yet this year

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. Thousands of the right people clearly encountered a position, and they still respond with confusion and price-shopping. What is this condition, and what does it call for?

- **A.** Distribution failure: reach must double again before any judgment is possible
- **B.** Anomaly: confusion at scale usually reflects a tracking or attribution bug
- **C.** Success in disguise: confusion means the position is novel enough to stand out
- **D.** Position failure: the words are being seen and not working so revision is due  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q11. A builder sees failing signals and immediately starts rewriting the positioning sentence that same night. What step does the module insist comes first?

- **A.** A competitor scan, since failing signals usually mean someone else took the ground
- **B.** Diagnosis: distribution failure and position failure have opposite fixes  ✅
- **C.** A price cut, since cost is the fastest variable to test against confusion
- **D.** A cooling-off week, since decisions made on bad news nights get reverted

> **Answer:** B

### Q12. A builder commits to the 90-day test. Which set of behaviors does the commitment actually require?

- **A.** Daily posting across three platforms, with the sentence rewritten whenever engagement numbers dip
- **B.** Total silence: no changes and no promotion, so the position can be measured cleanly
- **C.** One unchanged position on every surface, weekly content and outreach, signals tracked monthly  ✅
- **D.** A paid ad campaign, since organic distribution alone cannot generate significant signals

> **Answer:** C

### Q13. Three weeks into the test, inbound is silent, and a builder drafts a message abandoning the niche. What does the module say about silence at this point?

- **A.** It is the expected value: referral loops have not had time to complete yet  ✅
- **B.** It is an early verdict: strong positions show measurable lift within two weeks
- **C.** It is ambiguous forever: silence can never be interpreted at any timeframe
- **D.** It is a distribution proof: three quiet weeks always mean weak reach

> **Answer:** A

### Q14. Day 90 arrives with failing signals despite real distribution, and a builder plans to change the audience, the problem, and the language all at once. What does the module say?

- **A.** Change one variable only, or the next test cannot attribute whatever happens  ✅
- **B.** Change all three: bundled revisions compress three test cycles into one
- **C.** Change nothing: 90 days is too short to justify touching any variable
- **D.** Change the price instead, since offer variables respond faster than words

> **Answer:** A

### Q15. It is month-end in the test, and a pile of raw inquiry notes and call summaries sits unprocessed. Per the module's cadence, what should the builder direct AI to do?

- **A.** Draft next month's content calendar, since production lapses sink more tests than analysis
- **B.** Rewrite the positioning statement using whatever language the inquiries contained
- **C.** Summarize the notes into a narrative memo for the builder's own private reflection
- **D.** Classify each item against the six signals and summarize the trend versus last month  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q16. On day one of the test, before any content ships, the module assigns one measurement task. What is it?

- **A.** Interviewing five past clients about the reasons they originally decided to buy
- **B.** Logging the baseline: current inbound rate, referral accuracy, and price objections  ✅
- **C.** Estimating the niche's total addressable market for comparison at the end of the test
- **D.** Screenshotting competitor websites to track how their positioning drifts over time

> **Answer:** B

### Q17. A builder locks the sentence on every surface, then spends the test doing no outreach and publishing nothing. Day 90 shows silence. What did the builder actually test?

- **A.** The position's pure organic pull, which is the cleanest reading available to any builder
- **B.** The niche's patience, which total silence after 90 quiet days measures fairly well
- **C.** Nothing at all: no one ever saw the sentence, so the market issued no grade either way  ✅
- **D.** The surfaces themselves, which clearly failed to convert the visitors they received

> **Answer:** C

### Q18. On day 90, a builder remembers two amazing calls vividly and wants to commit based on them, though the tracker shows flat counts. How does the module rule?

- **A.** Commit: two strong calls from the right niche outweigh a flat spreadsheet
- **B.** Split the difference: extend the test two weeks and recount the calls
- **C.** Quit: flat counts at day 90 end the test regardless of any bright spots
- **D.** Read the trend, not the anecdotes: the counts decide, not the memories  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q19. The day-90 review shows inbound up, referrals accurate, and price objections halved. What does the module prescribe as the response to this trend?

- **A.** Commit harder and consider raising prices: the position has earned its next test  ✅
- **B.** Bank the win and immediately broaden the target audience to capture more of the market
- **C.** Freeze everything: changing anything after a good quarter risks the streak
- **D.** Start a parallel position in a second niche while the first one quietly cruises

> **Answer:** A

### Q20. Day 90 shows failing signals, but the tracker also shows content reached a few dozen people and outreach barely happened. What is the prescribed move?

- **A.** Revise the position anyway: 90 days is the agreed deadline regardless of context
- **B.** Extend 30 to 60 days with real distribution: the position was never fairly tested  ✅
- **C.** Abandon the niche: weak distribution is itself evidence of low motivation to serve it
- **D.** Switch to paid channels exclusively and restart the whole clock from zero again

> **Answer:** B

### Q21. Day 90 shows failing signals despite strong, verified distribution to the right audience. What is the prescribed move now?

- **A.** Extend another 90 days: some positions simply mature on a slower curve
- **B.** Drop positioning work and compete on responsiveness and price instead
- **C.** Revise one variable, audience or problem or language, and restart the 90-day clock  ✅
- **D.** Keep the position but rebuild the website, since presentation must be the gap

> **Answer:** C

### Q22. One glowing conversation convinces a builder the position works, while ten price-shopping calls sit ignored in the tracker. What bias is the module warning against?

- **A.** Recency bias: the glowing call simply happened closest to the day of the review
- **B.** Anecdote bias: a vivid data point outweighing ten quiet ones in the wrong direction  ✅
- **C.** Confirmation lag: good news registers much more slowly than bad news in most trackers
- **D.** Sampling error: eleven total conversations are far too few for any conclusion at all

> **Answer:** B

### Q23. A landscaping-niche builder wants to quit at week four: two inquiries, both price-shoppers. The log shows nine outreach emails total and content that reached almost no landscapers. What is the diagnosis?

- **A.** Distribution failure: the position was barely encountered, so the fix is reach, not rewriting  ✅
- **B.** Position failure: these price-shopping inquiries prove the wording attracts only the wrong buyers
- **C.** Niche failure: landscapers demonstrably do not buy custom software systems from builders
- **D.** Offer failure: the price-shopping pattern points directly at the number, not the niche

> **Answer:** A

### Q24. A builder delays the 90-day test until they can afford an analytics suite with dashboards. What does the module say the test actually requires?

- **A.** The suite matters: signal classification at any real scale needs proper tooling behind it
- **B.** A CRM at minimum, since spreadsheets lose track of inquiries past a certain volume
- **C.** Nothing at all: sufficiently strong builders can track all six signals from memory alone
- **D.** A simple spreadsheet and the discipline of monthly review: the habit, not the dashboard  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q25. One principle governs this module, from the signal lists to the 90-day clock. Which statement expresses it?

- **A.** Positioning succeeds through persistence: hold every position long enough and it eventually lands
- **B.** Positioning is intuition: builders who need trackers never had the instinct to win
- **C.** The market grades your position through behavior, and the job is to read that grade honestly  ✅
- **D.** Positioning is presentation: signals respond to design quality far more than to words

> **Answer:** C
