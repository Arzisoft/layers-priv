# Module 3: Solution Validation — Exam

> Exam for **Module 3: Solution Validation** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. You are prompting a CRM for dentists. Your assumptions: the AI can build it, dentists struggle with follow-ups, and office managers will change their workflow for a new tool. Which do you test first?

- **A.** The assumption that your AI can build the CRM, since without a working product there is nothing to put in front of anyone
- **B.** The assumption that dentists struggle with follow-ups, since problems must always be fully re-verified before every single new test
- **C.** The assumption that you can price the tool competitively, since revenue potential shapes every later build decision
- **D.** The assumption that office managers will change their workflow, because if that one fails nothing else about the idea matters  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q2. A builder trims her AI-built budgeting app from twelve features down to five and proudly calls it her MVP. According to this module, what does minimum viable actually mean?

- **A.** A smaller version of the full product that keeps the core features and cuts everything that is merely nice to have for launch
- **B.** The smallest thing you can put in front of the market that tests the riskiest assumption behind your specific idea  ✅
- **C.** The cheapest version of the product your AI can generate quickly so you can start collecting real user feedback
- **D.** A polished prototype with just enough design quality that early users take the unfinished product seriously

> **Answer:** B

### Q3. You direct your AI to build a single page describing your meal-planning tool as if it already exists, with one signup button. In this landing page test, what is your core demand signal?

- **A.** The total time visitors spend reading the page, since sustained attention reveals genuine interest in the concept
- **B.** The number of social shares the page collects, since word of mouth is the strongest early indicator of demand
- **C.** The percentage of visitors who click the call to action or sign up, measured against thresholds set in advance  ✅
- **D.** The qualitative feedback visitors leave behind, since real language shows exactly what people actually want

> **Answer:** C

### Q4. Your waitlist for an AI-built invoicing tool hits 800 emails in two weeks. A friend says you have proven demand. How should this module tell you to treat those signups?

- **A.** As strong proof of demand, since 800 people voluntarily handed over their contact information for the unbuilt product
- **B.** As a directional signal only, since emails are free to give and a notify-me signup is a weak form of commitment  ✅
- **C.** As a reason to skip pre-sales entirely, since a list of that size will surely convert once the product ships
- **D.** As market-sizing data, since waitlist volume mainly helps you estimate the total addressable market later on

> **Answer:** B

### Q5. Ten dental offices each put down a fifty dollar deposit for early access to a follow-up tool you have not built yet. Why does the module treat this as the gold standard of validation?

- **A.** Because deposits create social proof you can show other prospects, which makes your later marketing much easier
- **B.** Because early buyers become committed beta testers, giving you feedback before your AI builds anything real
- **C.** Because the deposits fund your build, letting you develop the product without risking your own savings at all
- **D.** Because money is the one signal that cannot lie to you, unlike clicks, signups, or verbal enthusiasm from fans  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q6. Before prompting an automated follow-up tool into existence, you personally send patient follow-ups for three dental offices for two weeks. What makes this concierge MVP so valuable?

- **A.** It proves the service can scale, since handling three offices by hand shows the automated version will hold up
- **B.** It generates early revenue that quietly funds development while your AI builds the automated version behind it
- **C.** You learn exactly what the product must do and whether customers value the outcome before any feature exists  ✅
- **D.** It creates a public case study that lends your landing page credibility when you launch the real product

> **Answer:** C

### Q7. A builder launches what looks like an AI resume-review app, but she secretly writes every review herself overnight. Customers experience a real-feeling product. What test is this, and what does it reveal?

- **A.** A concierge MVP, revealing whether she can deliver the outcome by hand before automating the entire workflow
- **B.** A fake door test, revealing how many people will click on a promised feature that does not actually exist yet
- **C.** A smoke test, revealing whether the core promise of the product attracts traffic from her audience
- **D.** A wizard-of-oz test, revealing whether people use and value the experience before the engine is ever built  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q8. Your AI-built bookkeeping app shows an Export to QuickBooks button that leads to a page saying coming soon, want early access? What is this tactic, and what does it measure?

- **A.** A wizard-of-oz test measuring whether users tolerate manual work hidden behind an automated-looking interface
- **B.** A concierge MVP measuring whether you can deliver QuickBooks exports by hand for your very first customers
- **C.** A fake door test measuring real demand for that specific feature through the clicks it actually collects  ✅
- **D.** A waitlist tactic measuring the future size of your market for accounting integrations at scale

> **Answer:** C

### Q9. You run a fake door test and 60 users click the nonexistent feature. Some feel misled when they hit the coming soon page. What does the module say you must do?

- **A.** Quietly remove the button and rebuild trust later, since acknowledging the test would embarrass your brand publicly
- **B.** Tell people the truth after they click and close the loop honestly, since signal is the goal, not deception  ✅
- **C.** Offer every clicker a discount code as compensation, since goodwill gestures matter more than explanations
- **D.** Ship the feature immediately, since demand is now proven and delivering fast is the only honest response

> **Answer:** B

### Q10. Before launching a landing page test for a fitness app, a builder writes: 100 visitors, 15 signups, 3 pre-orders, or I do not build. Why does the module require this before the test runs?

- **A.** Setting pass and kill thresholds in advance stops you from rationalizing weak results after you see them  ✅
- **B.** Written targets let you report progress to mentors and keep your accountability partners fully informed
- **C.** Advance numbers help you calculate exactly how much ad spend will reach statistical significance
- **D.** Public commitments create urgency for your audience, which improves conversion rates during the testing window

> **Answer:** A

### Q11. A builder gets 3 signups from 200 visitors, well below his written threshold, and says: not bad for a first try, I will build anyway. Which pitfall from this module is he committing?

- **A.** Testing the easy assumption instead of the risky one, since visitor traffic was never the real question here
- **B.** Treating waitlist signups as proof of demand, since three emails cannot possibly represent a real market
- **C.** Moving the goalposts after the test, talking himself past a kill signal and into months of wasted building  ✅
- **D.** Skipping the concierge phase, since he should have delivered the service by hand before testing anything

> **Answer:** C

### Q12. A vibecoder spends six weeks prompting a full scheduling platform with logins, billing, and dashboards, then calls it his MVP test. What is the module's verdict on this approach?

- **A.** It is a valid test as long as he sets pass and kill thresholds before showing the platform to its first users
- **B.** It is a smart move, since AI makes building cheap enough that shipping the full product is the fastest test
- **C.** It is acceptable if the platform stays private, since unreleased software carries no real validation risk
- **D.** It was not a minimum test at all, it was the product, and the demand question remains completely unanswered  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q13. A builder proudly demos that her AI can generate a working legal-document tool, and calls the idea validated. What mistake does the module warn about here?

- **A.** She proved the easy assumption that she can build it, when the risky one is whether anyone will use and pay  ✅
- **B.** She skipped the waitlist stage, which the module requires before any working software gets demonstrated
- **C.** She showed the demo too early, since polished products should only appear once market sizing is complete
- **D.** She tested with the wrong audience, since lawyers rarely respond honestly to early-stage product demonstrations

> **Answer:** A

### Q14. Two builders test the same pet-sitting app idea. One collects 500 waitlist emails, the other gets 12 paid pre-orders at 25 dollars. Whose evidence is stronger, and why?

- **A.** The waitlist builder, since 500 data points beat 12 and larger samples always produce more reliable signals
- **B.** The pre-order builder, since payment is a far stronger commitment than a free notify-me email signup  ✅
- **C.** They are roughly equal, since both tests measure interest and differ only in the size of their audiences
- **D.** Neither is meaningful yet, since real validation only arrives after the product launches to the public

> **Answer:** B

### Q15. You want to learn what a bookkeeping product must do by doing the work openly by hand for five clients, with no software interface at all. Which test fits, and how does it differ from wizard-of-oz?

- **A.** A concierge MVP, where the manual work is visible to the customer instead of hidden behind a product-like front  ✅
- **B.** A wizard-of-oz test, where you openly tell every customer that a human is doing all of the work behind the scenes for now
- **C.** A fake door test, where the bookkeeping offer is a button that measures who reaches for the stated promise
- **D.** A smoke test, where you advertise the service broadly and measure which client segments respond the most

> **Answer:** A

### Q16. You have four assumptions behind a tutoring marketplace idea and cannot decide what to test first. Which toolkit item does the module prescribe, and how does it work?

- **A.** The Assumption Map, a one-page list ranking each assumption by how likely it is wrong and how fatal it would be  ✅
- **B.** The Signal Threshold Sheet, ranking your assumptions by how cheaply each one can be tested within a week
- **C.** The Pre-Sale Offer Script, since asking for money immediately reveals which assumption matters the most
- **D.** The One-Page Landing Test, since a live page surfaces every assumption at once through real visitor behavior and clicks

> **Answer:** A

### Q17. In this module, a builder wants to know what she is allowed to direct her AI to build while validating a language-learning idea. What does the module permit, and what must it include?

- **A.** A working prototype of the core feature, plus a built-in feedback form so early users can help shape the roadmap
- **B.** A private beta with up to ten users, including analytics and a pre-sale checkout wired into the product
- **C.** Nothing at all, since building anything before pre-orders arrive violates the core validation mindset
- **D.** Only the one-page landing test: headline, problem, promised solution, one call to action, analytics wired in  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q18. You are drafting a pre-sale pitch for an unbuilt course-hosting tool. According to the module's Pre-Sale Offer Script, what belongs in the offer?

- **A.** What you are building, when it ships, what early buyers get, and a refund promise, all delivered honestly  ✅
- **B.** A countdown timer and limited seats, since scarcity is the main driver that converts interest into payment
- **C.** A detailed feature roadmap and technical architecture, since serious buyers expect complete transparency
- **D.** Testimonials from beta users, since social proof is required before anyone pays for unbuilt software

> **Answer:** A

### Q19. A builder about to launch a smoke test wants to guarantee she will read the results honestly no matter what happens. Which toolkit item is designed for exactly this?

- **A.** The Assumption Map, which forces her to list every way the test could mislead her before it goes live
- **B.** The AI Audit Prompt, which has her model interpret the raw numbers afterward without any human bias
- **C.** The Signal Threshold Sheet, a written commitment made before the test of what counts as pass, kill, or iterate  ✅
- **D.** The Pre-Sale Offer Script, which converts every ambiguous signup into an unambiguous paid yes or a revealing no

> **Answer:** C

### Q20. Your pre-sale for a niche podcast-editing tool gets zero buyers from 300 targeted visitors. You feel like you failed. How does the module frame this outcome?

- **A.** As a marketing failure, since a zero result almost always means the pitch was weak rather than the underlying idea
- **B.** As a win, since the market said no before you spent months building, saving your time, energy, and credibility  ✅
- **C.** As inconclusive, since pre-sales for niche tools need thousands of visitors before the results mean anything
- **D.** As a pivot signal, since zero buyers means you should immediately rebuild the offer for a broader market

> **Answer:** B

### Q21. A builder refuses to run a concierge phase for his meal-prep service because manually building plans for ten clients obviously cannot scale. What is the module's response?

- **A.** He is right, since validation tests that cannot scale produce signals that vanish the moment real growth begins
- **B.** He should automate immediately, since AI tooling makes the manual phase an unnecessary and outdated ritual from a slower era
- **C.** He should run a waitlist instead, since list growth is the scalable equivalent of concierge-style learning
- **D.** He is missing the point, since concierge is not supposed to scale, it is supposed to teach him what to build  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q22. A builder wants to test whether commuters will trust an app to auto-book their train tickets, without building the booking engine. Users should feel like the product is real. Which test fits best?

- **A.** A landing page test, since a simple signup page is the cheapest way to measure trust in automated booking
- **B.** A pre-sale, since asking commuters for money upfront is the only way to test trust in an unbuilt product
- **C.** A wizard-of-oz test, where she books tickets manually behind an interface that feels fully automated  ✅
- **D.** A concierge MVP, where she openly emails commuters each morning offering to buy their tickets for them

> **Answer:** C

### Q23. A builder has three signals for her AI journaling app: 40 enthusiastic interview quotes, 900 waitlist emails, and 7 paid pre-orders. Which signal should she weight most, per the module?

- **A.** The 7 pre-orders, since payment is the strongest signal and clicks, signups, and enthusiasm can all mislead  ✅
- **B.** The 900 waitlist emails, since sample size ultimately determines how much confidence any demand signal deserves
- **C.** The 40 interview quotes, since qualitative depth explains motivations that raw numbers can never capture
- **D.** All three equally, since honest validation means triangulating every signal without playing any favorites

> **Answer:** A

### Q24. A month after a fake door test on his fitness app, a builder still has not contacted the 85 people who clicked for early access. What does the module warn happens here?

- **A.** Nothing serious, since clickers rarely remember a coming soon page and the demand signal was already captured
- **B.** He burns trust with the exact people who wanted his product most, since he never closed the loop honestly  ✅
- **C.** He loses legal standing, since collecting clicks without follow-up violates consumer protection rules
- **D.** He contaminates his data, since stale clickers convert at lower rates and will skew his future testing

> **Answer:** B

### Q25. Which statement best captures the governing principle of Module 3, Solution Validation?

- **A.** A real problem does not prove your solution, so run the smallest test of your riskiest assumption before you build  ✅
- **B.** Building fast with AI removes most validation risk, so ship the complete product quickly and let real usage decide the verdict
- **C.** Validation means collecting as many positive signals as possible before committing to a single build direction
- **D.** The best solutions come from deep customer empathy, so extended interviews should replace live market testing

> **Answer:** A
