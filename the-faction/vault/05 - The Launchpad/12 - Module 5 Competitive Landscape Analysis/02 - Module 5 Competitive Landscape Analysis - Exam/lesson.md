# Module 5: Competitive Landscape Analysis — Exam

> Exam for **Module 5: Competitive Landscape Analysis** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. You direct your AI to map competitors for your invoicing tool for mobile dog groomers and it finds zero direct rivals. You are tempted to celebrate the wide open market. What does the module say this usually means?

- **A.** You have first mover advantage, so you should build fast and lock in the market before rivals ever notice the space.
- **B.** The market is untapped, so you can safely skip customer interviews and go straight to building your first version with AI.
- **C.** It is usually a warning sign that no market exists, because nobody is currently paying to have this problem solved at all.  ✅
- **D.** Your AI simply searched badly, so you should rerun the analysis with broader queries until real competitors finally appear.

> **Answer:** C

### Q2. You pitch your idea to a fellow Faction builder, search the market together, and find six funded companies already doing it. Your heart sinks and you consider quitting. How does the module frame this discovery?

- **A.** Six competitors means the window has closed, since markets only reward the first two or three entrants into any category.
- **B.** Those six companies are free market research proving people pay for this, so your job shifts to finding underserved gaps.  ✅
- **C.** You should build the same product cheaper, since undercutting incumbent pricing is the fastest wedge into a proven market.
- **D.** You should merge the best features of all six into one superior tool so nobody has any reason to pick a rival product.

> **Answer:** B

### Q3. Mapping the landscape for your dentist CRM, your AI lists Salesforce, generic scheduling apps, and paper appointment books. You want to sort these into the module's three competitor tiers. Which set is correct?

- **A.** Enterprise rivals, small business rivals, and free tools, sorted by the size of the company selling each competing product.
- **B.** Direct competitors, indirect competitors, and the status quo, meaning how people cope today with no real product at all.  ✅
- **C.** Funded startups, bootstrapped tools, and open source projects, ranked by the resources each one has available to outbuild you.
- **D.** Market leaders, mid market challengers, and niche players, arranged by each one's share of total industry revenue.

> **Answer:** B

### Q4. Your AI maps rivals for your dentist CRM and you start planning a feature war against Salesforce and HubSpot. Per the module, which competitor is almost always the biggest one you actually face?

- **A.** The market leader, because its brand recognition means every prospect compares your new product against it before anything else.
- **B.** The cheapest rival, because price sensitive small practices will nearly always default to the lowest cost option they can find.
- **C.** The newest funded startup, because it can outspend you on advertising and ship features faster than a solo builder can.
- **D.** The status quo, because the dentist is really leaving a paper appointment book and a receptionist's memory, not Salesforce.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q5. Reviewing your competitor map, you notice the leading tool is built for enterprise clinics while small practices get an afterthought tier. Which of the module's four gap types does this represent?

- **A.** An underserved segment gap, where the incumbent serves enterprises well but treats your smaller target segment generically.  ✅
- **B.** A pricing gap, because enterprise tools always cost more than any small practice could reasonably afford to pay every single month.
- **C.** A UX gap, since enterprise software is by definition harder to use than tools designed for smaller teams and local offices.
- **D.** A missing integration gap, because enterprise platforms rarely connect to the everyday tools that small practices rely on.

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. Mining reviews for a rival booking tool, you find paying customers calling it powerful but miserable to use, with a confusing setup that takes weeks. How does the module classify this opening?

- **A.** A poor UX gap, one of the four predictable places gaps hide, and a genuine opening you could position your own build against.  ✅
- **B.** A saturation signal, since customers who complain about usability are usually locked in and unwilling to ever switch to new tools.
- **C.** Noise you should ignore, because unhappy reviewers are a vocal minority and the silent majority is satisfied enough to pay.
- **D.** A feature gap, meaning the fastest response is matching the incumbent's full feature list with a cleaner interface on top.

> **Answer:** A

### Q7. A physical therapist tells you she skipped a popular clinic tool because it never talks to the billing software her office lives in every day. Which gap type from the module did that incumbent leave open?

- **A.** A pricing gap, since the real objection behind most integration complaints is that connecting separate systems costs too much.
- **B.** An underserved segment gap, because physical therapists are clearly a niche that the incumbent never intended to serve at all.
- **C.** A missing integrations gap, where the tool fails to connect with the software the customer already depends on every day.  ✅
- **D.** A UX gap, because a clunky import and export workflow is fundamentally a design problem rather than a technical one.

> **Answer:** C

### Q8. Your AI finds the dominant tool in your space charges 300 dollars per seat monthly with annual contracts, locking out the solo operators you want to serve. Which gap type is this, per the module?

- **A.** A segment gap only, since price is just a symptom and the module says to log this under underserved segments instead.
- **B.** A UX gap, because complicated pricing pages are part of the overall user experience that customers judge tools by.
- **C.** No gap at all, since incumbent pricing reflects what the market will bear and undercutting it is always a losing move.
- **D.** A pricing gap, where the product is overpriced or wrongly structured for your segment, locking out a whole audience.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q9. You ask your AI to draft positioning for your dentist CRM against the big incumbents. Per the module, what should a strong positioning statement name?

- **A.** Your full feature list, your launch date, and a price at least 30 percent below the cheapest incumbent tier available.
- **B.** Your mission, your founder story, and the emotional transformation customers feel after switching to your product.
- **C.** Your target segment, the gap you close, and the reason the incumbent cannot or will not close that gap themselves.  ✅
- **D.** Your product category, your top three competitors, and the venture funding you would need in order to outbuild them.

> **Answer:** C

### Q10. A builder in your circle wants to take on HubSpot by shipping an easier CRM with fewer features at a lower price. Based on the module, what is the stronger way to beat an incumbent?

- **A.** Be specifically right for a segment they treat generically, like a dentist CRM with built in insurance verification.  ✅
- **B.** Match the incumbent's feature list first, because prospects compare checkbox grids before anything else.
- **C.** Wait for the incumbent to stumble publicly, then move fast to capture customers who churn during the public backlash.
- **D.** Outspend them on ads in one small region, establish a local monopoly there, and expand outward one territory at a time.

> **Answer:** A

### Q11. Your AI runs a saturation check on the crowded meal planning space and you must judge its verdict. Which pattern would tell you the competition validates the market rather than closes it?

- **A.** Products are mature and well loved, differentiation has collapsed to price, and switching costs hold customers firmly in place.
- **B.** Competitors are growing, customers still complain loudly in reviews, and specific segments remain visibly underserved today.  ✅
- **C.** The market leader was recently acquired, which proves the big players believe the category has already peaked in total value.
- **D.** Every rival offers a free tier, showing the market clearly has room for one more free product to grab attention quickly.

> **Answer:** B

### Q12. Scanning the note taking app market, your AI finds beloved mature products, rivals competing mostly on price, and users locked in by years of stored data. What does the module call this pattern?

- **A.** A validation signal, because so many surviving products prove customers will always pay for another note taking tool.
- **B.** An underserved segment gap, since locked in users are by definition being served poorly by their current everyday tools.
- **C.** Saturation, where mature loved products, price only differentiation, and high switching costs argue against entering.  ✅
- **D.** A timing problem, meaning you should wait two quarters and then rerun the analysis before making any real decision.

> **Answer:** C

### Q13. You want to turn rival products into a feature roadmap for your build. Per the module's review mining toolkit, where should you dig first?

- **A.** One to three star reviews on G2, Capterra, app stores, and Reddit, seeking repeated complaint patterns from paying users.  ✅
- **B.** Five star reviews, because they reveal exactly which features delight customers enough to copy into your own product.
- **C.** The incumbents' press releases and changelogs, since those show where each company is investing its own product roadmap next.
- **D.** Influencer video reviews, because they reach the widest audience and shape how the market perceives every rival tool.

> **Answer:** A

### Q14. You are two weeks into a 40 tab competitor spreadsheet for your pet sitter booking idea and still adding rows. What does the module say about how to scope this research?

- **A.** Cap it at five to seven competitors, go deep on the top three, and finish in days, since research past decision is hiding.  ✅
- **B.** Keep expanding the map until you have covered every rival, since one missed competitor can invalidate the whole analysis you built.
- **C.** Hand the spreadsheet to your AI to maintain permanently, so the map stays current while you focus on building features.
- **D.** Pause research and launch immediately, because any time spent studying competitors is time stolen from shipping product.

> **Answer:** A

### Q15. A fellow builder proudly shows you a three week old competitive spreadsheet with 40 tabs and no customer conversations booked. Which pitfall from the module are they living out?

- **A.** Feature list comparison, because giant spreadsheets always devolve into checkbox grids instead of real outcome analysis.
- **B.** The no competitors celebration, since they are using research volume to convince themselves that the market sits empty.
- **C.** Analysis paralysis, where research past the point of decision becomes procrastination in a productivity costume.  ✅
- **D.** Copying incumbent pricing blind, since long spreadsheets push builders to copy the market leader's rates.

> **Answer:** C

### Q16. Your AI produces a giant checkbox grid showing every rival has more features than your planned first version, and you panic. How does the module tell you to read this?

- **A.** Incumbents will always have more features than you; segments and gaps win, so compare customer outcomes, not checkboxes.  ✅
- **B.** Feature parity is the entry ticket, so delay your launch until your AI has built the top twenty features on the grid.
- **C.** The grid proves saturation, so the responsible move is to kill the idea and restart the validation process on something brand new.
- **D.** Pick the three most common features, build only those, and price at half the market average to compensate your buyers.

> **Answer:** A

### Q17. Setting prices for your solo operator CRM, you consider simply copying the market leader's tiers since they clearly work. What is the module's warning about this move?

- **A.** Copying is safe only if you also copy their annual contract structure, since pricing and terms must always travel together.
- **B.** You should instead price at exactly half the incumbent's rate, because a deep discount is the proven wedge for market entry.
- **C.** Copying is fine for the launch quarter, but you must revisit pricing once you land your first hundred paying customers.
- **D.** Their pricing reflects their costs, segment, and sales motion, not yours, so adopting it blindly imports their strategy.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q18. Your AI delivers a tidy competitive report with a promising gap for your bookkeeping tool, and you are ready to start building tomorrow. What does the module say must happen before you trust that gap?

- **A.** Rerun the same prompt in two other AI models and only proceed when all three reports agree completely on the exact identical gap.
- **B.** Ask the AI to cite at least ten sources per claim, because a well cited report can be trusted as validated research.
- **C.** Publish the report to your audience and let comments and reactions confirm whether the gap resonates with the market.
- **D.** Confirm the gap through real customer conversations, since the AI maps the landscape but only customers prove it is real.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q19. You are adapting the module's audit prompt to have your AI analyze rivals to your tutoring marketplace idea. Why does the prompt end by demanding the three strongest reasons AGAINST building?

- **A.** It gives you rebuttals to memorize for investor meetings, where objections about competition always come up before anything.
- **B.** It pads the report to a professional length, since short competitive analyses tend to look lazy to stakeholders.
- **C.** It forces an honest risk assessment instead of flattery, letting the landscape argue against the idea without softening.  ✅
- **D.** It tricks the AI into revealing rival weaknesses, because criticism prompts surface details that praise prompts tend to hide.

> **Answer:** C

### Q20. You are setting up the module's Competitor Map before directing your AI to fill it in for your home bakery order tool. Which columns and scope does the toolkit prescribe?

- **A.** Name, funding raised, employee count, and tech stack for every rival you can find, updated monthly as the market shifts.
- **B.** Name, tier, target segment, pricing, and standout complaint, capped at five to seven rows as your working document.  ✅
- **C.** Name, feature count, star rating, and launch year for the top twenty tools, ranked by their total download volume.
- **D.** Name, CEO, marketing channels, and estimated ad spend for the top ten rivals, refreshed weekly throughout validation.

### Q21. You score each rival one to five on segment fit, UX, integrations, and pricing using the Gap Scorecard, and every competitor lands fours and fives across the board. What should you conclude?

- **A.** Take the saturation signal seriously, because uniformly high scores mean incumbents leave few genuine openings to attack.  ✅
- **B.** Your scoring is too generous, so rescore with harsher criteria until at least one exploitable gap finally emerges for you.
- **C.** The market is validated and safe to enter, since strong products everywhere prove customers pay well in this category.
- **D.** You should compete on brand personality instead, because when scores tie, marketing polish decides who wins customers.

> **Answer:** A

### Q22. A builder sees Salesforce and HubSpot dominating CRM and abandons her dentist CRM idea on the spot. According to the module, what did she miss?

- **A.** That both giants are vulnerable to a price war, since a leaner AI built tool could undercut their per seat rates by half or more.
- **B.** That neither incumbent handles insurance verification workflows or recall scheduling the way a dental office actually works.  ✅
- **C.** That CRM is a saturated category, so the correct lesson is to leave dental software entirely and find an emptier market.
- **D.** That she could partner with either giant instead, reselling their platform to dentists with a thin customization layer.

> **Answer:** B

### Q23. Your AI counts fourteen logos in your niche and you nearly declare the market saturated on that number alone. Per the module, what should you actually read to judge saturation?

- **A.** Total funding raised across the category, because heavy investment nearly always means the space is already overcrowded.
- **B.** The age of the oldest incumbent, since categories older than a decade rarely reward brand new entrants of any kind.
- **C.** App store download rankings, because raw distribution numbers show whether attention in the niche is fully exhausted.
- **D.** Review velocity and complaint patterns, not the number of logos, since those reveal growth and unmet demand directly.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q24. Your competitor map for a field crew scheduling tool shows most prospects run on spreadsheets, group texts, and memory rather than rival software. How should this shape your positioning, per the module?

- **A.** Ignore those prospects entirely, because people who have not bought software yet are unqualified leads for a new product.
- **B.** Position against the biggest software rival anyway, since winning that comparison also wins over the spreadsheet crowd.
- **C.** Lead with your total feature count, because prospects using spreadsheets need to see everything they are currently missing.
- **D.** Position against the status quo itself, because if you cannot beat do nothing and inertia, the feature war is irrelevant.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q25. What is the governing principle of Module 5, Competitive Landscape Analysis?

- **A.** Enter markets with the fewest competitors, because empty landscapes give a solo builder the longest runway for growth.
- **B.** Treat competitors as data rather than a verdict; they prove people pay, and their gaps show where you should build.  ✅
- **C.** Out feature the incumbents quickly, because AI assisted building lets a solo founder match any roadmap in weeks.
- **D.** Avoid crowded categories entirely, because saturation is the default state of any market that is worth entering today.

> **Answer:** B
