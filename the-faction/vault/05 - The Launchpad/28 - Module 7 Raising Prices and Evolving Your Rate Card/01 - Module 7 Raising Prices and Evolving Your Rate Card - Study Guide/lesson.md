# Pricing Your Builds

### T7 The Launchpad | Module 7 Study Guide

## Module 7: Raising Prices and Evolving Your Rate Card

> Direct AI to keep your rate card honest about who you have become, because your prices should grow at the same pace as your capability.

## Why This Matters

The price you set a year ago described a builder who no longer exists. Skills compound, delivery speeds up, proof accumulates, and rates that stand still quietly become a discount. Raising prices is not a risky event; it is scheduled maintenance on a business that is improving.

## Core Concepts

**Raise signals, not calendars alone.** The clearest triggers: you are booked past comfortable capacity, prospects accept quotes without hesitation, your proof library has visibly outgrown your rates, and referrals arrive pre-sold. Any two of these means the market is already paying your next price; your card just has not caught up.

**Demand is the honest meter.** Consistently full pipeline at the current rate is the market voting for an increase. Price is a valve on demand: raising it trades volume you cannot serve for margin you can keep.

**New clients first, existing clients second.** The lowest-risk raise sequence: new prospects get the new rate immediately, since they have no anchor to your old one. Existing clients follow later, with notice, once the new rate has proven itself in the market.

**The increase conversation is notice, not negotiation.** Existing clients get honesty and runway: the new rate, when it takes effect, generous notice, and appreciation. No apology spiral, no justification wall; delivered like scheduled maintenance, it reads like scheduled maintenance.

**Grandfathering is a strategic gift with a shape.** Keeping loyal clients at legacy rates can be right, but design it: a defined period, a stated gap to the new rate, and an explicit endpoint or migration path. Open-ended grandfathering becomes a shadow rate card that grows more expensive to honor every year.

**Repositioning resets the anchor entirely.** Incremental raises move a number; repositioning changes what the number is attached to: a sharper niche, outcome framing, productized tiers from Module 4, proof-led selling. When your rate needs to jump rather than creep, reposition, then price the new position.

**The rate card is a living document.** Rates by engagement type, package tiers, minimums, and rush terms, reviewed on a schedule, with each change logged. It grows with you; the review cadence, twice a year for most builders, is what keeps drift from accumulating.

## How It Works

Running a price increase properly:

1. Confirm the signals: capacity, acceptance rate, proof growth. Direct AI to summarize your last two quarters of quotes and outcomes.
1. Set the new rate with Module 2's lenses, not a guess: value delivered has grown; reprice against it.
1. Roll to new business first; watch acceptance for a full cycle.
1. Notify existing clients with runway: effective date one or two projects out, or 60 to 90 days for retainers.
1. Decide grandfathering deliberately per client: who gets legacy pricing, at what gap, until when.
1. Log everything in the rate card; schedule the next review before this one closes.

## Directing AI

1. "Analyze my last two quarters: quote acceptance rate, time-to-yes, and capacity. Tell me whether the raise signals are present."
1. "Draft the rate increase notice for my retainer clients: new rate, 90 days runway, appreciation, no apology, no justification wall."
1. "Model grandfathering options for these four clients: legacy rate periods, gaps to the new card, and the cost of each option over a year."
1. "My rates need to jump 60 percent to match my current proof. Design the repositioning that makes that number credible, not just announced."
1. "Build my rate card as a living document: engagement types, tiers, minimums, rush terms, and a changelog; set the next review date."

## Common Mistakes

1. **Waiting for permission.** No client will ever suggest you raise your rates; the signal comes from demand, not from kindness.
1. **Raising everyone at once.** Springing a new rate on existing clients with no notice converts loyalty into resentment overnight.
1. **The apology spiral.** Announcing an increase wrapped in three paragraphs of guilt invites negotiation on all of it.
1. **Open-ended grandfathering.** Legacy rates without endpoints compound into serving your oldest clients at your newest costs.
1. **Big jumps with no repositioning.** A 60 percent raise on an unchanged offer reads as arbitrary; the same raise on a repositioned offer reads as the market rate.
1. **A frozen rate card.** No review cadence means your pricing describes the builder you were, indefinitely.

## Real-World Application

A builder's pipeline has been full for five months, quotes close within days, and their portfolio now includes three named case studies with hard numbers. Rates have not moved in 18 months. Directed by the playbook, they raise new-business rates 35 percent; acceptance does not drop. Sixty days later, retainer clients receive notice: new rate in 90 days, thanks for the partnership, no apologies. Two of three accept without comment. The third, a legacy client on thin margins, gets a designed option: 12 more months at a 15 percent gap to the new card, then migration. One prospect balks at the new rate; the free capacity absorbs a better-fit client within three weeks. Annualized, the raise adds five figures of revenue with zero additional delivery hours.

## Decision Framework

- **Booked solid and quotes closing instantly?** The market already voted; raise new-business rates now.
- **Proof library outgrown the rates?** Reprice against current value, not founding-era value.
- **Existing clients on old rates?** Notice with runway, delivered as maintenance, not negotiation.
- **Loyal client who cannot absorb the jump?** Designed grandfathering: defined gap, defined endpoint.
- **Need a jump, not a creep?** Reposition first; price the new position second.
- **Rate card older than six months?** Review it; log what changed and schedule the next pass.

## Tool and Platform Notes

Direct AI to run the quarterly numbers behind raise decisions: acceptance rates, time-to-close, capacity utilization, revenue per delivery hour. The rate card lives wherever you work daily, a Notion page or doc, with a changelog; treat it like versioned infrastructure. Update your pricing pages from Module 4 the same day the card changes; drift between the card and the public pages recreates the document-drift problem from Module 5. Revenue changes have tax consequences; the accountant, not this course, owns that conversation.

## Key Takeaways

- Raise on signals: full capacity, instant acceptance, outgrown proof; any two means you are late.
- New clients get the new rate first; existing clients get honest notice with runway.
- The increase conversation is maintenance, not negotiation: no apology, no justification wall.
- Grandfathering is designed: defined gap, defined endpoint, never open-ended.
- Big jumps ride on repositioning; the rate card is living infrastructure with a review cadence.
