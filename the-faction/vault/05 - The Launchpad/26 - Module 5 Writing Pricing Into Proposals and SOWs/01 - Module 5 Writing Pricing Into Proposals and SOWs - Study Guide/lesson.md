# Module 5: Writing Pricing Into Proposals and SOWs — Study Guide

# Pricing Your Builds

### T7 The Launchpad | Module 5 Study Guide

## Module 5: Writing Pricing Into Proposals and SOWs

> Direct AI to draft proposals where the price lands after the value case is made, inside a document built to be signed, not studied.

## Why This Matters

A price that survived the conversation can still die in the document. Where pricing sits in a proposal, how options are presented, and what the payment terms demand all decide whether the number you defended on the call gets signed or reopened. This module covers pricing inside documents; the full craft of proposals and SOWs gets its own course downstream.

## Core Concepts

**Price comes after the case, never before it.** A proposal that opens with the number invites judgment before context. The reading order is problem, outcome, approach, then investment. By the time the price appears, it should be the reasonable conclusion of everything above it.

**The proposal sells; the SOW protects.** A proposal argues why this project at this price; the statement of work defines exactly what will be delivered, what is excluded, and what change costs. Pricing appears in both, but its job differs: persuasive framing in the proposal, precise terms in the SOW.

**Options belong in threes, at most.** One option invites yes-or-no; two invites cheap-or-expensive; three shapes a choice between versions of yes. More than three re-opens the analysis you were closing. Each option needs a name, a scope, and a price; differences must be legible in seconds.

**The investment section is a frame, not a table.** Name the section Investment, restate the outcome in one line, present the price cleanly, and put the detail beneath it. A wall of line items invites itemized negotiation; a clean number tied to an outcome invites a decision.

**Payment terms are pricing.** Deposit size, milestone schedule, final payment trigger, and late terms decide when the money actually arrives. A strong price with weak terms is a discount in disguise: 12,000 collected 90 days late is not 12,000.

**Deposits and finals bracket the risk.** The deposit, commonly 30 to 50 percent for project work, filters unserious buyers and funds the start; the final payment against defined delivery criteria ends the engagement cleanly. Everything between rides on milestones from Module 3.

## How It Works

Placing pricing inside the document set:

1. Structure the proposal: problem, outcome, approach, proof, then the Investment section; the price appears once the case is complete.
1. Present up to three named options with legible differences, the recommended one visually centered or marked.
1. Write the Investment section as outcome plus number; keep line-item detail in an appendix or the SOW, where it informs instead of invites haggling.
1. Mirror the numbers into the SOW with precision: scope, exclusions, change process, milestone amounts, and acceptance criteria per payment.
1. State terms explicitly: deposit due to begin, milestone triggers, final payment window, and what late payment does; then direct AI to check that proposal and SOW numbers agree.

## Directing AI

1. "Restructure this draft so the price appears after problem, outcome, approach, and proof; flag anything that leaks numbers early."
1. "Turn this scope into three named options with prices, differences legible at a glance, and the middle option as the recommendation."
1. "Draft the Investment section: one outcome sentence, the price, and payment terms; move all line-item detail to the SOW appendix."
1. "Write the SOW pricing terms for this project: deposit, milestone amounts with acceptance criteria, final payment trigger, and late terms."
1. "Cross-check this proposal and SOW: list every number that appears in both and flag any mismatch or term stated only once."

## Common Mistakes

1. **The price on page one.** Leading with the number invites judgment before the case exists; it reads as a quote, not a proposal.
1. **A la carte pricing walls.** Fifteen line items with prices invite the client to build their own cheaper project from your parts.
1. **Options that overwhelm.** Five choices with subtle differences stall decisions; the prospect defers, and deferred proposals die.
1. **Proposal-SOW drift.** Numbers that differ between documents, or terms stated in only one, become disputes at invoice time.
1. **No payment terms until the invoice.** Terms introduced after signing feel imposed; terms inside the proposal are simply the deal.
1. **Final payment on "satisfaction."** The Module 3 rule applies to documents: finals trigger on defined delivery criteria, not feelings.

## Real-World Application

A builder sends a beautifully argued proposal for a 14,000 analytics build: the problem is quantified, the approach is sharp, and the price sits on the last page after the case. The client signs in two days. At invoice time, the trouble starts: the proposal said 14,000, but the hastily attached SOW still showed an earlier 12,500 draft number, and no deposit terms appeared in either document. The client, reasonably, pays 12,500, late, with no deposit ever collected. The builder directs AI to rebuild the document pipeline: one source of truth for numbers, a cross-check pass that compares proposal and SOW before anything sends, and standard terms, 40 percent deposit, milestones with acceptance criteria, final net 14, embedded in the proposal template itself. The next five projects sign and pay without a single number dispute.

## Decision Framework

- **Is the number appearing before the case is made?** Restructure; investment follows outcome.
- **Are there more than three options?** Cut to three or fewer with legible differences.
- **Is line-item detail sitting in the Investment section?** Move it to the SOW or appendix.
- **Do the proposal and SOW numbers match exactly?** Cross-check before sending, every time.
- **Are payment terms in the document the client signs?** If not, they are not agreed terms.
- **Is the final payment tied to defined criteria?** If it triggers on satisfaction, rewrite it.

## Tool and Platform Notes

Direct AI to maintain your proposal and SOW templates as a matched pair, with pricing sections that pull from the same numbers. Document tools with e-signature, PandaDoc, Docusign, or a well-built Notion-plus-PDF flow, shorten the gap between yes and signed. Invoicing through Stripe or Wave should mirror the milestone schedule automatically. The Proposals and SOWs course downstream covers the full documents; the pricing sections you build here drop straight into it.

## Key Takeaways

- Price appears after problem, outcome, approach, and proof; it is the conclusion, not the opener.
- The proposal frames the investment; the SOW nails the terms; the numbers must match exactly.
- Three named options at most, differences legible in seconds, recommendation marked.
- Clean outcome-plus-number beats line-item walls that invite itemized haggling.
- Terms are pricing: deposit to start, milestones with criteria, final against defined delivery.
