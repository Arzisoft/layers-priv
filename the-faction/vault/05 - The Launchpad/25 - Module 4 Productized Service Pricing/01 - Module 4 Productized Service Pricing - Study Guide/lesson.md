# Module 4: Productized Service Pricing — Study Guide

# Pricing Your Builds

### T7 The Launchpad | Module 4 Study Guide

## Module 4: Productized Service Pricing

> Direct AI to turn your best custom work into packages with prices on them, so selling stops requiring a custom quote every single time.

## Why This Matters

Custom quotes cost you unbillable hours and force every prospect through a sales conversation. Productizing turns work you have already done well into a repeatable offer with a fixed scope and a posted price. It is how a solo builder starts selling like a business instead of interviewing for every project.

## Core Concepts

**Productization is packaging repetition.** Look at your delivered projects: the same automation, the same audit, the same portal keeps appearing with minor variations. That repeated core, standardized and named, is a productized service: defined scope, defined deliverables, defined timeline, fixed price.

**Repetition is where the margin compounds.** The second and tenth delivery of a package cost you less than the first: prompts, templates, checklists, and infrastructure all get reused. The price stays constant while your cost per delivery falls, which is the productized version of keeping the AI speed advantage.

**Tiering serves different budgets without custom quotes.** Good, better, best. A three-tier structure catches small buyers, standard buyers, and premium buyers with one page. Tiers differ by scope and outcome, not by effort theatrics: each tier must be a complete, honest offer.

**The middle tier is the product.** Most buyers pick the middle option. Design the middle tier as the offer you actually want to sell, use the lower tier to make entry easy, and let the top tier anchor the price range while serving buyers who want everything handled.

**The menu and the custom quote coexist.** Productized packages handle the repeatable eighty percent; custom quoting remains for genuinely novel work. The menu also disciplines custom pricing: when a prospect asks for something bespoke, the packages anchor what bespoke costs.

**Pricing pages sell while you build.** A productized offer needs a page: what you get, who it is for, the price, the timeline, the process, and proof. You direct AI to build these pages, and they answer the pricing conversation before it starts.

## How It Works

Turning custom history into a productized offer:

1. Mine your history: direct AI to review past projects and flag the repeated deliverable cores.
1. Standardize the core: fixed scope, named deliverables, a timeline you can hit every time, and exclusions.
1. Price it with Module 2's lenses: value to the typical buyer, market context, floor check; the price is fixed, so scoping discipline from Module 3 applies.
1. Build the tiers: strip the core down for the entry tier, extend it with outcomes premium buyers want for the top tier.
1. Direct AI to build the pricing page and the delivery assets: intake forms, checklists, templates, so each sale runs the same playbook.

## Directing AI

1. "Review these six project descriptions and identify the repeated deliverable core that could become a productized package."
1. "Draft a good-better-best tier structure for my automation audit: scope, deliverables, timeline, and price logic per tier."
1. "Build a pricing page for this package: what you get, who it is for, price, timeline, process, and proof section, in my site's stack."
1. "Stress-test these tiers: where would a buyer feel forced into an upgrade, and which tier cannibalizes another? Propose fixes."
1. "Create the delivery kit for this package: intake questionnaire, kickoff checklist, and completion report template."

## Common Mistakes

1. **Productizing what you have never delivered.** Packages describe proven work; guessing at scope you have not done twice makes fixed pricing a gamble.
1. **Tiers that differ by punishment.** Stripping the entry tier until it fails buyers poisons trust; every tier must genuinely work.
1. **Four or more tiers.** Choice overload kills the page; three is the ceiling for almost every builder offer.
1. **Pricing tiers by effort spread.** Tiers priced on your hours instead of buyer outcomes recreate the hourly trap inside the menu.
1. **Letting the menu take custom work.** Forcing novel projects into packages ships bad fits; the quote path exists for a reason.
1. **A menu with no anchor.** Without a visible top tier, the middle tier becomes the expensive option instead of the reasonable one.

## Real-World Application

A builder has delivered eleven client-intake automations in a year, each quoted custom, each eating a week of unbillable sales effort. They direct AI to mine the eleven projects: the core is identical, and only integrations vary. The result is a three-tier menu. Intake Basic at 2,500: forms, routing, and notifications in two weeks. Intake Pro at 5,500: Basic plus CRM integration and reporting, the tier built to sell. Intake Complete at 9,500: Pro plus a second workflow and a quarter of support. A pricing page replaces the discovery calls for small deals; buyers arrive pre-anchored. Over the next quarter, sales conversations drop from hours to minutes on packaged deals, delivery cost falls with each repetition, and the two genuinely custom prospects that arrive get quoted against the menu's anchors.

## Decision Framework

- **Have you delivered this same core three or more times?** Productize it; the repetition is the signal.
- **Is the work genuinely novel each time?** Keep it in the custom quote path; do not force the menu.
- **Does a tier exist only to make another look good?** Cut or fix it; every tier must be a real offer.
- **Are buyers all choosing the top or bottom tier?** The middle tier is mispriced or misdesigned; rebalance.
- **Is a bespoke request arriving?** Quote it custom, anchored explicitly against the menu.
- **Is delivery cost falling with repetition?** Keep the price; that margin is the point of productizing.

## Tool and Platform Notes

Direct AI to build pricing pages in your existing stack: a Next.js page, a Framer or Webflow section, or a simple hosted page all work; consistency with your positioning matters more than the tool. Payment links per tier through Stripe make the menu transactable. The delivery kit lives as templates and checklists you reuse; version them as they improve. Track per-package economics in a simple sheet: price, delivery hours, tool costs per sale, so margin growth is visible.

## Key Takeaways

- Productize the work you have already repeated: fixed scope, fixed deliverables, fixed price.
- Margin compounds through repetition; the price holds while delivery cost falls.
- Three tiers: easy entry, the middle offer you want to sell, a top anchor that serves real buyers.
- Tiers differ by scope and outcome, never by sabotaging the lower tiers.
- The menu handles the repeatable; custom quotes remain for the novel, anchored by the menu.
