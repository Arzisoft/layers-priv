# Module 6: Discounting, Negotiation, and Holding Your Price — Study Guide

# Pricing Your Builds

### T7 The Launchpad | Module 6 Study Guide

## Module 6: Discounting, Negotiation, and Holding Your Price

> Direct AI to prepare you for the pressure moments, because negotiations are won by whoever decided their moves before the conversation started.

## Why This Matters

Every builder eventually meets the client who asks for less. What you do in that moment either protects the pricing structure you built across five modules or quietly dismantles it. Discounting is a tool with narrow, legitimate uses; unplanned, it is just erosion with good manners.

## Core Concepts

**Discounting is rare and always purchased.** A discount given for nothing teaches the client that your prices are opening bids. The legitimate discount is exchanged for something concrete: a case study and referral rights, prepayment in full, flexible timeline slack you can schedule around, or committed future volume. No concession without consideration.

**Volume and loyalty pricing are structures, not favors.** A retainer client on committed monthly work can carry different economics than a one-off project, openly and by design. That is a published structure with clear thresholds, not an in-the-moment cave. The difference is whether the lower number was designed in advance or extracted under pressure.

**The negotiation conversation has a shape.** The ask comes: "can you do better on price?" The disciplined response is a question, not a counter: what is driving the number, budget ceiling or value doubt? Budget problems get scope solutions. Value doubts get proof. Only after diagnosis do you decide whether anything moves.

**Trade scope, not price.** The strongest response to a real budget cap: hold the rate, shrink the work. Remove a deliverable, phase the project, or defer the nice-to-haves. The client controls spend; your price-to-value ratio survives intact. A price cut with unchanged scope teaches that the first number was padded.

**Price-only buyers are a segment to avoid.** Some prospects negotiate price because price is all they see; no proof, framing, or scope will change them. They churn fast, demand most, refer worst, and anchor your reputation at the bottom. Walking away is a pricing decision, and often the most profitable one available.

**Walking away needs a script.** Decline cleanly and warmly: the price stands, the door stays open, and no counteroffer follows the goodbye. Some walkaways return as full-price clients; the ones who do not were not your clients.

## How It Works

The pressure sequence, prepared in advance:

1. Before any negotiation, know your floor from Module 2, your tradeable scope items, and your walk-away line.
1. When the ask arrives, diagnose: "Help me understand, is this a budget ceiling or does the value feel off?"
1. Value doubt: return to proof and the cost-of-problem math. No number moves while trust is the issue.
1. Budget ceiling: offer a scope trade or phasing. The rate holds; the package shrinks.
1. If a discount is ever given, it is exchanged: name the consideration, put both sides in writing, and time-box it.
1. If the buyer wants only a lower number, decline warmly and finally. Record the outcome in your pricing journal.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Discounting at the first flinch.** Conceding before diagnosis; most asks are tests, and an instant drop fails the test.
1. **Free discounts.** Cutting price without getting anything in return converts your rate card into a suggestion.
1. **Trading price instead of scope.** Same work, lower number: the client learns the padding was real and negotiates every future invoice.
1. **Negotiating without a floor.** In-the-moment arithmetic under social pressure always rounds down.
1. **Confusing loyalty structures with caving.** A designed retainer rate is strategy; an improvised discount for a loud client is precedent.
1. **Chasing price-only buyers.** Winning them requires becoming the cheap option, which repels the clients your positioning was built for.

## Real-World Application

A builder quotes 11,000 for a booking automation. Procurement responds: "we have 8,500, take it or we go elsewhere." Old habits would split the difference. Instead the builder asks what is driving the number and learns it is a genuine quarterly budget cap, not doubt about the build. The builder holds the rate and offers two paths: the full scope phased across two quarters at 11,000 total, or an 8,500 version this quarter with the reporting module and one integration deferred. Procurement takes the 8,500 version, then adds the deferred scope the next quarter at full price anyway. Total collected: 11,000, on schedule, with the rate intact and a client who learned the builder's numbers are real.

## Decision Framework

- **Is the ask a test or a real constraint?** Diagnose before anything moves; most asks are tests.
- **Is it value doubt?** Proof and problem math, never a number; discounting doubt confirms it.
- **Is it a genuine budget cap?** Trade scope or phase the work; the rate holds.
- **Is a discount strategically worth it?** Only exchanged, named, written, and time-boxed.
- **Is this buyer price-only?** Decline warmly; the most expensive clients are the cheapest ones.
- **Did you concede something today?** Log it in the pricing journal and decide what changes next time.

## Tool and Platform Notes

AI is your sparring partner: direct it to play the procurement hardliner, the friendly haggler, and the silent flincher, and rehearse until your responses are boring. Keep the scope-trade list per proposal ready before calls; improvising trades under pressure leaks value. The pricing journal from Module 1 earns its keep here: every negotiation logged, every concession priced, patterns reviewed quarterly.

## Key Takeaways

- Discounts are purchased with consideration: case study, prepayment, commitment, or flexibility; never free.
- Diagnose the ask before responding: budget ceilings get scope solutions, value doubts get proof.
- Trade scope, phase work, hold the rate; a price cut with unchanged scope poisons every future quote.
- Volume and loyalty pricing are designed structures, not pressure responses.
- Walking away from price-only buyers is a legitimate, often profitable pricing decision.
