# Module 4: Market Sizing and Demand Signals — Exam

> Exam for **Module 4: Market Sizing and Demand Signals** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. You are sizing the dentist CRM market and your AI finds 130,000 US practices. Which number does the module say actually matters for a solo builder planning year one?

- **A.** The customers you can realistically win in year one, a slice smaller than TAM or even your reachable SAM segment.  ✅
- **B.** The full 130,000 practice TAM, because a bigger total market leaves more room to grow once your product gains real traction.
- **C.** The SAM, since every practice you could theoretically reach with marketing counts toward your first-year plan.
- **D.** The industry's total software spend, since dollars in the category matter more than counting individual practices.

> **Answer:** A

### Q2. A builder tells you the dental software market is 3 billion dollars, so capturing just 1 percent means 30 million. Why does the module call this top-down sizing self-deception?

- **A.** One percent is too ambitious for a first year; careful builders model a tenth of a percent and grow from that base.
- **B.** Top-down figures come from analyst reports that age badly, so the 3 billion number itself is probably outdated.
- **C.** It ignores competitors, and percentage claims only hold in markets where nobody else is fighting for the share.
- **D.** There is no mechanism that delivers the 1 percent; counting customers, price, and win rate bottom-up is truer.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q3. You direct your AI to size demand for an invoicing tool for landscapers. Which inputs build a proper bottom-up estimate?

- **A.** A countable number of customers, times a realistic price, times a realistic win rate you can actually defend.  ✅
- **B.** Total industry revenue divided by the number of competitors, giving the share a new entrant should expect to win.
- **C.** The market's projected growth rate applied to current TAM, discounted for the years you need to reach real scale.
- **D.** Search volume for your keywords times average ad cost, converting interest directly into revenue terms for you.

> **Answer:** A

### Q4. Your niche scheduling app shows strong search volume, but you have gathered no other evidence yet. How should you treat a single demand signal?

- **A.** One strong signal is enough to build on, since search volume measures people wanting a solution most directly.
- **B.** Signals only count when all five come back strongly positive, so one signal means shelving the idea right away.
- **C.** Treat it as a hint; three or more signals pointing the same way is what turns scattered clues into evidence.  ✅
- **D.** Search volume alone misleads and should be ignored until competitor revenue confirms real spending exists here.

> **Answer:** C

### Q5. You learn dentists already pay 300 dollars a month for clunky practice software they complain about. What does this existing spend tell you?

- **A.** It is the strongest signal: proof of budget, proof of pain, and a price anchor to position your product against.  ✅
- **B.** It is a warning sign, because entrenched spending means dentists sit locked in contracts you cannot break into.
- **C.** It mostly reveals pricing norms; whether dentists would switch tools requires a completely separate signal check.
- **D.** It shows saturation, so a solo builder should hunt for a nearby niche where nobody pays for software just yet.

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. Your AI finds zero paid tools in your target niche, and nobody currently spends money on this problem at all. According to the module, what game are you now playing?

- **A.** A wide-open land grab: with no competitors taking budget, you can define pricing and own the category early on.
- **B.** A timing race: the first paid tool in an unpaid niche usually wins, so building fast matters more than evidence.
- **C.** A marketing challenge: demand exists but is invisible, so heavier promotion will surface the buyers you need.
- **D.** A budget-creation game: instead of stealing existing spend, you must convince people to pay at all, a harder path.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q7. AI agents are trending hard and your feed is full of hot takes urging you to build in the space. What boring evidence separates a paying market from a trending topic?

- **A.** Rapid follower growth for the topic's biggest creators, since audience size eventually converts into paying customers.
- **B.** High engagement on launch posts, because comment volume shows intensity of interest better than raw traffic does.
- **C.** Coverage from serious media outlets, since journalists tend to validate markets earlier than procurement budgets.
- **D.** Paid competitors surviving for years, recurring subscriptions, job listings, and procurement budgets for the pain.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q8. Search interest in your niche spiked hard for six weeks, and you are tempted to start building immediately. What does the module tell you to check first?

- **A.** Whether the spike came from one viral post or many sources, since multi-source spikes reliably indicate demand.
- **B.** Whether competitors shipped products during the spike, because fast-follower activity confirms demand is real.
- **C.** Whether search volume and community activity held up over 12 to 24 months, because six hot weeks is only a spike.  ✅
- **D.** Whether the spike aligned with a funding cycle, since investor attention is what turns spikes into stable paying markets.

> **Answer:** C

### Q9. Your niche has 500 reachable customers who might pay 10 dollars a month, and you model a 3 percent win rate. How does the module classify this niche for a solo builder?

- **A.** Too small: even optimistic conversion cannot clear an income goal here, so the math describes a hobby project.  ✅
- **B.** Viable: 500 customers is plenty, because niche products routinely convert 20 percent of a tightly defined audience.
- **C.** Promising: small paying niches nearly always expand once a good product exists, so early math understates them.
- **D.** Undecidable: math alone cannot judge a niche, so you should build an MVP first and measure the real conversion.

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. You find a niche that could pay you well but looks laughably small to a venture capitalist. What makes a niche perfectly sized for a solo builder?

- **A.** It shows explosive growth potential, because a small niche must become tomorrow's big market to justify the whole build.
- **B.** It can pay you a real living while staying small enough that the big players ignore it instead of competing there.  ✅
- **C.** It carries zero competitors of any kind, since a solo builder cannot afford to fight anyone for a small market.
- **D.** It sits inside a large TAM, so investors can still fund you later even if your starting wedge looks tiny today.

> **Answer:** B

### Q11. You have twenty minutes to size the market for a retention tool aimed at independent gym owners. How does the Napkin TAM Method work?

- **A.** Pull three analyst reports on the fitness software market, average their TAM figures, and claim a 10 percent capture as your goal.
- **B.** Count customers via industry stats or directories, multiply by a realistic annual price, then take a 1 to 5 percent reachable share.  ✅
- **C.** Survey 20 gym owners on their willingness to pay, then extrapolate their answers across the national gym population.
- **D.** Estimate competitor revenue from public review counts, then assume you capture half of the smallest player's income.

> **Answer:** B

### Q12. Before building, you want to know whether people actually search for the problem your tool solves. What do free tools like Google Trends and Ubersuggest show you?

- **A.** The exact revenue a keyword can generate, letting you convert monthly search counts directly into a forecast.
- **B.** Which competitors bid on the term, which matters more than volume because bidding reveals who owns the customer.
- **C.** Whether people search for this problem, how often they do, and whether volume is growing, flat, or already dead.  ✅
- **D.** The demographic profile of searchers, confirming that the people looking actually match your target customer closely.

> **Answer:** C

### Q13. You cannot find public revenue numbers for the top competitor in your niche. How does the module tell you to run competitor revenue recon?

- **A.** Book a demo call and ask their sales rep about customer counts directly, since teams often share rough numbers.
- **B.** Track their hiring velocity on LinkedIn, since headcount growth is the one reliable public proxy for revenue.
- **C.** Check pricing pages and review counts on G2 and Capterra alongside public reports to estimate what they earn.  ✅
- **D.** Skip the estimate, since private companies cannot be sized and their revenue does not change your own math.

> **Answer:** C

### Q14. You scored your idea 0 to 2 on each of the five demand signals using the scorecard and landed at a total of 3. What does the module tell you to do with that score?

- **A.** Treat 3 as a passing start, since validated ideas often begin with weak signals that strengthen with evidence.
- **B.** Rescore using only your two strongest signals, because weak categories dilute the picture for niche products.
- **C.** Use it as a tiebreaker only, since totals matter just when you are choosing between two competing ideas.
- **D.** Be worried: under 4 total is a warning, while 7 or more across the signals means you keep moving forward.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q15. A subreddit with 80,000 members complains daily about the exact problem your product would solve. What does the module say this community proves?

- **A.** Strong demand: sustained complaining at that scale is the clearest available evidence of willingness to pay.
- **B.** A distribution channel: 80,000 gathered prospects matter more than any spend data you could hope to collect.
- **C.** Product direction: complaint threads act as validation and a spec, letting you skip all the other demand signals.
- **D.** Interest, not budget: talk is free, and free is exactly what many of those complaining members expect to pay.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q16. Dentists love your CRM demo, yet every deal stalls before anyone actually pays you. Which sizing pitfall did you most likely ignore?

- **A.** Treating a spike as a trend, since demo enthusiasm frequently peaks and fades before contracts can get signed.
- **B.** Ignoring who controls the budget: dentists feel the pain, but an office manager often picks and signs for software.  ✅
- **C.** The 1 percent fallacy, since your revenue model assumed a market share that no mechanism actually delivers to you.
- **D.** Confusing conversation with demand, since demo compliments are talk and talk indicates interest rather than any budget.

> **Answer:** B

### Q17. Your AI finds dozens of companies hiring billing coordinators to do manually the exact task your tool automates. What does this job-posting signal show?

- **A.** That the market prefers human labor, meaning software was already tried and rejected by these hiring companies.
- **B.** That automation is arriving from bigger vendors, since hiring waves usually precede enterprise software rollouts.
- **C.** That companies pay real salaries to solve this problem manually, which is evidence of budget already committed.  ✅
- **D.** That the niche is enterprise-only, so a solo builder should target smaller firms without such job listings.

> **Answer:** C

### Q18. You are filling out your Demand Signal Scorecard and want to classify each signal correctly. What does search volume specifically show?

- **A.** Budget already allocated to the pain, since searching for solutions implies money was set aside to buy one.
- **B.** People actively looking for a solution, which is distinct from the proof of payment other signals provide.  ✅
- **C.** Sustained engagement in the niche, the same underlying behavior community activity measures over longer time.
- **D.** Proof that money changes hands, since search platforms report volume for commercially active queries.

> **Answer:** B

### Q19. You are debating whether to spend an afternoon sizing your market or to just start directing your AI to build. Why does the module say the stakes favor sizing first?

- **A.** Sizing takes hours while building takes months, so honest numbers today can save you an entire wasted season.  ✅
- **B.** Sizing produces investor-ready figures, and no builder should write a prompt before the pitch math is finished.
- **C.** Building first locks in architecture, and pivoting a finished product costs more than any research ever would.
- **D.** Sizing reveals your exact revenue ceiling, letting you set pricing precisely before the first customer arrives.

> **Answer:** A

### Q20. A builder skips a niche that could pay 8,000 dollars a month because a VC would laugh at it, then enters a hot crowded category instead. Which pitfall is this?

- **A.** The 1 percent fallacy: assuming a small niche converts at rates no acquisition mechanism actually delivers.
- **B.** Treating a spike as a trend: hot categories are often six-week spikes dressed up as durable paying markets.
- **C.** Sizing the whole market instead of the reachable slice, since hot categories inflate every single number you touch.
- **D.** Fearing small markets on principle: a quiet niche paying you well beats tenth place in a crowded hot category.  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q21. Your niche could support about 7,000 dollars a month in revenue, far below anything a VC-backed startup would chase. Why can this still be a genuine win for you?

- **A.** Because small markets always grow, and holding the niche early guarantees you the larger market that follows it.
- **B.** Because you direct AI and keep costs low, a market supporting 5,000 to 10,000 dollars a month can pay you well.  ✅
- **C.** Because VC disinterest makes acquisition offers cheaper, letting you exit the niche profitably within a year.
- **D.** Because niches under 10,000 dollars a month attract no competition, which makes your marketing essentially free.

> **Answer:** B

### Q22. Your AI reports 130,000 dental practices in the US, but your channels can realistically reach about 500 of them. Which number should your market math be built on?

- **A.** The 500 you can actually touch, because the reachable slice is the only base an honest revenue model stands on.  ✅
- **B.** The 130,000, because total market size sets the ceiling, and ceilings are what long-term plans get built upon.
- **C.** A midpoint near 65,000, balancing ambition with realism so your estimate is neither timid nor badly inflated.
- **D.** Neither: skip customer counts and model revenue from competitor pricing pages and their public review volumes instead.

> **Answer:** A

### Q23. A competitor in your niche has charged 49 dollars a month for six years and shows hundreds of reviews on G2. How does the module read this situation for you?

- **A.** Their revenue is your proof of market: money demonstrably changes hands here, which validates demand for you.  ✅
- **B.** Their tenure is a barrier: six years of customer lock-in means the remaining market is too picked over to enter.
- **C.** Their price sets your ceiling: a new entrant must undercut at half their rate to pull away meaningful customers.
- **D.** Their reviews are the risk: hundreds of vocal users means switching costs run too high for any solo builder.

> **Answer:** A

### Q24. You are scoring the fifth demand signal, industry spend, for a compliance tool aimed at small clinics. What does industry spend tell you about the market?

- **A.** How fast the category is growing, which predicts whether budgets will exist by the time your product finally ships.
- **B.** The gap between big and small buyers, letting you price your product between the enterprise and consumer tiers.
- **C.** That budget is already allocated to this pain, meaning money is committed in the industry before you show up.  ✅
- **D.** The exact revenue ceiling for your product, since no tool captures more than an industry currently spends.

> **Answer:** C

### Q25. Stepping back from any single tactic or tool, what is the governing principle of Module 4, Market Sizing and Demand Signals?

- **A.** Choose the largest possible market, because an ambitious TAM gives a solo builder the most room for error and growth.
- **B.** Prove a market big enough to sustain you exists, using honest bottom-up math and converging demand signals, before you build.  ✅
- **C.** Trust community enthusiasm over financial evidence, since passionate users are the root of every durable market.
- **D.** Defer all sizing until after launch, because only real sales data can ever tell you how big a market truly is.

> **Answer:** B
