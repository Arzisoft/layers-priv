# Module 5: Competitive Differentiation — Exam

> Exam for **Module 5: Competitive Differentiation** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. A prospect's inbox holds six pitches, every one claiming to use cutting-edge AI. A builder wants to stand out in that pile. What does the module say about the AI claim itself?

- **A.** It still works if phrased precisely: naming specific models restores its distinctiveness
- **B.** It differentiates nothing anymore: when everyone claims it, the claim answers no question  ✅
- **C.** It works against newcomers but still separates veterans from the weekend hobbyists
- **D.** It should be doubled down on: repetition is what turns a common claim into a brand

> **Answer:** B

### Q2. A builder sits down to prepare a real answer to why you, out of everyone? Which five levers does the module put on the table?

- **A.** Specialization, process, speed, guarantee, and industry knowledge  ✅
- **B.** Price, portfolio, personality, persistence, and personal network
- **C.** Branding, advertising, referrals, partnerships, and content volume
- **D.** Certifications, awards, testimonials, case studies, and reviews

> **Answer:** A

### Q3. One builder serves anyone; another serves only veterinary clinics and knows their software, their regulations, and their busy season cold. Which lever is the second builder pulling?

- **A.** Process: the vet-only builder has systematized delivery into a repeatable method
- **B.** Guarantee: narrow focus lets the builder underwrite outcomes others cannot
- **C.** Speed: familiarity with one niche compresses every project timeline
- **D.** Specialization: serving one niche deeply is itself the differentiator  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q4. A builder brands their delivery as the 14-Day Install, with the same five steps on every engagement. What does this named method signal to prospects?

- **A.** Rigidity: sophisticated buyers read fixed processes as inflexibility in disguise
- **B.** Scale ambitions: named methods are what agencies use to prepare for hiring
- **C.** A system instead of improvisation: the work has a shape that has survived repetition  ✅
- **D.** Marketing polish: the name changes perception while the work stays identical

> **Answer:** C

### Q5. An agency quotes a quarter for a build; a solo builder quotes three weeks. The module says the shorter quote is credible for a specific reason. Which?

- **A.** The builder directs AI instead of managing headcount, so the speed is structural, not bravado  ✅
- **B.** Solo operators always beat agencies on speed because they skip all the internal meetings
- **C.** Three weeks is the industry's psychological sweet spot for most mid-sized projects
- **D.** The agency is padding its quote, so any confidently shorter number naturally reads as more honest

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. Two proposals are identical except one line: if this system does not deliver, I keep working at no charge until it does. Which lever is this, and what is it doing?

- **A.** The guarantee: the builder carries the risk that every other proposal leaves on the client  ✅
- **B.** The process: a defined remediation step signals a mature, disciplined delivery practice
- **C.** The specialization: only a niche expert could ever afford to make that kind of promise
- **D.** The speed: offering unlimited rework implicitly promises a much faster initial delivery pass

> **Answer:** A

### Q7. During a first call, a builder mentions the niche's compliance deadline before the client does and names the software the client uses. What is this lever, and why does it close deals?

- **A.** Preparation: researching each prospect individually signals hunger for the work
- **B.** Industry knowledge: anticipating problems outsiders discover late reads as insider trust  ✅
- **C.** Specialization theater: rehearsed details simulate depth without requiring it
- **D.** Consultative selling: leading with questions makes prospects sell themselves

> **Answer:** B

### Q8. A builder considers differentiating on price, reasoning that undercutting always wins the deal. What structural property of price does the module warn about?

- **A.** Price wars are actually illegal in some industries, exposing discounters to real regulatory risk
- **B.** Low prices attract high volumes, and volume overwhelms a solo builder's limited calendar
- **C.** Clients distrust cheap offers, so discounts suppress close rates across the board
- **D.** Any competitor can copy it instantly by typing a smaller number, so the race has no bottom  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q9. A builder who competes on price notices their client roster churns constantly while trust-driven buyers never call. What does the module say about who low prices select for?

- **A.** Price-sensitive clients are famously loyal once won, so the churn must come from delivery problems
- **B.** Low prices select randomly: the roster's churn reflects the market itself, not the price
- **C.** Discount hunters who will leave for the next discount, while trust buyers filter themselves out  ✅
- **D.** Enterprise procurement teams, who buy cheap but then negotiate hard on everything else

> **Answer:** C

### Q10. The module says a builder who defaults to being cheaper is broadcasting a message to the market. What is the message?

- **A.** Efficiency: this builder passes AI-driven savings straight to their clients
- **B.** No other answer exists to why you, so the number is doing all the talking  ✅
- **C.** Volume strategy: this builder profits on quantity rather than relationships
- **D.** New entrant energy: aggressive pricing signals hunger and fresh capacity

> **Answer:** B

### Q11. One builder says: I am great with dental practices. Another shows a named clinic's no-show rate falling 38 percent. What distinction is the module drawing between them?

- **A.** Claim versus proof: the statement asserts expertise while the number demonstrates it  ✅
- **B.** Modesty versus bragging: specific numbers can read as showing off to some conservative niches
- **C.** Retail versus wholesale trust: statements work in person, while numbers work online
- **D.** Speed versus depth: claims are faster to produce while real proof takes years to earn

> **Answer:** A

### Q12. A builder is assembling a proof portfolio from scratch. Which four asset types does the module specify?

- **A.** Certifications, degrees, tool badges, and platform partner status
- **B.** Awards, press mentions, follower counts, and conference talks
- **C.** Case studies, testimonials, portfolio pieces, and public content  ✅
- **D.** Referrals, discounts, free trials, and money-back guarantees

> **Answer:** C

### Q13. A builder drafts their first case study and asks what structure makes it work as proof. What does the module prescribe?

- **A.** The client's history, the builder's biography, and the story of how they met
- **B.** A technical architecture walkthrough with screenshots of every component
- **C.** Three testimonial quotes arranged around a photograph of the deliverable
- **D.** Problem, what was built, outcome, and the number that makes it concrete  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q14. A project just wrapped and the client is delighted. The builder plans to request a testimonial at the six-month mark, once results mature. What does the module say about this timing?

- **A.** Wise: testimonials with matured results carry more evidentiary weight
- **B.** Backwards: ask while the win is fresh when enthusiasm writes the best quote  ✅
- **C.** Irrelevant: testimonial quality depends on the asker's template, not timing
- **D.** Risky either way: testimonials should be drafted by the builder and approved

> **Answer:** B

### Q15. A builder publishes a weekly piece answering real questions their niche asks. The module distinguishes this from asserting expertise. What is the mechanism?

- **A.** Demonstration: explaining the niche's problems well is expertise made visible, not claimed  ✅
- **B.** Frequency: a weekly publishing cadence builds algorithmic reach that mere assertions never earn
- **C.** Reciprocity: free answers create a social debt that converts into paid work later
- **D.** Awareness: content keeps the builder's name in circulation between referral events

> **Answer:** A

### Q16. The module contrasts being known for one thing with being available for everything. What does it call the second condition?

- **A.** A growth stage: generalists are specialists who have not yet chosen
- **B.** A safety net: broad availability cushions the swings of a narrow niche
- **C.** A volume play: availability wins the long tail that specialists ignore
- **D.** A description of unemployment: no association forms, so no name comes to mind  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q17. A builder worries that being known for one thing means abandoning their range of skills. How does the module resolve this?

- **A.** It confirms the cost: specialists must retire the skills outside their niche
- **B.** It recommends rotating the one thing quarterly so every skill gets its season
- **C.** One thing means one association strong enough to repeat, not one skill in the toolbox  ✅
- **D.** It suggests publishing the full skill list somewhere prospects rarely look

> **Answer:** C

### Q18. The module describes specialization feeding knowledge, knowledge sharpening content, content generating proof, proof justifying guarantees. What is it describing, and who gets it?

- **A.** A funnel: any builder gets it by publishing enough content at the top
- **B.** A treadmill: specialists get it, and it explains their eventual burnout
- **C.** A flywheel that compounds, and only builders who pick a lane get to ride it  ✅
- **D.** A lottery: the loop exists but luck decides which builders it rewards

> **Answer:** C

### Q19. A builder enters a new niche with zero case studies and zero testimonials. Which levers does the module say are honestly available on day one?

- **A.** None: differentiation requires proof, so the first months must be undifferentiated
- **B.** Speed and guarantee: deliver fast and carry the risk while proof is manufactured  ✅
- **C.** Industry knowledge alone: everything else must wait for finished projects
- **D.** Borrowed proof: general portfolio pieces substitute until niche ones exist

> **Answer:** B

### Q20. Six months in, a builder has finished eight solid projects but has no case studies, no testimonials, and no content. What does the module call this situation?

- **A.** Proof left unmanufactured: the raw material exists and nobody turned it into assets  ✅
- **B.** A marketing problem: the work stays invisible because no advertising budget promotes it
- **C.** Normal accumulation: proof assembles itself organically over several years of work
- **D.** A positioning failure: eight finished projects without proof means the niche itself was wrong

> **Answer:** A

### Q21. A competitor copies a builder's positioning statement word for word. The builder panics. What does the module say the competitor could not copy?

- **A.** The wording rhythm: copied statements always read as slightly off to attentive buyers
- **B.** The niche relationships: tight communities reject obvious copycats on principle
- **C.** The pricing structure: imitators rarely see the full offer stack behind the statement
- **D.** The proof: case studies, testimonials, and published expertise stay with their owner  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q22. Two builders publish weekly. One writes: Five Ways AI Changes Business. The other answers deposit-policy questions from a salon owners' group. Whose content differentiates, and why?

- **A.** The first: broad topics reach more readers, and reach is what content is for
- **B.** The second: niche answers prove expertise to the exact audience that buys  ✅
- **C.** Both equally: consistency of publishing matters more than topic selection
- **D.** Neither: content cannot differentiate because everyone publishes constantly

> **Answer:** B

### Q23. A freelancer stuck losing $45 gigs on Fiverr repositions with a niche, one case study, one video testimonial, and weekly posts in the niche's group. A chain later hires her at four times her old rate without bidding. What changed?

- **A.** She left the comparison list entirely: proof made her the only visible expert in the room  ✅
- **B.** Her price rose, and premium pricing by itself signals higher quality to bigger buyers
- **C.** The chain was an outlier: enterprise-level buyers ignore the rates that small buyers fight over
- **D.** Timing: the niche's busy season inflated demand well beyond the supply of builders

> **Answer:** A

### Q24. A builder's niche lives in a trade association newsletter and a private Facebook group, but the builder publishes only on a tech blogging platform. What does the module say about placement?

- **A.** Correct choice: technical platforms confer a level of credibility that trade groups cannot
- **B.** Publish everywhere simultaneously: placement matters far less than total volume
- **C.** Keep the tech platform but layer paid ads on top to bridge into the niche's own spaces
- **D.** Publish where the buyers gather: the trade group beats the tech platform for this niche  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q25. One principle governs this module, from the five levers to the proof engine. Which statement expresses it?

- **A.** Differentiation is inherited: early movers in a niche keep the advantage forever
- **B.** Differentiation is declared: keep saying what makes you different loudly, often, and everywhere
- **C.** Differentiation is built: levers you can honestly pull, backed by proof you manufacture  ✅
- **D.** Differentiation is priced: the market simply reads your rate as your quality signal

> **Answer:** C
