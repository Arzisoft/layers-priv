# Module 4: Building Your Portfolio — Exam

> Exam for **Module 4: Building Your Portfolio** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. What is a portfolio's actual job for an AI Directed Engineer?

- **A.** Proving technical vocabulary, since prospects hire whoever names the most tools and platforms
- **B.** Archiving everything ever built, since completeness separates professionals from hobbyists
- **C.** Converting a stranger's doubt into confidence that you can solve their specific kind of problem  ✅
- **D.** Satisfying certification requirements, since credentials remain invalid without a published site

> **Answer:** C

### Q2. A prospect visits your portfolio. What should each project communicate first?

- **A.** The problem it solved and the result it produced, told in terms a business owner cares about  ✅
- **B.** The full technology stack, since informed prospects evaluate builders by their tooling choices
- **C.** The number of hours it took, since efficiency statistics are the strongest form of proof
- **D.** The AI conversation transcripts, since raw prompts are the most honest record of the work

> **Answer:** A

### Q3. You're newly certified with no client work to show. How do you build a portfolio anyway?

- **A.** Wait until a client hires you, since demonstration projects without a paying customer mislead
- **B.** Display your certification documents alone, since credentials outweigh projects at the start
- **C.** License stock case studies, adapting published examples from established firms into your own
- **D.** Direct AI to build real solutions to real problems, your own or a niche's, and document them  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q4. What structure makes a case study land with business readers?

- **A.** Chronological build log: every step in order, so readers experience the project as you did
- **B.** Problem, approach, result: the situation before, what you did, and the measurable change after  ✅
- **C.** Technical appendix first, since credibility must be established before any story is believable
- **D.** Client biography, since readers connect with people long before they connect with outcomes

> **Answer:** B

### Q5. Why do short demo videos outperform static screenshots in a builder's portfolio?

- **A.** Software is motion; watching a real flow work builds a level of belief that images cannot  ✅
- **B.** Videos rank higher in search engines, which is the primary source of portfolio traffic
- **C.** Screenshots can be faked, while videos are impossible to stage or edit in a meaningful way
- **D.** Videos autoplay on most platforms, forcing engagement from visitors who would have skimmed

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. How many projects does an effective early portfolio need?

- **A.** At least twenty, since volume is how a new builder compensates for the absence of famous clients
- **B.** Exactly one, since focus means never showing a second project until the first goes viral
- **C.** A few strong, relevant, well-documented pieces beat a large collection of shallow ones  ✅
- **D.** None, if your certification is current, since credentials make project evidence redundant

> **Answer:** C

### Q7. How should your portfolio handle the fact that AI built the software under your direction?

- **A.** Hide it completely, since disclosure invites prospects to attempt the very same thing themselves
- **B.** Mention it only if asked directly, keeping the method ambiguous in all written material
- **C.** Apologize for it, framing AI direction as a budget alternative to real engineering work
- **D.** Own it openly as the method: you direct AI through a professional process and own the output  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q8. Where should your portfolio live?

- **A.** A site you control, plus visibility wherever your target clients actually spend their attention  ✅
- **B.** Only inside freelance marketplaces, since independent sites cannot be verified by any prospects
- **C.** A private document shared after a signed agreement, protecting your work from being copied
- **D.** Social platforms exclusively, since owning a website stopped mattering once feeds took over

> **Answer:** A

### Q9. When possible, why link to a live, working version of a build instead of describing it?

- **A.** Live links are cheaper to maintain than written descriptions, which require regular editing
- **B.** Letting a prospect click around removes the trust gap that words and images always leave  ✅
- **C.** Live software cannot be criticized, since anything running in production is beyond question
- **D.** Search engines require working links before a portfolio site can be indexed properly at all

> **Answer:** B

### Q10. You want to feature a client build in your portfolio. What has to happen first?

- **A.** Nothing; delivering the work automatically grants you unlimited rights to display all of it
- **B.** Waiting one calendar year, after which any delivered work becomes publicly usable by default
- **C.** Getting the client's permission, and agreeing on what details, data, and names can appear  ✅
- **D.** Rebuilding it from memory with different colors, which removes the need for any conversation

> **Answer:** C

### Q11. You serve one niche. How should that shape the portfolio?

- **A.** It shouldn't; portfolios must stay general so no future opportunity is excluded by focus
- **B.** Show unrelated industries deliberately, since range impresses prospects more than relevance
- **C.** Split into a dozen micro-portfolios, one for every service any client could ever request
- **D.** Lead with the problems, language, and examples of that niche so prospects feel understood  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q12. What turns a decent case study into a persuasive one?

- **A.** Length, since ten-page case studies signal depth that shorter write-ups cannot communicate
- **B.** Numbers: hours saved, revenue affected, errors eliminated, measured change over adjectives  ✅
- **C.** Exclusivity, since case studies behind an email gate convert better than public ones do
- **D.** Celebrity association, since mentioning well-known brands nearby transfers their credibility

> **Answer:** B

### Q13. Your portfolio still leads with work from a year ago that no longer reflects your capability. What is the cost?

- **A.** Prospects judge you at your old ceiling; a portfolio ages backward unless you keep feeding it  ✅
- **B.** Nothing, since portfolios are historical archives and prospects expect dated material inside them
- **C.** Legal exposure, since displaying superseded work violates consumer accuracy regulations
- **D.** Search penalties, since engines demote sites whose newest entry is over ninety days old

> **Answer:** A

### Q14. Beyond finished builds, what portfolio element differentiates an AI Directed Engineer most?

- **A.** A gallery of certificates and badges, since visual credentials outweigh explanations of method
- **B.** A list of every AI model used with version numbers, proving currency with frontier tooling
- **C.** A public pricing calculator, since transparent rates are the strongest differentiation there is
- **D.** Evidence of your process: how you scope, direct, verify, and ship, the system behind results  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q15. Which item should come out of your portfolio?

- **A.** A small project for an unknown local business, since minor clients dilute the brand you build
- **B.** A build where the client only gave a short testimonial, since brief praise reads as unenthusiastic
- **C.** A dated build that no longer runs properly, since one broken demo taxes everything's credibility  ✅
- **D.** Anything built partly with AI assistance, since mixed-method work confuses the positioning

> **Answer:** C

### Q16. Do personal projects belong in a professional portfolio?

- **A.** Yes, when they solve a real problem and are framed by the need, the build, and the outcome  ✅
- **B.** No, since work without an invoice attached is a hobby and reads as such to serious buyers
- **C.** Only if they earned revenue, since profitability is what converts a project into a credential
- **D.** Only as a hidden page, shown when a prospect explicitly asks what you build for yourself

> **Answer:** A

### Q17. How should a portfolio piece open?

- **A.** With the technology chosen, since informed buyers scan for the stack before reading anything
- **B.** With the problem in the client's words, since recognition pulls a prospect into the story  ✅
- **C.** With your biography, since trust in the person precedes any interest in the work itself
- **D.** With the price of the engagement, since qualified prospects self-select when costs lead

> **Answer:** B

### Q18. How do testimonials work hardest in a portfolio?

- **A.** Collected on a separate praise page, so their total volume can be experienced all at once there
- **B.** Anonymous and unattributed, since names distract from the substance of the compliment
- **C.** Written by you and approved by the client, ensuring each one covers the desired points
- **D.** Placed next to the relevant project, specific about the result, attributed to a real person  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q19. You want a specific company as a client. What is the boldest portfolio move available to an AI Directed Engineer?

- **A.** Direct AI to build a working slice of their problem's solution and lead your pitch with it  ✅
- **B.** Publish an open letter describing their weaknesses, since public critique commands attention
- **C.** Buy advertising targeting their employees, since internal pressure reaches decision makers
- **D.** Copy their existing product entirely, proving capability through complete reproduction

> **Answer:** A

### Q20. A technical stakeholder asks to evaluate the quality behind a portfolio piece. What serves you best?

- **A.** Refusing, since methods are trade secrets and inspection requests signal a bad-faith buyer
- **B.** Sending everything with no framing, since sheer volume of material demonstrates full transparency
- **C.** Showing how quality is enforced: your review process, testing approach, and security practices  ✅
- **D.** Redirecting to your testimonials, since satisfied clients answer every technical question

> **Answer:** C

### Q21. Your portfolio attracts inquiries, but mostly from bad-fit prospects. What does that reveal?

- **A.** Success; any inquiry volume is positive, and fit gets determined later during the paid discovery
- **B.** The portfolio is aimed wrong; what you display and how you frame it decides who reaches out  ✅
- **C.** Nothing; inquiry quality is random and unrelated to how a portfolio presents its work
- **D.** The market is bad, since portfolio presentation cannot influence which prospects respond

> **Answer:** B

### Q22. How should a portfolio demo video be structured?

- **A.** Outcome first and short: show the result working within seconds, then the highlights that matter  ✅
- **B.** Long and complete: a full tour of every screen, since prospects distrust anything abbreviated at all
- **C.** Music-driven with no narration, since explanation reduces the emotional impact of the visuals
- **D.** Live and unedited, including the mistakes, since polish signals staging to modern viewers

> **Answer:** A

### Q23. When is the right time to create the portfolio material for a build?

- **A.** Years later, once enough distance exists to evaluate which projects deserve documentation
- **B.** Only after the client relationship ends completely, avoiding any appearance of promotion
- **C.** Never during delivery, since documentation effort visibly slows the work a client is funding
- **D.** During the build: capture the before-state, progress, and results while they're happening  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q24. How do you know whether your portfolio is actually working?

- **A.** Compliments from other builders, since peer recognition is the leading indicator of quality work
- **B.** Time visitors spend on the site, since attention duration converts to revenue predictably
- **C.** Qualified inquiries and closed work it contributes to, tracked by asking what convinced them  ✅
- **D.** Awards submitted and won, since third-party judging is the only objective measure available

> **Answer:** C

### Q25. Which principle should drive how you build and maintain your portfolio?

- **A.** Perfection before publication: nothing goes public until it cannot possibly be criticized
- **B.** Proof over polish: show real problems solved, with results, updated as your capability grows  ✅
- **C.** Volume over selection: every project ever touched belongs in front of every prospect
- **D.** Mystery over clarity: reveal little, since intrigue generates more inquiries than information

> **Answer:** B
