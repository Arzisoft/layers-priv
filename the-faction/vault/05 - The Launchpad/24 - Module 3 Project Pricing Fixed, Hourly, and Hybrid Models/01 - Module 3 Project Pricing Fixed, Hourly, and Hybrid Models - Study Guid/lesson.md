# Module 3: Project Pricing: Fixed, Hourly, and Hybrid Models — Study Guide

# Pricing Your Builds

### T7 The Launchpad | Module 3 Study Guide

## Module 3: Project Pricing: Fixed, Hourly, and Hybrid Models

> Direct AI to pressure-test your project structure before you quote, because the pricing model you pick decides who carries the risk.

## Why This Matters

The number matters, but the structure around it matters just as much. Fixed, hourly, and hybrid are not interchangeable wrappers; each one moves risk between you and the client. Builders who default to one model for everything end up either eating scope creep or capping their own upside.

## Core Concepts

**Fixed price sells certainty.** One number for a defined outcome. Clients love it because their risk is capped. You carry the delivery risk, which means fixed pricing is only as safe as your scoping is sharp. Well-scoped fixed projects are where AI-speed margins live.

**Hourly sells time, and time is the wrong product.** Hourly pricing feels safe because every hour gets paid, but it caps income at hours worked, punishes your efficiency, invites micromanagement of timesheets, and turns every AI-driven acceleration into a client discount. This is the hourly trap, and it hits vibecoders hardest because their hours are the most compressed.

**Hybrid splits the risk deliberately.** A fixed base for the defined scope plus an hourly or day rate for work beyond it, or a fixed discovery phase followed by a fixed build quote. Hybrids fit projects where part of the work is knowable and part is genuinely open-ended.

**Scoping accuracy is what makes fixed pricing safe.** A fixed price on a vague scope is a donation. Define deliverables, exclusions, assumptions, and the change process in writing. The sharper the scope, the more of the AI speed advantage you keep.

**Milestones structure payment and trust.** Deposit before work begins, payments tied to defined milestones, final payment on delivery, never on "when we feel done." Milestones protect cash flow, cap your exposure to nonpayment, and give clients checkpoints that build confidence.

**Each model protects you somewhere.** Fixed protects your margin when scope is tight. Hourly protects you when scope is unknowable, like exploratory or rescue work on someone else's mess. Hybrid protects you when a project has both kinds of work in it.

## How It Works

Choosing the structure for a real project:

1. Assess scope certainty: can the outcome be defined precisely enough to write exclusions?
1. If yes, go fixed: scope document, price from Module 2's lenses, milestone schedule attached.
1. If the work is genuinely unknowable, quote hourly or day-rate with a weekly cap and reporting rhythm, and revisit once the unknowns resolve.
1. If part is defined and part is open, structure a hybrid: fixed base plus rate card for the open portion, or paid discovery first, then a fixed build quote.
1. Whatever the model: deposit up front, milestone payments, final payment against delivery, and a written change process.

## Directing AI

1. "Here is my project description. List every ambiguity a client could exploit under a fixed price, and draft the exclusions section that closes each one."
1. "Structure this project as fixed, hourly, and hybrid. For each, show who carries which risk and what happens if the work runs 40 percent over."
1. "Draft a milestone payment schedule for this 12,000 build: deposit, two milestones, and delivery, with acceptance criteria per milestone."
1. "A client insists on hourly for a well-defined build I can deliver fast. Model my revenue both ways and draft language steering them to fixed."
1. "Design a paid discovery offer for this messy legacy project: deliverables, fixed price, timeline, and how it feeds the fixed build quote after."

## Common Mistakes

1. **Fixed price on a vague scope.** The classic donation: every ambiguity becomes free work, and the client controls the ambiguity.
1. **Hourly on well-defined fast work.** The trap in action: your four-day AI delivery bills a fraction of what the outcome is worth.
1. **No change process.** Without a written path for scope changes, every "small addition" is a negotiation you lose politely.
1. **Work before deposit.** Starting unpaid signals the invoice is negotiable and hands the client all the leverage.
1. **Milestones tied to vibes.** Payments triggered by "satisfaction" instead of defined acceptance criteria stall exactly when you need them.
1. **One model for everything.** Structure is a per-project decision; defaulting to your comfortable model ignores where the risk sits.

## Real-World Application

A builder takes on a rescue project: a half-finished SaaS someone else abandoned, plus a clearly defined client portal the same company wants next. Quoting both fixed would be a donation; the rescue scope is unknowable until the code is opened. Quoting both hourly would trap the portal work under the hourly ceiling. The builder structures it as a hybrid: the rescue runs at a day rate with a weekly cap and Friday reports, and once the codebase is mapped, the remaining rescue work converts to a fixed quote alongside the portal at a fixed 11,000 with deposit and two milestones. The client gets certainty where certainty is possible; the builder gets protection where it is not.

## Decision Framework

- **Can you write the exclusions list without guessing?** Fixed; your speed becomes margin.
- **Is the scope unknowable until you are inside it?** Hourly or day rate with a cap, then convert when it clarifies.
- **Is it half defined, half exploratory?** Hybrid: fixed base plus rate card, or paid discovery then fixed.
- **Is the client demanding hourly for defined work?** Show both models' math; sell the certainty of fixed.
- **Is nonpayment risk real?** Deposit and milestone structure regardless of model.
- **Did scope change mid-project?** Invoke the written change process; do not absorb it.

## Tool and Platform Notes

Direct AI to draft scope documents, exclusions lists, milestone schedules, and change-order templates; these are reusable assets that sharpen with every project. Keep a rate card current even if you rarely quote hourly: it anchors your hybrid rate and your change-order pricing. Payment terms live in invoicing tools like Stripe or Wave with deposits and milestones as separate invoices. Contract language deserves professional review; templates are a start, not legal advice.

## Key Takeaways

- Pricing structure allocates risk: fixed puts delivery risk on you, hourly puts cost risk on the client, hybrid splits it.
- The hourly trap caps income and converts AI speed into client discounts; avoid it for defined work.
- Fixed pricing is safe exactly to the degree that scope, exclusions, and change process are written.
- Deposits and milestone payments with acceptance criteria protect cash flow under every model.
- Choose structure per project by scope certainty, not by habit.
