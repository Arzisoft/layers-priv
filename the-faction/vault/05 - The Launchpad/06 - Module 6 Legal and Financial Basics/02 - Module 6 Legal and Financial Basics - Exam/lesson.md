# Module 6: Legal and Financial Basics — Exam

> Exam for **Module 6: Legal and Financial Basics** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. Why do builders commonly form a legal entity like an LLC instead of operating personally?

- **A.** An entity is required before any business bank will process incoming payments of any size
- **B.** It helps separate business obligations from personal assets and signals a real business operation  ✅
- **C.** Entities pay no taxes in most regions, converting every dollar of revenue directly into personal income
- **D.** Clients are legally barred from hiring individuals, so an entity is the price of admission

> **Answer:** B

### Q2. At minimum, what should a client contract establish?

- **A.** The full history of the relationship, documenting every conversation that preceded signature
- **B.** Only the price, since scope and timing are operational details that don't belong in contracts
- **C.** What you'll deliver, what you'll be paid, when, who owns the work, and what happens if things go wrong  ✅
- **D.** Penalties against the client for indecision, since consequences are what make clients truly responsive

> **Answer:** C

### Q3. A long-time acquaintance wants a build and says a contract feels insulting between friends. What's the reality?

- **A.** Written terms protect the friendship by making expectations explicit before money stress tests them  ✅
- **B.** They're right; contracts between people with existing relationships legally void themselves anyway there
- **C.** A contract is unnecessary if you have text messages, since chat history outranks formal documents
- **D.** The friendship itself is the contract, and courts consistently treat personal history as binding

> **Answer:** A

### Q4. What does a limitation of liability clause do in a services contract?

- **A.** It removes all responsibility from the builder, making any level of negligence fully consequence-free
- **B.** It guarantees the client unlimited refunds, since liability always flows toward the provider
- **C.** It prevents either party from ever suing, converting all disputes into private negotiations
- **D.** It caps what you could owe if something goes wrong, typically tied to what you were paid for the work  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q5. When should ownership of the delivered work transfer to the client?

- **A.** Per the contract — commonly upon full payment, so the work isn't owned by someone who hasn't paid  ✅
- **B.** The moment work begins, since clients own everything produced during any engagement they have started
- **C.** Never; builders should permanently own client systems and lease usage rights back monthly
- **D.** One year after delivery, since a waiting period protects both parties from early disputes

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. Why keep a separate bank account for the business from day one?

- **A.** Personal accounts legally cannot receive business payments over a small threshold in most regions today
- **B.** Clean separation makes taxes, bookkeeping, and any entity protections dramatically simpler to keep  ✅
- **C.** Business accounts earn government-subsidized interest rates unavailable to personal accounts
- **D.** Clients can see your account type when paying and will cancel deals over a personal account

> **Answer:** B

### Q7. What belongs on every invoice you send?

- **A.** A handwritten signature, since unsigned invoices are unenforceable in most business regions
- **B.** Your full cost breakdown including subcontractor rates, since clients are owed margin visibility
- **C.** A daily late fee starting immediately, since aggressive terms are the norm in all professional billing
- **D.** What the charge covers, the amount, the due date, payment method, and who to contact with questions  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q8. A client is 30 days past due on an invoice. Where was this battle actually won or lost?

- **A.** In court, since litigation is the only mechanism that reliably produces payment from late clients
- **B.** In the friendship, since personal warmth is what determines whether businesses pay on time
- **C.** Up front: payment terms, due dates, late consequences, and deposits agreed before work began  ✅
- **D.** At delivery, since withholding the final product until wire transfer is the standard practice

> **Answer:** C

### Q9. You're newly self-employed and revenue is arriving. What tax habit prevents the classic disaster?

- **A.** Setting aside a share of every payment as it arrives and learning your estimated payment duties  ✅
- **B.** Waiting until year-end to think about taxes, since projections made any earlier are always wrong anyway
- **C.** Spending freely, since business owners can deduct their way out of any tax bill afterward
- **D.** Keeping revenue in cash, since undeposited income is not yet taxable in most jurisdictions

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. Why track business expenses carefully from the first month?

- **A.** Expense reports are required attachments on every invoice that gets sent to enterprise clients today
- **B.** Legitimate business costs generally reduce taxable income, and rebuilding records later is painful  ✅
- **C.** Untracked expenses void business insurance policies retroactively in most business regions
- **D.** Banks review expense discipline before approving the accounts businesses need to operate

> **Answer:** B

### Q11. What does professional liability insurance broadly cover for a builder?

- **A.** Your personal health costs, since freelancers access medical coverage through work policies
- **B.** Client bankruptcy, reimbursing you for invoices that an insolvent client never actually paid
- **C.** Claims that your professional work or advice caused a client financial harm, plus defense costs  ✅
- **D.** Equipment theft only, since professional policies are physical-property products at their very core

> **Answer:** C

### Q12. When does hiring an accountant or tax professional clearly pay for itself?

- **A.** Never for builders, since modern software makes professional guidance strictly redundant
- **B.** Only past seven figures in revenue, since professionals decline smaller engagements anyway
- **C.** Only after a tax authority contacts you, since professionals exist to resolve active disputes only
- **D.** Early — once real money flows, entity and filing decisions compound, and mistakes cost more than fees  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q13. What is the healthiest way to think about contracts in client relationships?

- **A.** They protect both sides by writing the deal down while everyone agrees, before stress distorts it  ✅
- **B.** They are weapons for the party with better lawyers, so avoid signing anything wherever it is possible
- **C.** They are formalities nobody reads, existing to satisfy accountants rather than shape behavior
- **D.** They replace trust entirely, since a signed document makes the relationship itself irrelevant

> **Answer:** A

### Q14. A client wants you to contractually guarantee their AI-built app will hit a revenue target. How do you respond?

- **A.** Sign it, since confidence in your own work means accepting any metric the client proposes
- **B.** Sign it but privately plan to renegotiate once the build demonstrates early positive results
- **C.** Decline; you can commit to delivering defined, working scope, not outcomes you don't control  ✅
- **D.** Counter with a lower revenue target, since negotiation is about meeting in the middle somewhere

> **Answer:** C

### Q15. Why should your contract explicitly address ownership and rights in the AI-built deliverable?

- **A.** Because AI-assisted work is unownable by default, making every such engagement legally void otherwise
- **B.** Because clarity on who owns what — their deliverable, your reusable tools and methods — prevents disputes  ✅
- **C.** Because AI vendors claim automatic ownership of outputs unless contracts override them expressly
- **D.** Because clients cannot legally resell AI-built software without a notarized transfer document

> **Answer:** B

### Q16. You'll handle real customer data inside a client's systems during the build. What should exist before you touch it?

- **A.** Nothing special; access granted by the client transfers all responsibility for the data to them
- **B.** A press embargo, since data-touching engagements attract media interest that must be managed
- **C.** Your public liability disclaimer posted on your website, which covers all client data by default anyway
- **D.** Confidentiality obligations in writing and clarity on what data you may access, use, and must protect  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q17. What is the practical difference between operating as yourself and through a properly maintained entity if a project goes badly wrong?

- **A.** A properly run entity can help keep business claims aimed at business assets rather than personal ones  ✅
- **B.** There is none in any circumstance, since courts always treat businesses and owners identically
- **C.** Entities make you immune from lawsuits entirely, converting legal risk into a solved problem
- **D.** Operating personally is safer, since plaintiffs are far more reluctant to sue named individuals directly

> **Answer:** A

### Q18. What records should a builder keep as a baseline?

- **A.** Contracts, invoices, payments, expenses, and key client communications, organized and retrievable  ✅
- **B.** Only tax filings, since every other document loses its legal relevance after the year closes out
- **C.** Everything on paper exclusively, since digital records remain inadmissible in most disputes
- **D.** Nothing beyond bank statements, since the money trail reconstructs any needed history later

> **Answer:** A

### Q19. This module gives business education, not legal advice. When is paying a real lawyer clearly worth it?

- **A.** Never at your size, since legal review is an enterprise practice with no small-business version
- **B.** For every email you send clients, since routine correspondence carries the most legal exposure
- **C.** For your contract template, entity setup questions, and any dispute with real money at stake  ✅
- **D.** Only after being sued, since preventive legal spending is money spent on pure hypotheticals

> **Answer:** C

### Q20. Beyond profit, what does the deposit-and-milestones payment structure protect?

- **A.** Your tax rate, since staged revenue is taxed more favorably than lump-sum project payments
- **B.** The client's budget approval process, which requires staged invoices in most organizations
- **C.** Your rankings on freelance platforms, which reward accounts with frequent small payments
- **D.** Your cash flow: you never carry weeks of delivered work while waiting on a single payment  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q21. Why do experienced independent builders keep a cash runway?

- **A.** Banks require proof of reserves before renewing the accounts business owners depend on
- **B.** Project income is lumpy; reserves let you decline bad deals and survive slow months calmly  ✅
- **C.** Cash reserves are tax-deductible in most regions, making savings effectively free to build
- **D.** Clients check your reserves during procurement and reject builders who look undercapitalized

> **Answer:** B

### Q22. A client's procurement team sends their standard contract. Which term should make you slow down and negotiate?

- **A.** A requirement to invoice monthly, since billing frequency is the core economics of a deal
- **B.** A clause naming their company correctly, since precise legal names create firm binding obligations
- **C.** Unlimited liability, or rights to everything you own including your reusable tools and methods  ✅
- **D.** A due date for the work, since delivery deadlines don't belong in legal documents at all

> **Answer:** C

### Q23. How should your AI tooling subscriptions and infrastructure costs factor into the business?

- **A.** They shouldn't; tool costs are personal expenses since the subscriptions are in your name
- **B.** Passed through to each client with receipts, since itemized tool billing is the standard
- **C.** Ignored until they exceed revenue, since early-stage businesses shouldn't track small costs
- **D.** Budgeted as real overhead your pricing must cover, and tracked like any business expense  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q24. You're selling services across state or national borders. What should you check rather than assume?

- **A.** Nothing; services are universally untaxed, unlike products, in every jurisdiction worldwide
- **B.** Whether sales tax, VAT, or similar obligations apply to your services where you're selling  ✅
- **C.** Only currency conversion rates, since tax obligations never cross borders with the work
- **D.** Your client's tax compliance, since the buyer carries all obligations in cross-border work

> **Answer:** B

### Q25. Which principle captures the minimum viable business infrastructure every builder needs?

- **A.** Entity, contracts, separate money, taxes planned, records kept — boring systems that prevent disasters  ✅
- **B.** Handle it all later; infrastructure is procrastination disguised as diligence for early-stage builders
- **C.** Copy whatever a bigger competitor does, since their infrastructure is already proven at greater scale
- **D.** Maximize complexity early, since sophisticated structures impress both clients and lenders alike

> **Answer:** A
