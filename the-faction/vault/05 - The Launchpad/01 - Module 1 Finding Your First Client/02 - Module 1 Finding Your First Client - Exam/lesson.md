# Module 1: Finding Your First Client — Exam

> Exam for **Module 1: Finding Your First Client** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. You just earned your certification and want your first paying build. Where do first clients most reliably come from?

- **A.** Your existing network: people who already know you and businesses one introduction away from someone who does  ✅
- **B.** Paid advertising campaigns, since ads reach thousands of strangers faster than any personal outreach can
- **C.** Freelance marketplaces exclusively, since businesses never hire builders they first meet outside those platforms
- **D.** Recruiters, since companies route all project-based engagements through professional staffing agencies

> **Answer:** A

### Q2. You have no client history yet. What is the strongest asset you can lead with in a pitch?

- **A.** A list of the certifications you hold, since credentials substitute directly for delivered client work
- **B.** A discounted rate far below market, since price is the only lever available before you have references
- **C.** A working product you directed AI to build, since a live demonstration proves capability beyond claims  ✅
- **D.** A polished company name and logo, since first clients judge builders primarily on brand presentation

> **Answer:** C

### Q3. You're pitching a small business owner who doesn't care about technology. How should you describe what you do?

- **A.** Walk them through your thirteen-layer methodology so they understand the full depth of your process
- **B.** Describe the business outcome: the hours saved, the revenue unlocked, or the problem that disappears  ✅
- **C.** List the AI models and platforms you work with so they can verify you use current professional tools
- **D.** Compare yourself to traditional developers point by point, emphasizing how much faster AI building is

> **Answer:** B

### Q4. Why does 'I build for anyone who needs software' underperform as first-client positioning?

- **A.** It violates advertising standards, since business claims must always name one specific customer type
- **B.** It exposes you to too many industries, and each new industry requires a separate business license
- **C.** It attracts too many clients at once, and early builders cannot handle more than one at any time
- **D.** A specific audience with a specific problem makes referrals, pitches, and proof dramatically easier  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q5. What is the primary goal of a first discovery conversation with a potential client?

- **A.** To present your full pricing structure so the budget conversation is finished before scoping begins
- **B.** To demonstrate as many of your past builds as possible so the client sees your complete range
- **C.** To understand their problem, workflow, and what a win looks like, before proposing anything at all  ✅
- **D.** To get a signed contract in the meeting, since deals that leave the room usually never close

> **Answer:** C

### Q6. A prospect is interested but hesitant to commit to a large build. What is the smart first move?

- **A.** Propose a small, clearly scoped starter engagement that delivers real value and builds trust fast  ✅
- **B.** Offer to do the entire project for free, since a fully completed system is the only proof that matters
- **C.** Push for the full project anyway, since reducing scope signals you doubt your own capability
- **D.** Walk away immediately, since hesitant prospects always become the most difficult clients later

> **Answer:** A

### Q7. What separates effective outreach from spam when you contact a business owner you don't know?

- **A.** Message length, since serious professional outreach always requires several detailed paragraphs
- **B.** The platform used, since email is inherently professional and social messages are inherently spam messages
- **C.** Volume, since sending the identical message to hundreds of businesses proves your seriousness
- **D.** Specificity: referencing their actual business and a real problem you can credibly help them solve  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q8. Why does a low-cost audit or assessment work well as an entry offer for a builder?

- **A.** It obligates the client to hire you for the full build, since audits create a binding commitment
- **B.** It gives the client a low-risk taste of your thinking and gives you a map of their real needs  ✅
- **C.** It replaces the need for a portfolio, since audits are only purchased from established firms
- **D.** It generates most of your income, since audits should be priced higher than the builds that follow

> **Answer:** B

### Q9. A warm prospect went quiet after a great first conversation two weeks ago. What now?

- **A.** Follow up with something useful: a relevant idea, example, or resource tied to the problem discussed  ✅
- **B.** Move on permanently, since silence after a good conversation always means the answer is a final no
- **C.** Send a message expressing disappointment, since guilt is a documented driver of stalled deal recovery
- **D.** Have a colleague reach out pretending to be a competing bidder to create urgency around your offer

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. You finished a successful first project and the client is happy. What is the highest-leverage next move?

- **A.** Immediately raise your rates on this client, since satisfaction is the moment to extract more value
- **B.** Take a month off outreach, since one finished project generates inbound demand on its own for a while
- **C.** Ask them to keep your work confidential so competitors cannot copy the system you built for them
- **D.** Ask for a referral and a testimonial while the win is fresh, then document it as a case study  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q11. Which prospect is typically the best fit for a builder's first paid engagement?

- **A.** A large enterprise, since big companies have the budgets to survive a first-timer's learning curve
- **B.** A pre-revenue startup wanting a full product for equity, since equity compensates for cash risk
- **C.** A small business with a painful, specific, repetitive process that software could clearly improve  ✅
- **D.** A government agency, since public contracts are awarded to newcomers to encourage competition

> **Answer:** C

### Q12. A prospect wants unlimited revisions, no written scope, and a handshake agreement. What should you do?

- **A.** Accept it, since flexibility on terms is the main competitive advantage that a new builder actually has
- **B.** Decline or restructure the deal; unclear scope and terms are how first projects turn into disasters  ✅
- **C.** Accept but charge double, since risky terms are acceptable whenever the price reflects the risk
- **D.** Accept and plan to renegotiate midway, once the client depends on you and has far less leverage

> **Answer:** B

### Q13. A prospect asks whether you personally write the code. How should an AI Directed Engineer answer?

- **A.** Directly: you direct AI agents through a professional process, and you own the quality of what ships  ✅
- **B.** Vaguely, steering the topic back to pricing before the client asks any more technical questions
- **C.** Claim that you hand-write everything, since clients only pay professional rates for hand-written code
- **D.** Say the question is irrelevant, since clients have no right to ask about production methods

> **Answer:** A

### Q14. How does posting your builds publicly change first-client acquisition?

- **A.** It mostly creates risk, since publishing proof of work invites competitors to undercut your pricing
- **B.** It lets prospects find, watch, and trust you before you ever pitch, so conversations start warmer  ✅
- **C.** It has no effect until you have a large following, since small audiences produce zero client leads
- **D.** It replaces the need for outreach entirely, since consistent posting guarantees inbound clients

> **Answer:** B

### Q15. What is a realistic expectation for landing a first paying client as a certified builder?

- **A.** Same week, since certification by itself generates inbound demand that converts almost immediately
- **B.** Two to three years, since the market only trusts builders with long-established track records
- **C.** It cannot be influenced, since first clients arrive randomly regardless of any effort you make
- **D.** Weeks to a few months of consistent outreach and visible work, faster with a warm network and demo  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q16. What should the first sentence of a pitch to a specific business accomplish?

- **A.** Show you understand their specific problem, so everything after it lands as relevant to them  ✅
- **B.** State your certification and credentials, so your authority is established before anything else
- **C.** Present your pricing immediately, so unqualified prospects filter themselves out up front
- **D.** Explain the history of AI Directed Engineering, so the client understands the discipline

> **Answer:** A

### Q17. A prospect asks why they should hire you instead of a traditional development agency. What is your honest edge?

- **A.** Agencies are always incompetent, so any alternative is automatically the safer choice for a client
- **B.** You are cheaper on every project, since AI-directed work always costs less than agency work does
- **C.** Speed and fit: you deliver working software in days or weeks at a scale and price matched to them  ✅
- **D.** There is no real difference, so the client should simply choose whoever responded to them first

> **Answer:** C

### Q18. Where should a builder look for early prospects beyond their immediate network?

- **A.** Only paid lead databases, since accurate business contact data cannot be gathered any other way
- **B.** Nowhere else, since prospecting outside your existing network wastes an early builder's time
- **C.** Random cold calling from a phone directory, since volume dialing outperforms targeted effort
- **D.** Where their niche already gathers: industry groups, local business meetups, online communities  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q19. Your first real prospect asks for a significant discount. What is the strongest response?

- **A.** Match whatever number they say, since any signed first client is worth more than the revenue
- **B.** Hold your price but adjust scope: trim the deliverable to fit their budget, not your rate  ✅
- **C.** Refuse to discuss it and end the conversation, since price questions signal bad-faith buyers
- **D.** Double your quote first so the discount lands where you wanted, a tactic clients never notice

> **Answer:** B

### Q20. What makes a first case study persuasive to future prospects?

- **A.** Technical depth: naming every platform, model, and tool involved so experts can verify the stack
- **B.** Length: a comprehensive multi-page report demonstrates more effort than a short summary does
- **C.** A before-and-after story with a measurable result: the problem, the build, and what changed  ✅
- **D.** Anonymity: hiding the client's identity and industry so competitors cannot poach the relationship

> **Answer:** C

### Q21. Outreach worked for two weeks, then you stopped to focus on delivery, and the pipeline died. What is the lesson?

- **A.** Prospecting is a rhythm, not a phase; keep a steady minimum of outreach even while delivering  ✅
- **B.** Delivery and outreach cannot coexist, so builders must alternate between them month by month
- **C.** The pipeline died for unrelated reasons, since two weeks of outreach fills it for a quarter
- **D.** Outreach was the wrong strategy from the start, since strong delivery makes pipelines self-fill

> **Answer:** A

### Q22. A friend's company offers to be your first client if you build the project for free, for the exposure. What is the trade-off?

- **A.** None; free first projects are the industry standard entry path and always convert into paid work
- **B.** Free work is rarely valued or prioritized by the client, and it anchors your worth at zero from day one  ✅
- **C.** None for friends specifically, since personal relationships guarantee that the exposure actually arrives
- **D.** Only a legal one, since unpaid commercial work is prohibited in most business jurisdictions today

> **Answer:** B

### Q23. In a strong discovery conversation, who should be talking most of the time?

- **A.** You, since the client needs a complete education in AI Directed Engineering before deciding
- **B.** Neither, since effective discovery is best conducted asynchronously through written questionnaires
- **C.** An even split, since balanced airtime is the standard measure of a professional conversation
- **D.** The prospect; your questions should have them describing their problem and their definition of a win  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q24. A skeptical prospect doubts AI-directed builds are production quality. What moves them further than any argument?

- **A.** A written guarantee of perfection, since contractual promises resolve technical doubts completely
- **B.** Industry statistics on AI adoption, since market data proves individual capability beyond question
- **C.** Showing them a relevant working build, or building a small piece of their problem in front of them  ✅
- **D.** A list of famous companies using AI somewhere in their stack, since association transfers trust

> **Answer:** C

### Q25. Which mindset best serves a builder hunting a first client?

- **A.** Lead with proof and service: build visibly, solve a real problem for a specific someone, ask for the work  ✅
- **B.** Wait to be discovered: quality work eventually attracts clients without any deliberate outreach effort
- **C.** Win on price alone: undercut every alternative until volume compensates for the margin given up
- **D.** Talk technology first: clients hire whoever demonstrates the deepest command of the newest tools

> **Answer:** A
