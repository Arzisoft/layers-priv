# Module 7: Ship: Your Validated Idea Brief — Exam

> Exam for **Module 7: Ship: Your Validated Idea Brief** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. A builder finishing her CRM for dentists validation is assembling the one-page brief and wants to confirm the required sections. What must the brief contain?

- **A.** Problem, evidence, and solution concept only, since buyer and market details belong in later Launchpad courses
- **B.** A full product spec, feature list, wireframes, tech stack choices, and a twelve month revenue projection for investors
- **C.** Problem, evidence, solution concept, target buyer, market size, competitive position, and the first build plan  ✅
- **D.** Whatever sections feel strongest, because a brief should showcase the idea rather than expose its weaker areas

> **Answer:** C

### Q2. A builder opens the Validated Idea Brief Template and wonders where to start. What does the module say about the order of completion?

- **A.** Begin with market size, because the revenue opportunity determines whether the other sections are worth writing
- **B.** Write the solution concept first, since everything else in the brief exists to justify what you plan to build
- **C.** Complete the sections in the template order, since each section builds on the one before it, starting with the problem  ✅
- **D.** Draft all seven sections at once in whatever order you like, then rearrange them into whatever sequence reads most persuasively

> **Answer:** C

### Q3. Sorting her Evidence Ledger, a builder has 40 compliments, 15 waitlist signups, and 3 prepayments, but room for only a few items in the brief. How should she choose what goes in?

- **A.** Include every positive comment, because volume of praise is the clearest sign that demand for the idea is real
- **B.** Keep the proof where people gave email, time, or money, and cut the compliments and hypothetical enthusiasm  ✅
- **C.** Lead with the most enthusiastic quotes, since energy from early conversations is what convinces future clients
- **D.** Weight all feedback equally so the brief stays objective and no single kind of evidence dominates it

> **Answer:** B

### Q4. A vibecoder's brief for his mobile app idea has grown to five pages of context and caveats. According to the module, what does this length signal?

- **A.** He does not understand the idea well enough yet; one page forces only the strongest evidence and clearest statements  ✅
- **B.** Nothing serious, since thorough documentation shows diligence and downstream courses will need the extra detail anyway
- **C.** He should split it into a deck instead, because complex ideas naturally need more room than a single page offers
- **D.** He is actually ahead of schedule, since the extra pages can become his positioning and proposal documents later

> **Answer:** A

### Q5. In the target buyer section, a builder writes 'dental practices' and moves on. What does the module say a strong buyer definition looks like?

- **A.** A broad category, because narrowing too early shuts out revenue from adjacent segments you have not tested yet
- **B.** Someone specific, like office managers at 2 to 5 dentist practices who still track recalls in spreadsheets  ✅
- **C.** A demographic profile with age, income, and region, matching the format used in traditional marketing plans
- **D.** Any label works at this stage, since the buyer definition gets refined later in the positioning course anyway

> **Answer:** B

### Q6. Filling in the competitive position line, a builder proudly writes 'no competitors exist for this.' How does the module treat that claim?

- **A.** As a strong moat, since being first to a problem means pricing power and zero pressure from any alternatives
- **B.** As a neutral fact to note and move past, since competition only matters after the product actually launches
- **C.** As a reason to raise prices, because buyers who have no alternatives will accept whatever number you set
- **D.** As a warning sign, because a space where nobody else solves the problem usually means there is no market  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q7. A builder is unsure what the competitive position section of the brief should actually say. What does the module require?

- **A.** A detailed feature comparison table scoring your concept against every serious rival on price, speed, and capability
- **B.** One line naming who else solves this problem and why your angle wins anyway, backed by your Module 5 research  ✅
- **C.** A promise to out-execute rivals, since positioning is really about founder determination and shipping speed
- **D.** A list of competitor weaknesses only, so the brief keeps the reader focused on your own idea's strengths

> **Answer:** B

### Q8. For the market size section, a builder pastes '$5B industry' from a trade publication headline. Why does the module reject this move?

- **A.** Because headline figures are usually far too conservative, and a serious brief should project a bigger opportunity
- **B.** Because industry data belongs in an appendix, and the one-page brief itself should stay focused on the buyer
- **C.** Because top-down headline numbers are fantasy for your niche; the brief needs bottom-up math with your work shown  ✅
- **D.** Because market size is optional in a one-page brief and including any figure just adds unnecessary clutter

> **Answer:** C

### Q9. Drafting the first build plan, a builder lays out a six month roadmap covering every feature dentists mentioned. What should this section contain instead?

- **A.** The complete product vision, since your AI performs best when it can see the whole destination up front
- **B.** A polished MVP with every core feature included, because launching anything less damages your reputation with buyers
- **C.** A flexible outline with no fixed metric, so early user feedback can shape what success eventually means
- **D.** The smallest version that tests the riskiest remaining assumption, a timeline in weeks, and a success metric  ✅
