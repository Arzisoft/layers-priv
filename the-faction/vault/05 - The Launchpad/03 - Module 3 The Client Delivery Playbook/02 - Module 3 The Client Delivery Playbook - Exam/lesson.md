# Module 3: The Client Delivery Playbook — Exam

> Exam for **Module 3: The Client Delivery Playbook** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. What does a statement of work actually do for a build engagement?

- **A.** It replaces the invoice, since a signed scope document doubles as the payment request itself
- **B.** It defines in writing what will be delivered, by when, for how much, and under which conditions  ✅
- **C.** It protects only the client, which is why experienced builders avoid signing one whenever possible
- **D.** It locks the project legally so that neither side can ever adjust anything after signature

> **Answer:** B

### Q2. Beyond listing deliverables, what should a strong SOW state just as clearly?

- **A.** The builder's complete work history, so the client can verify experience before the start
- **B.** The names of every AI agent and tool involved, so the client can audit the production stack
- **C.** The client's annual revenue, so the pricing can be validated against their ability to pay
- **D.** What is explicitly out of scope, so 'I assumed that was included' never becomes a dispute  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q3. What is the purpose of a kickoff meeting after the SOW is signed?

- **A.** Aligning on goals, timeline, communication, and access needs so delivery starts without friction  ✅
- **B.** Renegotiating the price now that the client is committed and the switching costs work in your favor
- **C.** Presenting finished work, since the strongest kickoff is a build that is already fully complete
- **D.** Introducing every subcontractor by name, since clients must approve each person individually

> **Answer:** A

### Q4. Three people at the client company give you conflicting feedback on the same feature. What should have been set at kickoff?

- **A.** A rule that only written feedback counts, so conflicting verbal opinions can be safely ignored
- **B.** A larger budget, since conflicting stakeholders are resolved by building every requested version
- **C.** A single decision maker on the client side whose call is final whenever stakeholders disagree  ✅
- **D.** A policy that the builder decides all disputes, since technical authority belongs to the builder

> **Answer:** C

### Q5. Why deliver a build in defined milestones instead of one final reveal?

- **A.** Clients see progress early, feedback arrives while it's cheap to act on, and trust builds each step  ✅
- **B.** Milestones triple the number of invoices, which increases the total revenue earned on the same scope
- **C.** Partial deliveries hide the true state of the project, keeping the client calm until the end
- **D.** Milestones are required by software law in most regions once a project crosses a minimum size

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. What communication rhythm keeps a client relationship healthy during delivery?

- **A.** Communicating only when the client asks, since unprompted updates signal insecurity about progress
- **B.** Daily hour-long calls, since maximum contact time is the strongest known predictor of satisfaction
- **C.** One update at the midpoint and one at the end, keeping the client's attention on outcomes only
- **D.** A predictable update cadence the client never has to chase: what shipped, what's next, what's blocked  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q7. What is the most convincing form of progress update in an AI-directed engagement?

- **A.** A long written report, since detailed documents demonstrate more effort than demonstrations do
- **B.** A short demonstration of working software, since seeing the thing run beats any description of it  ✅
- **C.** A percentage number, since clients understand 60% complete better than any feature walkthrough
- **D.** The AI agent's raw activity logs, since unedited output is the most transparent evidence of the work

> **Answer:** B

### Q8. A milestone is going to slip by four days. When and how do you tell the client?

- **A.** After the deadline passes, since early warnings create anxiety about problems that may resolve
- **B.** Never directly; deliver quietly when ready, since most clients do not track dates that closely
- **C.** As soon as you know, with the reason and the recovery plan, so the client hears it from you first  ✅
- **D.** Only if they ask, since volunteering a delay invites penalty conversations that you can easily avoid

> **Answer:** C

### Q9. How do you prevent the 'it's done' versus 'no it isn't' argument at the end of a milestone?

- **A.** Define acceptance criteria up front: the specific, testable conditions that mean a deliverable is done  ✅
- **B.** Deliver everything two weeks early, since surplus time neutralizes disagreements about quality
- **C.** Keep completion undefined deliberately, preserving your flexibility to declare milestones finished
- **D.** Let the client define done after delivery, since their satisfaction is the only measure that truly counts

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. Client feedback is arriving as scattered messages across email, calls, and chat. What do you direct?

- **A.** Respond to each item the moment it arrives, since immediate reaction proves your attentiveness
- **B.** A single consolidated feedback list per review round, so nothing is lost and changes get prioritized  ✅
- **C.** Ignore everything not sent by the project's decision maker, since only their channel is official
- **D.** Pause the project until the client hires a project manager to fully organize their communication for them

> **Answer:** B

### Q11. You need client input to proceed, and they've gone silent for a week. What is the professional move?

- **A.** Proceed on your own assumptions without a note, since delivery speed excuses unauthorized guesses
- **B.** Stop work silently and wait, since the delay is now contractually the client's responsibility alone
- **C.** Send a final warning that the project will be cancelled, since threats reliably restore engagement
- **D.** Follow up with the specific decision needed, the options, and the timeline impact of more silence  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q12. What belongs in a complete handoff at the end of a build?

- **A.** Credentials transferred, documentation, a walkthrough of the system, and clarity on support next  ✅
- **B.** The final invoice only, since anything more invites questions that extend the engagement without pay
- **C.** The raw AI conversation logs, since clients own every prompt used during their engagement
- **D.** A list of the bugs you know about but did not fix, delivered verbally so it leaves no record

> **Answer:** A

### Q13. How should revisions be handled in the engagement structure?

- **A.** Unlimited revisions forever, since ongoing changes are how client relationships stay warm
- **B.** No revisions at all, since professional work is accepted exactly as delivered the first time
- **C.** A defined number of revision rounds in scope, with further changes handled as new scope  ✅
- **D.** Revisions traded informally for testimonials, keeping the arrangement flexible and undocumented

> **Answer:** C

### Q14. The client verbally approves a significant scope change on a call. What must happen before you build it?

- **A.** Nothing; verbal approval from a decision maker is binding and writing it down only signals distrust
- **B.** Confirm it in writing with the price and timeline impact, so the agreement exists outside memory  ✅
- **C.** A new kickoff meeting, since any scope change formally restarts the engagement from the start
- **D.** A cooling-off week, since same-day approvals are legally revocable in most business regions

> **Answer:** B

### Q15. Mid-project, you and the client remember the agreed scope differently. What settles it?

- **A.** The written SOW; whatever it says stands, and anything outside it is priced as new work  ✅
- **B.** The client's memory, since the customer's recollection takes precedence in service work
- **C.** Your memory, since the builder's technical understanding of scope is the expert record
- **D.** A compromise where you build half of the disputed item, splitting the difference evenly

> **Answer:** A

### Q16. What should exist in writing within a day of the kickoff meeting?

- **A.** The complete final architecture, since kickoff is where every technical decision gets locked
- **B.** A press release announcing the engagement, since public commitment keeps both sides honest
- **C.** The client's full org chart, since delivery requires knowing every employee by name and role
- **D.** The timeline, communication plan, access checklist, and decision owners, sent as a recap  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q17. The client is two weeks late providing content and access you need. How should the timeline respond?

- **A.** Absorb the delay silently, since the deadline is your commitment regardless of the cause
- **B.** Cancel the engagement, since client-side delays predict failed projects with high reliability
- **C.** Shift dependent dates and document it: client-caused delays move the timeline visibly, without blame  ✅
- **D.** Double your pace once the inputs arrive, since compressing your own work is the professional standard

> **Answer:** C

### Q18. What are the three things every status update should cover?

- **A.** Hours worked, tools used, and the current invoice total, so the client can audit the spend
- **B.** What shipped since last time, what happens next, and anything blocked that needs their action  ✅
- **C.** Team morale, weather impacts, and industry news, so updates feel personal rather than clinical
- **D.** Competitor activity, market trends, and a motivational quote, keeping the client's spirits high

> **Answer:** B

### Q19. Why include a live walkthrough and training session at handoff instead of just sending credentials?

- **A.** It creates a final billable meeting, extracting one more paid session out of the closing engagement
- **B.** It lets you present unfinished areas as intentional choices before the client explores alone
- **C.** It is required for the warranty to activate, since untrained clients void support agreements
- **D.** Adoption is the real finish line; a system the client can't confidently operate isn't delivered yet  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q20. What should the engagement say about support after handoff?

- **A.** A defined window and scope for fixes, with ongoing support offered as a separate arrangement  ✅
- **B.** Nothing, since silence lets each side assume whatever support terms suit them best later on
- **C.** Lifetime free support, since permanent availability is the standard cost of winning the deal
- **D.** No support of any kind ever, since professional handoffs transfer all responsibility instantly

> **Answer:** A

### Q21. AI direction lets you build fast, and the client now expects every request same-day. What went wrong?

- **A.** Nothing; same-day delivery of every request is the correct standard for AI-directed engineering work
- **B.** The client discovered your actual pace, which should have been concealed from the beginning
- **C.** Speed was never framed; fast delivery needs stated boundaries or it silently becomes the baseline  ✅
- **D.** The AI is too fast, so the fix is artificially delaying deliveries to reset client expectations

> **Answer:** C

### Q22. A productive call produces several decisions. What is the habit that protects the project?

- **A.** Trusting shared memory, since summarizing calls in writing signals you weren't fully listening
- **B.** Recording every call secretly, so a verbatim archive exists if any disagreement surfaces later
- **C.** Making no decisions on calls at all, since spoken agreements have no place in professional work
- **D.** A short written recap sent the same day: decisions made, owners assigned, and dates affected  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q23. During a demo, the client excitedly requests a feature that's clearly outside scope. What is the right move in the moment?

- **A.** Commit to it immediately, since enthusiasm is fragile and paperwork can catch up afterwards
- **B.** Capture it visibly, affirm the idea, and route it to the change process rather than committing live  ✅
- **C.** Explain in detail why the idea is bad, since honest pushback in the moment builds the deepest trust
- **D.** Pretend it was already planned, since clients respond poorly to hearing that anything is extra

> **Answer:** B

### Q24. What marks a professionally closed engagement?

- **A.** The final payment clearing, since money received is the only closure a relationship needs
- **B.** The client's silence, since no complaints within a week of delivery means the work is accepted
- **C.** Delivery confirmed against the SOW, handoff complete, final payment settled, next steps stated  ✅
- **D.** Deleting your copies of the project, since retaining materials creates liability after closing

> **Answer:** C

### Q25. Which principle should govern how you run every client engagement?

- **A.** Run delivery as a repeatable system: scope written, progress visible, decisions documented, handoff done  ✅
- **B.** Improvise each engagement fresh, since process is what slow agencies use in place of talent
- **C.** Optimize for maximum billable change requests, since scope movement is where the margin lives
- **D.** Keep the client at a distance during the build, since involvement only invites interference and delay

> **Answer:** A
