# Module 3: Designing Your Offer — Exam

> Exam for **Module 3: Designing Your Offer** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. A builder is about to design an offer and the module insists one sentence gets written before anything else. Which four parts must that sentence contain?

- **A.** The problem, the outcome delivered, how long it takes, and what it costs  ✅
- **B.** The features, the technology stack, the support terms, and the payment schedule
- **C.** The audience, the competitors, the differentiators, and the proof points
- **D.** The deliverable, the milestones, the revision policy, and the contract length

> **Answer:** A

### Q2. A prospect listens politely to a list of capabilities: automated routing, custom dashboard, API integrations. Nothing lands. Per this module, what role should that list have played?

- **A.** The opener: capabilities establish credibility before any outcome can be believed
- **B.** No role at all: feature lists should be removed from sales conversations entirely
- **C.** Justifying the price after the transformation sells not carrying the sale itself  ✅
- **D.** A leave-behind document: features persuade later, in private, not in the meeting

> **Answer:** C

### Q3. A builder has the transformation sentence and now needs the full structure that makes an offer concrete and safe to accept. Which five layers form the stack?

- **A.** Discovery, proposal, contract, delivery, and a follow-up review cycle
- **B.** Audience, message, channel, cadence, and a conversion tracking plan
- **C.** Hook, story, offer, urgency, and a scarcity element that forces the close
- **D.** Core deliverable, timeline, price, guarantee, and risk-reducing bonuses  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q4. A clinic owner hesitates: what if I pay and this system never works for us? The builder adds one specific offer component in response. Which, and what does it do?

- **A.** A bonus: extra bundled deliverables tip the client's value calculation past the point of hesitation
- **B.** A guarantee: it moves the risk of failure off the client's side of the table onto the builder's  ✅
- **C.** A discount: lowering the financial stake shrinks the imagined loss the client is picturing
- **D.** A testimonial: living proof that other buyers survived the purchase helps dissolve the fear

> **Answer:** B

### Q5. An offer includes a training video, thirty days of tweaks, and a template pack, all cheap for the builder to provide. What job are these doing in the offer?

- **A.** Reducing the client's fear of being stranded after launch, at little cost to the builder  ✅
- **B.** Raising the anchor price so the core deliverable looks reasonable by contrast
- **C.** Filling out the proposal page so the offer looks substantial next to competitors
- **D.** Creating upsell paths, since each bonus can later convert into a paid service

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. One builder sells the No-Show Recovery System, live in three weeks, at a fixed price. Another sells custom development, scoped on request. What advantage does the first structure buy?

- **A.** Higher margins: named products carry brand premiums that custom quotes cannot
- **B.** A yes without translation: the client can accept it without a 45-minute explanation  ✅
- **C.** Legal protection: fixed names and scopes are easier to defend in disputes
- **D.** Search traffic: named systems rank for queries that custom services never match

> **Answer:** B

### Q7. Every sales call a builder runs requires forty-five minutes just to explain what the engagement might involve. What is this length of explanation a symptom of?

- **A.** Complex clients: sophisticated buyers demand thorough walkthroughs before deciding
- **B.** Good diligence: careful explanation is what separates professionals from gig workers
- **C.** An unpackaged offer: the service has no fixed shape, so every call must invent one  ✅
- **D.** Weak slides: the explanation is fine but the visual materials fail to carry it

> **Answer:** C

### Q8. A builder quotes a defined-scope, repeatable build and is choosing a pricing model. The module's default for this situation is which model, for what reason?

- **A.** Hourly, because defined scopes make hours predictable and easy to invoice
- **B.** Retainer, because every build becomes a long-term relationship eventually
- **C.** Value-based percentage, because defined outcomes make gains measurable
- **D.** Fixed price, because the scope is known and the model rewards delivering fast  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q9. A builder gets dramatically faster by directing AI, then notices monthly income falling. They bill hourly. What is the structural explanation?

- **A.** Hourly billing punishes speed: every efficiency gain shrinks the invoice it produces  ✅
- **B.** Faster delivery reads as lower quality, so clients quietly negotiate the rates down over time
- **C.** AI tooling costs are eating the margin that the extra speed should have created
- **D.** Faster turnaround attracts smaller projects, which simply bill fewer hours by their nature

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. A client asks for help untangling a decade-old system where nobody knows what is connected to what. Which pricing approach does the module accept here?

- **A.** Hourly or a paid discovery phase, because the scope is genuinely unknowable up front  ✅
- **B.** Fixed price with a very large buffer, because certainty is always worth paying extra for
- **C.** Free assessment first, because unknowable scopes cannot ethically be billed
- **D.** Retainer from day one, because open-ended messes are ongoing engagements by definition

> **Answer:** A

### Q11. A system shipped last month, and the client wants the builder available for maintenance, improvements, and questions going forward. Which model fits this relationship?

- **A.** Hourly, since maintenance work arrives unpredictably and should bill as it comes
- **B.** A retainer: ongoing value, ongoing availability, ongoing predictable payment  ✅
- **C.** Fixed price per request, packaging each future ask as its own mini project
- **D.** Equity or revenue share, since ongoing involvement earns a stake in outcomes

> **Answer:** B

### Q12. The module describes a common two-step revenue ladder for builders. What is the sequence?

- **A.** Retainer first to fund discovery, then fixed price once the scope emerges
- **B.** Hourly first to establish trust, then fixed price once estimates sharpen
- **C.** Free pilot first to prove value, then retainer once dependence develops
- **D.** Fixed price for the build, then a retainer for the relationship after it ships  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q13. Asked what a project costs, a builder answers: it depends, every business is different. How does the module say this lands with prospects?

- **A.** As flexibility: sophisticated buyers appreciate pricing tailored to their situation
- **B.** As honesty: premature numbers damage trust more than deferred ones ever could
- **C.** As amateur or ambush: no visible number reads as inexperience or a trap waiting  ✅
- **D.** As an invitation to negotiate: open pricing starts the collaborative conversation

> **Answer:** C

### Q14. A builder writes the timeline layer of an offer stack. Which phrasing meets the module's standard?

- **A.** Live in three weeks: a concrete duration, stated in weeks, with a small buffer built in  ✅
- **B.** As soon as possible: speed language signals hustle far better than fixed dates do
- **C.** Timeline flexible to your needs: adaptability is what busy clients seem to value most
- **D.** Depends on the number of revision rounds: honest conditionality protects both parties equally

> **Answer:** A

### Q15. A builder starts offer design by listing deliverables, then picks a price, then looks for a problem the package might solve. What does the module say about this order?

- **A.** It works fine for experienced builders whose instincts already encode the market's pricing norms
- **B.** It is backwards: offers build outward from the transformation sentence, not from deliverables  ✅
- **C.** It is the standard agency method, and it transfers well enough to most solo builders
- **D.** Order does not matter as long as all five stack layers exist by the end of the design

> **Answer:** B

### Q16. A builder reads a new offer to a real prospect as a test. Per the module, which reaction tells them the offer is designed well?

- **A.** Detailed technical questions, proving the prospect is engaging with the architecture
- **B.** A request for references, showing the prospect has moved into verification mode
- **C.** Silence and careful note-taking, the mark of a prospect doing serious evaluation
- **D.** Something like: when can you start? Clarity produces motion instead of questions  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q17. A builder fears offering a guarantee: what if I end up working free forever? Under what condition does the module call the guarantee cheap to offer?

- **A.** When the contract caps free work at ten hours, limiting the worst case
- **B.** When the client is small, since small clients rarely enforce guarantees
- **C.** When the problem is validated: a system built on proven demand rarely triggers it  ✅
- **D.** When the price is high enough that occasional payouts are absorbed easily

> **Answer:** C

### Q18. To beat competitors, a builder packs the offer with every service they can perform: builds, audits, training, support, content. What does the module predict?

- **A.** The offer now needs the long explanation again, because its shape disappeared into the pile  ✅
- **B.** Higher close rates: comprehensive offers reliably dominate narrower ones in competitive deals
- **C.** Better margins: bundling the services hides individual prices from easy comparison
- **D.** Stronger retention: clients who buy everything from one builder have nowhere else to go

> **Answer:** A

### Q19. A builder copies a successful competitor's $12,000 package price, reasoning that the market already accepted that number. What is the flaw?

- **A.** Price copying is legal but unethical, and niches punish it socially when discovered
- **B.** The competitor's price is probably outdated, since public pricing lags real rates
- **C.** Anchoring on one competitor ignores the cheaper majority who will undercut both
- **D.** The number came with positioning, proof, and an audience the copier does not have  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q20. A freelancer who quoted around $50 an hour, depends on the project redesigns everything into a named fixed-price system with a guarantee. Her next call lasts eleven minutes and closes. What changed?

- **A.** Her confidence: conviction closes deals, and the redesign mostly supplied conviction
- **B.** Her niche: the redesign coincided with reaching a warmer, better-fit audience
- **C.** The offer shape: a packaged transformation replaced an open-ended hourly maybe  ✅
- **D.** The price format: flat numbers close faster because math fatigue kills deals

> **Answer:** C

### Q21. A prospect says yes on a call, then goes silent for two weeks while the builder drafts paperwork. Which offer-infrastructure fix does the module suggest for this gap?

- **A.** A follow-up email sequence that keeps the deal warm through the drafting period
- **B.** A deposit link ready at the yes, so agreement converts to commitment immediately  ✅
- **C.** A signed letter of intent template that formalizes the yes before contracts
- **D.** A shorter contract, since long documents are where verbal yeses go to die

> **Answer:** B

### Q22. The module sets a concrete readability bar for the offer document itself. Which is it?

- **A.** One page a busy owner can read in about ninety seconds and know what they are buying  ✅
- **B.** Three pages maximum, structured as problem, solution, and investment sections
- **C.** A five-slide deck, since decision makers respond to visuals over paragraphs
- **D.** However long it takes: completeness protects the builder far more than brevity ever sells

> **Answer:** A

### Q23. Before sending an offer, a builder directs AI to list every question a skeptical prospect might ask. What happens with that list, per the module?

- **A.** It becomes the FAQ page, published separately so the offer document itself stays clean
- **B.** It gets held for the sales call, where objections are always best handled live
- **C.** It scripts the follow-up email sequence, one objection answered per message sent
- **D.** The top questions get answered inside the offer copy itself, before anyone asks  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q24. Under a fixed-price offer, a builder's delivery time drops from three weeks to four days thanks to directed AI. What is the economic result the module points to?

- **A.** A refund obligation, since the original price quote was justified by the longer timeline
- **B.** The effective rate soars: same price, fewer days, and the speed becomes an asset  ✅
- **C.** A pricing problem, since clients who learn of the speed will demand price cuts
- **D.** A volume trap: much faster delivery forces more projects just to hold income steady

> **Answer:** B

### Q25. One principle governs this module, from the transformation sentence to the deposit link. Which statement expresses it?

- **A.** Clients buy from whoever demonstrates the deepest technical capability in the room
- **B.** Clients buy relationships: offers matter less than trust accumulated over time
- **C.** Clients buy transformations they can say yes to not capabilities they must decode  ✅
- **D.** Clients buy price: everything else is presentation layered over the number

> **Answer:** C
