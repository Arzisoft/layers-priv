# Module 2: Cost-Based, Value-Based, and Market-Based Pricing — Study Guide

# Pricing Your Builds

### T7 The Launchpad | Module 2 Study Guide

## Module 2: Cost-Based, Value-Based, and Market-Based Pricing

> Direct AI to run the numbers behind all three pricing methods, then choose deliberately instead of defaulting to whichever feels safest.

## Why This Matters

Most builders price by feel, which usually means cost-based pricing with the costs undercounted. Knowing all three methodologies, and when each one applies, turns pricing from a guess into a decision. This is also where the AI advantage gets protected or given away: speed belongs in your margin, not in your discount.

## Core Concepts

**Cost-based pricing works up from your costs.** Add up what the project costs you, apply a margin, quote the total. It is simple, defensible, and blind: it ignores what the outcome is worth to the client, so it caps your price at a multiple of your effort.

**Your true costs include more than hours.** Tool subscriptions, API and model usage, hosting, your admin and revision time, taxes on the revenue, and the unbillable hours of selling and scoping. Builders who count only build hours routinely price below their real break-even.

**Value-based pricing works down from the outcome.** Start from what the problem costs the client or what the solution earns them, then price as a fraction of that value. A build that saves a firm 40,000 a year justifies a five-figure price regardless of whether it takes you three weeks or three days.

**Market-based pricing reads the going rate.** Research what comparable work sells for in your market and position against it. It keeps you from quoting absurdly high or low, but it assumes your work is comparable, which strong positioning specifically fights.

**The AI advantage belongs to you.** Directing AI means you deliver in days what agencies quote in months. Under cost-based logic, faster delivery means a cheaper price, which is why cost-based logic betrays vibecoders. Under value-based logic, the outcome is unchanged, so the price holds and the speed becomes margin, not discount.

**The three methods are lenses, not laws.** In practice you compute all three: cost sets your floor, market sets the context, value sets the target. A quote below cost is a loss; a quote wildly above market needs a story; a quote far below value is a gift.

## How It Works

Pricing a build with all three lenses:

1. Direct AI to compute your loaded cost: hours at your target rate, tools, API spend, hosting, admin overhead, and unbillable time amortized across projects.
1. Set the floor: loaded cost plus minimum acceptable margin. Below this, decline.
1. Research market context: what agencies, freelancers, and productized services charge for comparable outcomes.
1. Quantify value: the client's cost of the problem or gain from the solution, in their numbers, per month and per year.
1. Set the target price as a defensible fraction of annual value, sanity-checked against market context, never below the floor.

## Directing AI

1. "Compute my true cost for this project: 30 build hours, my tool stack at 180 a month across four projects, estimated API spend, and 20 percent admin overhead."
1. "Here is the client's situation: two staff spend 10 hours a week on manual reporting at 35 an hour. Model the annual cost of the problem."
1. "Research market rates for a custom client portal build: agency, freelancer, and productized tiers. Give ranges and what drives the spread."
1. "Price this build three ways: cost-plus at 40 percent margin, value-based at 15 percent of annual savings, and market-anchored. Recommend one with reasoning."
1. "I can deliver this in four days using AI. Argue why the price should not drop, and draft the language that sells outcome instead of hours."

## Common Mistakes

1. **Counting only build hours.** Ignoring tools, API costs, admin, and unbillable time prices the project below break-even.
1. **Cost-plus on high-value outcomes.** Charging effort multiples for outcomes worth ten times more caps your upside permanently.
1. **Passing the AI speed discount to the client.** Quoting less because it took days instead of weeks gives away the entire advantage.
1. **Market rates as ceiling instead of context.** Positioning exists so your work is not comparable; pricing to the average erases it.
1. **Value theater.** Claiming value-based pricing with numbers you invented; value pricing only holds when the client recognizes the math.
1. **No floor.** Without a computed floor, competitive pressure can talk you into projects that lose money politely.

## Real-World Application

A builder scopes an inventory automation for a distributor: about four days of AI-directed work. Cost lens: roughly 1,400 loaded, floor at 2,100. Market lens: agencies quote similar automations at 8,000 to 15,000 with six-week timelines. Value lens, built with the client's numbers: the manual process burns 31,000 a year in staff time and error corrections. The builder prices at 9,500, inside market range and under a third of first-year savings. The client compares it against the 15,000 agency quote and signs. The build takes four days; the builder's effective rate is what the discarded cost-plus habit would have called impossible, and the client still captured 21,000 of first-year value.

## Decision Framework

- **Is the outcome hard to quantify, like a small internal tool?** Cost-plus with honest loaded costs is fine.
- **Is the value measurable and large?** Value-based pricing; anchor to the client's own numbers.
- **Is the buyer comparing you to visible alternatives?** Use market context to frame, then differentiate.
- **Does the quote fall below your floor?** Decline or rescope; no method justifies losing money.
- **Did AI compress your delivery time?** Keep the price on the outcome; the speed is your margin.
- **Does the client dispute the value math?** Rebuild it with their numbers in the room; agreement precedes the quote.

## Tool and Platform Notes

Direct AI to build a reusable pricing calculator, a spreadsheet or small app, that takes hours, tool costs, API estimates, and overhead, and outputs your floor. Use AI research for market ranges, then verify against a few real data points: posted rates, community numbers from other builders, and quotes clients mention. Keep the value model in the client's terms and currency, and save every model; they compound into a library. Tax treatment of revenue and expenses is accountant territory, not guesswork.

## Key Takeaways

- Cost-based caps you at effort, value-based prices the outcome, market-based supplies context.
- Your true cost includes tools, API spend, admin, taxes, and unbillable time; count all of it.
- Use all three lenses on every serious quote: cost is the floor, market is the frame, value is the target.
- The AI speed advantage is yours to keep; price the outcome, not the hours.
- Value-based pricing works only with client-recognized numbers, never with invented ones.
