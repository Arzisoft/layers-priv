# Module 7: Ship: Your Positioning Package — Exam

> Exam for **Module 7: Ship: Your Positioning Package** (The Launchpad) — 25 questions, pass mark 80%. Correct answers are marked ✅ (read from each question's own post record; no quiz attempt was started to get them).

### Q1. A builder opens the capstone and asks what the finished deliverable actually contains. Which five components make up the positioning package?

- **A.** Mission, vision, values, a full brand voice guide, and a twelve-month revenue target
- **B.** Website copy, logo files, a pitch deck, a rate card, and a client onboarding form
- **C.** Positioning statement, offer description, value proposition, differentiators, and proof plan  ✅
- **D.** Niche research, competitor teardown, SWOT grid, persona sheets, and a channel distribution plan

> **Answer:** C

### Q2. A builder wonders whether to rush the capstone or treat it carefully, and asks what actually depends on it. What is the module's answer?

- **A.** Only the badge: the package is an exercise, and real client work will reshape it anyway
- **B.** The whole downstream chain: pricing, discovery calls, and proposals all run on this document  ✅
- **C.** The community profile: the package becomes a public page that other builders can browse
- **D.** Very little at first: packages only start to matter once a builder passes ten active client accounts

> **Answer:** B

### Q3. One proof plan reads: build credibility over time. Another reads: pharmacy case study written by Friday, two testimonial requests Monday, one post weekly. What separates them?

- **A.** Commitment versus wish: named artifacts with dates versus intentions with neither  ✅
- **B.** Scope: the first plan wisely leaves room for opportunities the second forecloses
- **C.** Risk: the second plan overpromises and will demoralize the builder by week three
- **D.** Nothing structural: both plans express the same strategy at different altitudes

> **Answer:** A

### Q4. A reviewer reads a finished package and checks one property above all others before approving it. Per this module, what is the quality bar?

- **A.** Brevity: every component fits on a single page with room to spare
- **B.** Originality: no component echoes phrasing found on competitor sites
- **C.** Polish: grammar, formatting, and design meet professional standards
- **D.** Coherence: all five parts tell one story about one audience and one promise  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q5. A package has a sharp statement, a solid offer, a tested sentence, strong differentiators, and a dated proof plan, yet each describes a slightly different business. What is this?

- **A.** The classic capstone failure: five decent parts describing five different businesses  ✅
- **B.** Healthy range: slight variation keeps the package flexible across different prospects
- **C.** A drafting stage: divergence is expected and resolves itself later in client work
- **D.** A positioning pivot in progress: the five parts are slowly converging toward a new center

> **Answer:** A

### Q6. Starting the capstone, a builder begins brainstorming a brand-new niche and fresh offers. What does the module say this module's work actually consists of?

- **A.** Reinvention: the capstone is the chance to correct every earlier module's choices at once
- **B.** Expansion: the package should add new positions beyond what the modules produced
- **C.** Integration: assembling what six modules already produced into one coherent document  ✅
- **D.** Validation: re-testing every prior conclusion before anything gets assembled

> **Answer:** C

### Q7. During the coherence pass, four components name pharmacies and one names clinics generally. The builder considers widening everything to healthcare. What does the module prescribe?

- **A.** Widen it: healthcare unifies the five parts without discarding any work
- **B.** Fix the outlier to match the four: coherence comes from tightening, not loosening  ✅
- **C.** Keep both audiences: a two-track package doubles the addressable market
- **D.** Start over on audience: mixed segments mean the sweet spot was never found

> **Answer:** B

### Q8. The package looks finished, and the builder wants one more check before shipping. What does the module's stress test look like?

- **A.** A/B testing two versions of the package on the website for a full week of live traffic
- **B.** A meticulous line-by-line grammar and formatting review performed by a second builder in the community
- **C.** A cost review confirming the offer's price actually covers the hours delivery will take
- **D.** AI role-plays a skeptical prospect, and every hard question must find an answer in the five parts  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q9. Two weeks past the coherence and stress checks, a builder is still reworking sentence rhythm and swapping synonyms. What does the module call this behavior?

- **A.** Market avoidance: polishing past coherence delays the grade the package needs  ✅
- **B.** Craftsmanship: packages carry the business and deserve every extra pass
- **C.** Prudence: shipping too early wastes the one first impression a niche ever allows
- **D.** Momentum loss: acceptable if the proof plan keeps running in parallel

> **Answer:** A

### Q10. The next course, Pricing Your Builds, opens by asking for an input from this package. Which one, playing what role?

- **A.** The proof plan, which will justify premium pricing once its artifacts actually exist
- **B.** The offer's price, which becomes the starting point the pricing course refines  ✅
- **C.** The differentiators, which convert directly into a percentage markup table
- **D.** The positioning statement, which determines the pricing model by niche

> **Answer:** B

### Q11. On a discovery call, a builder needs an opening that frames the whole conversation. Which package component does the module assign to that job?

- **A.** The proof plan, opening with the evidence that is coming over the next quarter
- **B.** The offer description, opening with price so unqualified prospects exit early
- **C.** The differentiators, opening with why competitors will disappoint them
- **D.** The value proposition: the one-sentence outcome that starts the conversation  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q12. Mid-call, a prospect pushes back: another builder quoted half your price. Which package component exists to arm this exact moment?

- **A.** The positioning statement, which reminds the prospect who they called
- **B.** The proof plan, which promises evidence that the premium will pay off
- **C.** The differentiators: the prepared answer to why you at this price  ✅
- **D.** The value proposition, which restates the outcome until price fades

> **Answer:** C

### Q13. A builder ships a vague package to move faster, planning to sharpen things later during client conversations. What does the module predict for that plan?

- **A.** Every downstream conversation becomes a renegotiation of who the builder is  ✅
- **B.** It works: real conversations sharpen positioning faster than any document
- **C.** Discovery calls will compensate, since scripts can cover a soft package
- **D.** Only pricing suffers: the other downstream courses tolerate vagueness

> **Answer:** A

### Q14. A builder drafts the proof plan and asks what belongs inside it. Which contents does the module specify?

- **A.** A publishing wishlist, a monthly budget for paid ads, and a target follower count per platform
- **B.** Dated case study and testimonial commitments, a content schedule, and numbers to capture  ✅
- **C.** A list of dream clients, a conference travel calendar, and a partnership target list
- **D.** Three revenue scenarios, a hiring timeline, and a quarterly business review cadence

> **Answer:** B

### Q15. Ninety days after shipping the package, a mentor checks in on the proof plan. What should exist by now if the plan did its job?

- **A.** A visible portfolio: written case studies, collected testimonials, published content  ✅
- **B.** A refined plan: ninety days of learning folded back into better, sharper intentions
- **C.** A bigger audience: follower growth is the leading early indicator that the proof is working
- **D.** A price increase: proof plans exist mainly to justify the next rate change upward

> **Answer:** A

### Q16. The role-played skeptic asks a hard question, and the builder finds no component answers it. Where does the module say the answer goes?

- **A.** Into a new sixth component created specifically for objection handling
- **B.** Into the discovery call script, where hard questions are best handled live
- **C.** Nowhere: some questions should remain unanswered to preserve mystique
- **D.** Into the weakest existing component, strengthened rather than footnoted  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q17. A builder asks how long the package should take, given that Modules 2 through 6 are complete. What does the module's case suggest?

- **A.** A quarter: assembly requires the same rigor as the original module work
- **B.** A week minimum: integration always surfaces conflicts that take days to resolve
- **C.** A couple of evenings: the parts exist, and this module is assembly plus checks  ✅
- **D.** An afternoon with AI: the whole document generates from prior outputs unattended

> **Answer:** C

### Q18. During assembly, a builder's differentiators brag about speed while the offer copy says timeline flexible. The coherence pass flags it. What is the correct repair?

- **A.** Delete the speed differentiator: guarantees about time invite disputes
- **B.** Fix the offer copy to match a speed claim so the package tells one story  ✅
- **C.** Keep both: flexibility and speed appeal to different buyer personalities
- **D.** Soften both into quality-focused language that neither promises nor bends

> **Answer:** B

### Q19. Weeks after shipping, a downstream course asks for the builder's value proposition, and the builder starts rewriting it from memory. What does the module say to do instead?

- **A.** Rewrite freely: downstream courses deserve fresher language than the package holds
- **B.** Merge the memory version with the package version and ship the resulting blend
- **C.** Ask AI to generate a new one, since the package version has aged by weeks
- **D.** Pull it from the package verbatim: per-course rewrites break the coherence  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q20. A builder finishes the pharmacy package in two evenings and feels almost guilty about the speed. What explains the pace, per the module's case?

- **A.** The parts already existed: the sweet spot, offer, sentence, and levers were built earlier  ✅
- **B.** Adrenaline: capstone deadlines compress work that would otherwise sprawl for weeks
- **C.** Luck: the pharmacy niche simply happens to be unusually easy to document quickly
- **D.** Corner-cutting: finishing in only two evenings means the coherence pass was surely skipped

> **Answer:** A

### Q21. Alongside the full package, the module asks for a condensed version. What is its specified form and purpose?

- **A.** A tweet-length version, testing whether the position survives extreme compression intact
- **B.** A slide deck, since most prospects expect visual materials at their first meetings
- **C.** One page that pastes into proposals and reads aloud cleanly on discovery calls  ✅
- **D.** An elevator script, carefully memorized word for word for use at networking events

> **Answer:** C

### Q22. Three months in, signals from the 90-day test suggest the audience component needs revision. How does the module say the package gets updated?

- **A.** Rebuild all five components from scratch so the new audience gets a clean start
- **B.** Leave the package fixed: shipped documents should not chase moving signals
- **C.** Fork the package: keep the old audience version live while testing the new
- **D.** Revise that one variable and keep the rest, since the protocol changes one thing  ✅

> **Answer:** D

### Q23. The proof plan tells the builder to start capturing baseline and outcome numbers on every new project. What future purpose does the module give for this habit?

- **A.** Tax documentation: outcome records simplify year-end deduction claims considerably
- **B.** Future case studies: proof needs data, and data must be captured while projects run  ✅
- **C.** Client billing: captured numbers justify the invoices whenever disputes arise later
- **D.** Team training: numbers let future hires study what good projects look like in practice

> **Answer:** B

### Q24. A builder writes the proof plan dates into the package document and considers that step complete. What additional step does the module require?

- **A.** Calendar the dates in the task system the builder actually checks daily  ✅
- **B.** Share the dates with a peer who agrees to enforce them socially
- **C.** Add financial penalties: unmet dates should cost something real
- **D.** Publish the dates publicly so the niche holds the builder accountable

> **Answer:** A

### Q25. One conviction governs this capstone and the whole course behind it. Which statement expresses it?

- **A.** Positioning is a document: once the package exists, the market simply takes care of itself
- **B.** Positioning is optional: strong delivery eventually outruns any vague identity
- **C.** A clear package makes everything downstream easier; a vague one makes everything a fight  ✅
- **D.** A package is temporary: serious builders outgrow positioning inside their first year
